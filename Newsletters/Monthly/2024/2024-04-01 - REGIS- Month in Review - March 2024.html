REGIS REPORT

01 April 2024
 

The Philippines broke its four-month win streak with a 0.6% drop in March as foreigners went back to selling the market.  The PSE was in consolidation mode for most of the month, though results season as well as rebal activity caused volatility. The FOMC’s guidance of rate cuts this year also influenced sentiment.

In dollar terms, the PCOMP is up 5.5% YTD, performing relatively better than ASEAN neighbors Indonesia, which is down 2.7%, and Thailand, which is lower by8.3%.  The Phils is also running ahead of Singapore which slipped 2.5% in 1Q and Malaysia which is up 2.6% for the first three months of the year.

CPI Inflation rose to 3.4% YoY in February, accelerating from 2.8% in January. TheFebruary print came in above consensus (3%) and near the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.8%-3.6%. The acceleration in inflation was fueled by faster increases in food CPI (4.6% in Feb from 3.5% in Jan) and fuel-related transport CPI (+1.2% in Feb vs -0.4% in Jan). Rice price inflation persisted, however. Rice CPI climbed 1.0% MoM and 23.7% YoY in February.

BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona expects inflation to quicken to 3.9% in March on the back of base effects.  The uptick is supportive of the view that the BSP will not ease monetary policy just yet in the next Monetary Board’s policy review scheduled for April 4. The BSP earlier discounted the possibility of a rate cut within the first half of the year, saying such a policy move is “too soon.” What would spur the BSP’s action or inaction should likely be CPI data in 2Q. CPI for this period should be influenced, to a large extent, by food prices because of El Nino.

The government recorded a budget surplus of P88bn in January, up 92% YoY. Disbursements rose 10% YoY due to a 58% jump in interest payments. Primary spending was up just 2% YoY. The government is targeting to lower the budget deficit by 10% YoY to P1.36tn in 2024, equivalent to ~5% of GDP. For 2023, the budget deficit  came in at P1.5trn, down 6% YoY. While revenues grew 7.9% YoY, expenses were up a mere 3.4%. Excluding interest costs, expenses were up just 1.1%. The fiscal deficit to GDP ratio stood at 6.2%, improving from 7.3% in 2022. While improved the large deficit should continue to constrain spending this year,  with the government’s budget up only 5% YoY. 

Progress on the planned PPP projects will play a key role in boosting infrastructure spending. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) has issued the implementing ruled and regulations (IRR) of the PPP Code.  The PPP Code is aimed at speeding up approval of PPP projects, among other things.

Key data
 

Market turnover improved to $124m (daily ave) this month vs $95m the month prior, as FTSE rebalancing flows helped stimulate trading activity, with bargain hunters and speculators taking advantage of the liquidity event. The index, though,traded within a tight range -- bouncing between 6800 to 7000 levels to close slightly lower for the month --  as investors continued to digest 4Q results and exercised caution, waiting for the Fed’s next move. Foreigners  turned net sellers in the market for $45m snapping four straight months of inflows. Bulk of the outflows came during the FTSE rebalancing where Philippines saw an estimated outflow of $176m.

Foreign participation was at at 50%, unchanged from February.

 

  • The PCOMP snapped its 4-month net foreign inflow streak mainly due to the FTSE rebalancing where our market overall saw outflows. Deletion EMI -US$34m was the top sold name, alongside SECB -$3m, and AGI -$5m.
  • Outside of FTSE flows, foreigners were sellers in property names as optimism for a near-term rate cut fizzled after February’s inflation print accelerated to 3.4%. Soft pre-sales in the last reporting round also weighed on the sector. Foreigners turned sellers in ALI; the stock saw the biggest outflow among its peers at -US$32m, with profit takers capping its share price after the stock hit new highs last February. SMPH also saw outflows, but the stock managed to close the month +3.4%.
  • URC continued to see selling pressure from foreigners after FY23 numbers came out behind expectations, pushing the stock’s share price back to June 2022 levels. The stock is now net foreign sold for the 12th straight month. Outflows for March stood at $28.6m.
  • Conglos meanwhile finished the month split with SM capping this month’s top foreign bought name +US$32.9m as investors continue to buy the stock post 4Q earnings. AC meanwhile fell 5.6% as foreigners turned sellers of the name after its upweight in the MSCI last February.  
  • Foreigners were back buying banks as interest rates continue to be elevated. Both BDO and MBT printed new 52-week highs in March, with foreign interest mostly skewed in MBT +US$24m after the company declared a higher dividend payout this year. BPI finished the month -5.6%, despite decent foreign buying.
  • There were a few notable gainers this March:  ICT printed a new all-time high this month (P344.00). MWC finished +16.5%, with locals taking advantage of the stock’s deletion from the FTSE index. Bargain hunters stepped into CNVRG sending its share price +10.7%.



 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The property sector was mostly weak, despite lower interest rates looming in the next few quarters. Outlook for the sector has been clouded by concerns on residential presales, with nearly every developer reporting declines in 4Q23. Aggregate reservation sales of the developers under Regis coverage contracted 14% QoQ and 6% YoY. Most developers are nowplanning to launch less projects this year to manage unsold inventory levels.

Large cap Ayala Land (-7.9% MoM) succumbed to selling pressure in March. Other property names Megaworld (-0.5% MoM), Filinvest Land (-5.6% MoM), and Vista Land (-3.0% MoM) ended the month lower.

While 4Q23 presales dropped 17% QoQ (37% YoY), Robinsons Land (+1.8% MoM) managed a gain for March, bolstered by FY23 profits coming in ahead of expectations (mall EBIT in line and all other segments better than expected).

Robinsons Land is jointly developing a 33-hectare property in Fort Bonifacio with SM Prime (+3.5% MoM), which this moth rebounded from a 7.7% drop in February.

A slew of disappointing 4Q23 results weighed on the consumer sector.

Wilcon (-17.7% MoM) dropped sharply, as investors digested FY23 results which featured a 7% YoY decline in SSS for 4Q and pre-operating expenses for new stores dragging EBIT down 12% for the quarter. Management noted that it is still seeing weakness in private construction activity as inflationary pressures weigh on consumer sentiment.
Also falling sharply was Universal Robina (-10.0% MoM) whose FY23 results came in behind expectations as soft demand persisted, resulting in flat Philippines branded consumer food (BCF) sales in 4Q23. Management is guiding for mid-high single digit top line growth in FY24 and continued margin improvement, broadly in line with Regis FY24 expectations.

Renewed concern on Jollibee’s (-7.7% MoM) international business dampened sentiment. Jollibee’s Philippine business continued to perform strongly in 4Q (EBIT +12% YoY), but the international segment reverted to an EBIT loss due to softness at CBTL and Highlands Coffee.
Century Pacific (+19.4% MoM) and Monde (+9.2% MoM) however outperformed the sector and the PSEi. Both names are up ~30% YTD each as investors favored the higher growth profiles of both.

Bloomberry (-1.6% MoM) reached a settlement with Global Gaming Philippines (GGAM) wherein Bloomberry (through a wholly-owned subsidiary) will effectively purchase 921mn BLOOM shares (8% of current shares outstanding) from GGAM at P18.32/shares (P16.9bn). The settlement would have a meaningful impact on BLOOM’s financials, raising net debt/equity from 1.2X to 2.0X (estimate, FY23). Furthermore, the purchase of GGAM’s stake will lower BLOOM’s public float by about 8ppt. All else held constant, this should result in a reduction in its weighting in the PSE Index.
At P18.32/share, the shares will be purchased by BLOOM at a 59% premium to last close (on announcement). Using the last price as the benchmark for “fair” value, the negative impact on BLOOM’s value would be P6.3bn (~US$110m). Spread over 10.5bn shares outstanding (post-buyback), that is equivalent to ~P0.60/share. The settlement nonetheless ends a decade-long overhang on BLOOM stock.

BLOOM is set to open its 2nd casino, Solaire Resorts North (SRN), in Quezon City in late May 2024. The site will expand BLOOM’s table capacity by 38% (slot capacity by 112%). Management expects SRN to reach EBITDA breakeven in one quarter, faster than Regis forecast of six months.
Meanwhile, online gaming firm DigiPlus (+39.3% MoM) reported net profit grew nearly 600% to P4.1bn for FY23 as revenues rose 300% to P27.3bn. PLUS has managed to report net profits nearly half of BLOOM’s P9.5bn for FY23. BLOOM’s GGR of P58bn (+16%) was in line with our forecast but EBITDA was dragged lower by a P670m retirement-related expense in 4Q23.

While buying interest for banks remained healthy as key beneficiaries of elevated rates, their share prices were mixed in March. While BDO (+1.0% MoM) managed another gain and laggard interest led Metrobank (+5.1% MoM) higher, BPI (-5.6% MoM) corrected. The bank was among the top gainers in the previous month, with an advance of +13.1%. BPI is selling its 15% stake in digital bank GoTyme for P902m to JG Summit. BPI became the owner of the stake when it acquired Robinsons Bank from the JG Summit group.
Security Bank (-3.4% MoM) was dragged lower by its deletion from the FTSE Index mid-month as well as profit disappointment. FY23 NPAT of P9.1bn was down 14% YoY, well behind forecast. The main drag came from provision expenses, which jumped 64% YoY on higher NPLs QoQ.

Telcos were up for the month, after a mixed performance in February. PLDT (+4.3% MoM) and Globe (+2.8% MoM) fared well, with share prices held up by 4Q results which showed a pick-up in revenues amid an improving pricing environment for the telcos.

Converge (+10.7% MoM) surged in March. The performance took its gain YTD to 18.9%, rebounding from a 47% decline for 2023. While operating revenue was down in 4Q (-1% YoY), net subscriber additions of 80k was a silver lining.

ICTSI (+10.0%) extended its gains in March, taking its YTD performance up to +28.8%. The stock was one of the top index movers for the month. While headline FY23 profit of ICTSI fell 17% YoY to US$648m due to the full write-down of receivables from the Sudanese government, recurring profit after perps distributions actually rose 8% YoY to US$648m, outperforming Street and Regis forecasts. Based on management’s pronouncements trends so far in 2024 appear to be encouraging, particularly with VICT’s (port in Melbourne) recovery, which was the key drag for the weakness in Asia in 2023. Management however admitted that a 2Q takeover for the Durban port in South Africa remains uncertain, given the elections on May 29.

Macro proxy holdcos were mostly down. Leading the losses were index stocks Aboitiz Equity (-11.6% MoM) and JG Summit (-11.2% MoM). Ayala Corp (-5.6% MoM) fell as well. The company reported FY23 results that came in below expectations on the back of write-offs (impairment charges etc) in 4Q, this time on Manila Water, Yoma, and Zalora.

Heavyweight SM (+3.4% MoM) ended higher. SM Investments acquired 29% of Citicore REIT (+2.6% MoM) for P5bn, equivalent to P2.65/share (roughly equivalent to previous close of P2.70/share). CREIT is a subsidiary of renewable power developer Citicore Renewable Energy Corp (CREC). CREIT collects lease revenue from some of CREC’s solar farms.

GT Capital (+0.1% MoM) was flat. GTCAP’s implied 4Q23 core profit was up 22% YoY, underpinned by Toyota’s profit rising 2x to P2.95bn and Federal Land recording a substantial +141% YoY jump in 4Q profit.

San Miguel (+7.6% MoM) and Metro Pacific (MPI) are finalizing the functional merger of their toll road assets, which is expected to include MPI’s operations in Indonesia. The two groups plan to list the merged entity as early as late this year.

The San Miguel-led consortium, New NAIA Infrastructure Corp., has been has been given a 15-year concession that can be extended for another 10 years. The government will rake in as much as P900bn from the privatization of the airport, comprising P30bn upfront, P2bn in annuity cost and an 82% share of airport revenue. NAIA Infrastructure Corp. said that they have already secured funds for the project, which will partly involve building a new terminal with a total capacity of 35mn pax per year (vs. current capacity of all four terminals at c. 32mn).

Meanwhile, DMCI (+3.6% MoM) managed a gain despite disappointing results due to shortfalls at Semirara (8.0% MoM). Semirara soared, however, despite 4Q numbers falling behind as management sounded firm about the payment of a “good” dividend in April, given its healthy cash pile.

Advancing even more sharply was Manila Water (+16.5% MoM) which reported FY23 profits that were well above forecast due to lower cash costs at the East zone. The company also declared a dividend of P1.129/sh, which is higher than our forecast and is equivalent to a yield of 6%.

Meralco (-8.5% MoM) declined. Meralco’s volumes for 1Q24 are trending an unusually high 9% YoY (4-5% average historically). The string was helped driven by a modest rebound in industrial usage, higher temperatures due to El Nino and the extra day with the leap year.

Meralco (through subsidiary MGen), Aboitiz Power (-4.1% MoM) and San Miguel (through San Miguel Global Power, SMGP) signed a deal for the three to jointly control 2,500MW power generation capacity and an LNG terminal. The deal was valued at US$3.3bn. Under the deal, MGen and Aboitiz Power will jointly invest in two of SMGP’s gas-fired power plants: the 1,278-MW Ilijan power plant and a new 1,320-MW combined cycle power facility. The Ilijan power plant resumed its operations last year, while the new power facility under Excellent Energy Resources, Inc. is scheduled start operations by the end of 2024

Shutdowns at AP’s biggest units drove its 4Q23 profits lower, dragging sentiment for the stock. Disappointing results also dragged ACEN (-7.5% MoM) lower. Income for 4Q was soft compared to earlier quarters, partly because of delays on new projects.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation accelerates to 3.4% in February. Headline CPI rose 3.4% YoY in February from 2.8% in January. This was above consensus (3%) and came in at the higher end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.8%-3.6%. The uptick was due to faster increases in food CPI (4.6% in Feb from 3.5% in Jan) and fuel-related transport CPI (+1.2% in Feb vs -0.4% in Jan). Rice price inflation persisted, with rice CPI climbing 1.0% MoM and 23.7% YoY in Feb (base effects could start to fade in Sep 2024). According to NEDA, international rice prices have started to ease and local supply is expected to increase with the dry season harvest beginning this month through April.

Meat prices climbed 0.7% YoY and 1% MoM in February after being fairly muted in previous months. Base effects resulted in lower vegetable prices (-11% YoY) but this will certainly fade in March. Meanwhile, fuel-related transport inflation rose 1.3% MoM and 1.2% YoY, accelerating from January (-0.4% YoY, flat MoM). Core CPI slowed to 3.6% in February from 3.8% in Jan. However, the “non-food” core items were broadly unchanged at +3.6% YoY for both months. BSP said that the risks to the inflation outlook have receded but remain tilted toward the upside. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. earlier discounted the possibility of a rate cut within the first half of the year, saying such a policy move is “too soon.”. The BSP’s next policy meeting is on April 4.

Finance Secretary believes 6% GDP growth is a reasonable target. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto considers 6% to be a realistic GDP growth target but “we will endeavor to hit 6.5%”. This is lower than the 6.5%-7.5% target set by the Development Budget Coordination Committee. Regis has FY24E GDP growth forecast at 6.0% and 2023 GDP growth at 5.6%. Secretary Recto also said the Marcos administration will focus on improve tax collection and would not introduce new taxes, except as a last resort. He said that the government’s fiscal position was “not that bad” as to require imposition of new taxes. Meanwhile, he wants to extend the corporate life of PSALM (Power Sector Assets and Liabilities Management Corp.) by another 25 years in order to help reduce the government’s deficit position. PSALM is scheduled to end its corporate life in June 2026. PSALM is currently prioritizing the sale of the 796MW Caliraya-Botocan-Kalayaan hydroelectric plant in Laguna. This could generate P50bn to P100bn (awarding in 2025). The rehabilitation of the Agus-Pulangi Hydropower complex in Mindanao is also ongoing.

Remittances rise 2.7% YoY in January. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 2.7% YoY to US$2.84bn in January. In PHP terms, remittances rose 4.5% YoY. Land-based remittances from USA (+2.7%), Saudi Arabia (+5.7%), UAE (+7.7%), and Singapore (+4.2%) contributed the most to growth this month. The BSP is projecting remittances to grow by 3% to US$34.5bn in 2024.

BSP sees lower current account deficit in 2024. The BSP lowered its 2024 current account deficit estimate to US$6.1bn (1.3% of GDP) from US$9.5bn (2.0%) previously. The 2024 target is also lower than the preliminary 2023 current account deficit of US$11.2bn (2.6% of GDP). The downgrade reflects a lower projected trade deficit of US$69bn (vs. US$74bn in previous forecast). This change brings the BOP surplus forecast to US$700mn, up from US$400mn previously. For 2025, the BSP is forecasting the BOP to revertto a deficit of US$500mn due to higher trade deficit of US$74bn (due to imports of goods for infrastructure) and lower capital inflows (due to elections in US and UK).

Trade deficit narrows to US$4.2bn in January. Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit stood at US$4.22bn in January, down 24% YoY and flat MoM. Imports fell 8% YoY to US$10.2bn in January on lower commodity prices. Imports of fuel fell 35% YoY while raw materials dropped 5% YoY. Consumer goods imports jumped 16% but capital goods imports still posted a decline of 6% YoY. 

Exports rose 9% YoY due to base effects (Jan-Apr 2023 were the weakest months for exports in 2023). Electronics exports rose 16% YoY, due to a strong recovery in exports to the USA (+23%) and HK (+79%).

 

BSP sees March CPI closer at 3.9%. The BSP sees inflation accelerating closer to 4% (or 3.9%) in March due to positive base effects according to BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona. Inflation in February was at 3.4% and the BSP sees inflation averaging 3.6% this year. The Philippine Statistics Authority will release March CPI data on April 5. The Monetary Board’s next policy review is scheduled for April 4.

February BoP deficit eases to US$196mn. Data showed that the balance of payments narrowed sharply to US$196mn in February, lower than the US$895mn recorded in Feb 2023. This reversed surpluses of US$2.2bn in Jan-Feb 2023. The reflected outflows primarily from the National Government's payments of foreign currency debt obligations. The GIR dipped 1.26% MoM to US$102bn in February.

BSP to review 3% interest rate cap on credit card transactions. The BSP will review this month whether to keep or hike the 3%/month interest rate cap on credit card transactions. The BSP reviews the interest rate ceiling on credit card transactions every six months. It raised the cap from 2%/month to 3%/month in January 2023.

FDI net inflows rise 30% YoY in December; FY23 inflows dip 7% YoY to US$8.9bn. Foreign direct investments posted a net inflow of US$826mn in December, up 30% YoY, due to an 86% YoY jump in nonresidents’ investments in debt instruments to US$527m. Reinvestment of earnings rose 4% to US$91m while investments in equity capital dropped 22% YoY to US$208m.
This brings FY23 FDI net inflows to US$8.9bn, down 6.6% YoY and equivalent to 2.0% of GDP. This is above the BSP’s target of US$8bn for the year.

Tourist arrivals sustain recovery in Feb. Tourist arrivals rose 23% YoY, 1% MoM to 582k, equivalent to 85% of 2019 average. After arriving in droves in January, Korean tourists fell 11% MoM to 156k (+23% YoY) or 94% of pre-Covid levels. Visitors from the USA rose 7% YoY to 92k or 4% above pre-Covid levels. Chinese tourists extended gains, jumping by 47% YoY to 49k but were still about 34% of pre-Covid levels.
This brings YT-Feb tourist arrivals to 1.16mn, up 24% YoY. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or reverting to 93% of 2019 levels (vs 2023A: 5.5mn or 66% of pre-Covid).

 

Growth of digital payments (surprisingly) re-acceleratesin February. Digital transactions made over the National Retail Payments System (NRPS) accelerated even further in February, surging to P1,231bn 41% YoY, compared to 25% in December, and 37% in January. More surprisingly, payments over both InstaPay (smaller-ticket, real-time) and PesoNet (larger, batched) accelerated. We had thought that PesoNet was maturing, with growth slowing in 2023. Growth was still faster at InstaPay, up an astounding 49% YoY to P496bn. Even more impressive was the number of InstaPay transactions, which skyrocketed 85% YoY (vs an already heady 73% in December). PesoNet payments came in at P735bn, re-accelerating 35% YoY in February, compared to the 15% YoY posted in December. 

Marcos wants more US semiconductor firms to expand in PHL. President Marcos is calling for US companies to set up wafer fabrication facilities in the country; upskilling of workforce would help the Philippines expand its role in the semiconductor industry beyond assembly and packaging.

US Trade Secretary Gina Raimondo reiterated a plan to crank out more than 120k engineers and process technicians by 2028 to boost output. The Philippines is one of seven countries that the US is partnering with to diversify its semiconductor supply chain under the CHIPS and Science Act. The Law provides US$52.7bn in federal subsidies to support chip manufacturing and persuade chipmakers with operations in China to relocate to the US or other friendly countries.

Maharlika to raise USD1bn for energy sector. Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) CEO and President Consing is currently seeking board approval to raise USD1bn (P56bn) for energy projects. After determining risk and sectoral limits, about 15% of MIC's P125bn seed capital, amounting to P18.75bn, will be its contribution to the fundraising for the energy sector. MIC intends a limited partnership (LP) with some sovereign wealth funds (SWFs) yet to be disclosed and also eyes private equity to raise the remainder of the intended fund. An official fundraising timeline was yet to be given but MIC plans to announce its first commitment, likely to an energy project, within three months.

PAGCOR expects industry GGR to hit P450bn-P500bn by 2027. PAGCOR, the gaming regulator, expects Philippine GGR to hit P450-P500bn by 2027, from P285bn in 2023. This would represent 13%-16% CAGR and would be due to opening of about six new casinos over the next few years – in Manila, Clark, Cebu, Boracay, and other areas. It also expects online gaming to grow during the period.

Finance Secretary revives plan to sell NAIA assets. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto is revisiting the previous plan to sell the assets of the Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA) and turn it into a new business district. NAIA is located in a 600-hectare property. Mr. Recto plans to auction the property in 25-hectare tranches.

 

Senate approves Bill on property valuation reform. The Senate approved on third reading Senate Bill 2386, the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act. The law will take effect two years after it is passed.

This seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. Assessed valued used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 3%-7% of pretax profit of the developers we cover. This law would likely have a large impact on developers with large land banks, such as Ayala Land, Filinvest Land, and Vista Land.

House passes CREATE MORE legislation on final reading. The Lower House approved House Bill No. 9794 or CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy). 
The proposed amendments include:
-Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%
-Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports
-Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board
-Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises
-IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements

 

Dept. of Finance proposes “compromise” to Lower House’s mining fiscal regime bill. The Department of Finance is proposing a simplified fiscal regime for the Philippine mining industry that is seen to generate as much as P10bn in additional revenues every year. The proposal is simpler than House Bill (HB) No. 8937 as it adopts fewer tiers and rates for easier compliance and implementation. HB 8937 was approved in the Lower House last Sep 2023. Based on its new proposal, the DoF expects the “compromise” mining fiscal regime to generate an average of P10.23 billion a year from 2025 to 2028. Chamber of Mines of the Philippines (CoMP) Chairman Michael T. Toledo said the proposed four-tier royalty rate will result in an increase in the annual effective tax rate to 60.6% from the 59.6% under HB 8937. This would be higher than those in Indonesia, Chile, Peru, South Africa, and Canada.

DOF refines CTRP Package 4 proposal. The Department of Finance refined its proposal for Package 4 of the Comprehensive Tax Reform Program (CTRP). The refined proposal seeks to maintain the structure of some products and instruments while deferring the implementation of certain provisions by 2028. Dividend income tax will remain unchanged until 2027, with a proposed harmonization of 10% in 2028. The stock transaction tax will gradually be reduced annually by 0.1%, from 0.6% to 0.1% in 2028. Refined proposal was presented by Undersecretary Karlo Adriano to finance sector stakeholders in a briefing on March 1, 2024.
