REGIS REPORT

29 November 2024
 

The PSEi posted its worst monthly decline in over 2 years, -7.41% MoM to 6613.85 in November, as foreign selling in the market accelerated following the conclusion of the US presidential elections. On the ground, a largely mixed outcome to the 3Q/9M results season also failed to help improve sentiment.

Net foreign selling in November hit US$349m, which has effectively erased the "real" net inflow into the market (considering the ~US$290m outflow in BLOOM related to the settlement of the case against GGAM from earlier this year.) Nominal net outflow YTD now stands at -US$305m, marking a quick fall from a peak inflow of nearly +US$100m just 6 weeks ago. With only a month before year-end, the Philippine shares look en route for another year of net outflows, which would mark the 7th consecutive year of net foreign selling (last inflow year 2017.)

The selloff during the month has brought down the benchmark's YTD gain to +2.54%, coming from a peak of over +17% (at 7,554.68) back in early October. Aside from Trump's expected policies dragging down sentiment on a few names like index heavyweight ICT -6.57% MoM, some of the weakness in the market stemmed from the tepid 3Q/9M results season as well, particularly in the property sector and casinos.

On the macro front, data released earlier in the month showed that CPI in October accelerated to 2.3% from 1.9% in September, inline with Street expectations and still at the lower end of the BSP's 2%-4% target range. The latest GDP print however missed forecasts, growing by only 5.2% in 3Q vs 5.7% estimate and 6.4% previously. The slowdown was attributed to slower growth in government spending (+5% vs 2Q’s +11.9%) and a contraction in exports (-1% vs +4.2% previously). The latest reading brings 9M24 GDP growth to 5.8%, below the government’s FY24 target of 6%-7% but inline with the median forecast of 5.8%. In order reach Regis’ FY24E of 6.0%, 4Q GDP growth should pick up to 6.5%.

Moving forward however, these data points support the case for another rate cut before the year ends. The BSP will have its next policy meeting on December 19, right after the Fed meeting on the 18th. Fed fund futures are currently pricing in a ~65% chance of a 25bp cut. Meanwhile BSP Governor Remolona said that policymakers will consider either a cut or a pause in their upcoming meeting, and further down the line, indicated that he sees potentially a total of 100bps of cuts for next year. If the BSP does indeed decide to cut in December, that will mark the third consecutive rate cut and bring the cumulative reduction this year to 75bps (from 6.5% back in June).

Along with the sharp weakness in shares and surge in foreign selling, the PHP weakened for the 2nd month in a row, -0.89% MoM to 58.635, adding to its heavy -3.69% slump in October. The PHP fluctuated around the crucial support level of 59 through most of the month. This prompted BSP Governor Remolona to comment that a potential slide to a new record low of 60 is possible, but he reiterated that officials will ensure that such movement will not be abrupt.

Trading volumes in November grew a mere 2% MoM to US$99m vs US$97M in October. Traders threaded the market cautiously, as Trump’s victory has posed uncertainty to emerging markets. Foreigners went risk off in the Philippines, ending four straight months of net inflow. The barrage of selling sent the YTD foreign flows back to the negative territory at -US$309m after briefly turning turning positive last month, with heavy outflows seen in large caps.



Foreign participation improved to 52%  largely due the MSCi rebalancing (estimated outflow of around US$20m).  Excluding the liquidity event, foreign-local split remained at 50-50.

  • Large caps took most of the beating in November as foreigners shifted to a risk off stance after Trump won the US Presidential Election. Locals too were  on back foot as outflows continued to be elevated and as the 3Q earnings season came to an end.
  • ICT was the top foreign sold name month with a net outflow of US$72m. The stock swung 12% to close -6.6% after Trump vowed to raise tariffs on Mexico, China and Canada. Property names ALI -US$62m and SMPH -US$32m were also in the top the net outflow list with both names falling more than 10% on the back of weak 3Q residential reservations sales.
  • Banks were not spared from the selloff despite reporting strong 3Q results. Both BDO and BPI both saw large outflows at US$33m each, while MBT saw outflow of US$6m. BDO though managed to eke out some gains come month end, after trailing early part of the month.  Market proxies SM and AC both turned net sold alongside the market.
  • No notable inflows this month. AREIT was the top foreign bought name but for only US$2.8m.

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Breadth of the market was sharply skewed to the negative in November with 26/30 index members in the red (and over 3/4 by more -2%). Worst laggard in the benchmark was BLOOM -28.83% as investors digested weak results. Its bottomline sunk to a net loss of -P472m in 3Q as the profitability of Solaire Entertainment City declined at a time when depreciation and interest expense related to Solaire North came into the books as well. With that sharp decline this month, BLOOM is now the 2nd worst performing stock in the benchmark YTD, -44.31%, only next to NIKL -45.8%. Its market cap is now down to US$1.07b, which is now lower than e-games player PLUS’ US$1.64b. PLUS shares conversely rallied +8.46% MoM in November and is now +172.5% YTD.

Other big laggards include ACEN -19.92% MoM after its 3Q core profit fell -69% QoQ due to seasonally low output and an outage at SLTEC. Furthermore, sentiment on renewables appear to be clouded as well by expectations that Trump will enforce policies that will favor fossil fuel sources. Another one was WLCON -19.25% after once again reporting weak 3Q numbers. Outlook moving forward also looks downbeat due to the worsening state of the real estate market.

In terms of sectors, one broadly negative space was the property sector (SMPH -13.87% MoM, ALI -12.54% and, outside the PSEi, RLC -13.78% and MEG -11.69%) as dismal pre-sales figures and rising inventories in 3Q soured sentiment across the board and vastly overshadowed the incremental benefit from expected further rate cuts. The imminent ban on POGOs by year-end also remains a major headwind for the sector and will likely result to a further increase in office vacancies, especially in the Manila Bay area.

On the opposite end, one bright spot once again, at least in terms of results, was the banking sector. That said, share price performance looked mixed this month with BPI -10.2% MoM dragged down by outflows, trailing behind peers BDO +1.11% and MBT +0.33%.

Consumer names were mostly lower as well notwithstanding inline or better than expected results by most companies. Biggest decliners in the sector was URC -19.9% MoM as its results stood out due to a substantial miss: 3Q net profit fell -54% YoY, dragged by its sugar and domestic branded segments. Amid the sharp weakness in share price, note that the company’s buyback program became quite active in the latter half of the month. That said, the stock still settled November at its lowest close in 13 years.

Another stock that had an active buyback program was index heavyweight and widely-held ICT, which basically tracked the losses in the broader index, -6.57% MoM. ICT was one of the most heavily sold names by foreign investors in the latter part of the month as sentiment on the name appears to be weighed down by expectations that Trump, as promised during his campaign, may enforce tariffs which could be detrimental for global trade.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

3Q24 GDP slows to 5.2% on weak government spending, lower exports. GDP grew at 5.2% in 3Q24, moderating from the 6.4% growth rate in 2Q. This came in below consensus forecast of 5.7%.

On the demand side, the slowdown can be largely attributed to the softness in government spending (+5.0%) and lower exports (-1.0%). Private consumption grew at a faster rate of 5.1% in 3Q from 4.7% in 2Q. Private investments likewise accelerated to 13.1% in 3Q24 from 11.6% in 2Q and rebounded from the anemic -0.3% growth rate in 3Q23 on the back of stronger durable equipment and inventory re-stocking. Construction slowed to 8.9%, after public construction moderated to 3.7% (from 22% in 2Q) and offset sustained growth in private construction (11.9% from 10.3%). Net imports grew, after goods exports fell 3%, weighing on overall GDP.

On the supply side, the agri sector continued to fare poorly, declining by 2.8% from 2.3% the previous quarter. Industry and services sectors also slowed to 5.0% and 6.3%, respectively

This brings 9M24 GDP growth to 5.8%, below the government’s target of 6-7% but in line with Bloomberg consensus median forecast of 5.8%. To reach Regis’ FY24E of 6.0%, 4Q GDP should pick up to 6.5%.

BSP Governor signals easing on course. BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that either a 25bps cut or a pause is on the table at the Monetary Board’s final policy meeting on Dec 19, saying “We are still in the easing cycle. Either we cut in Dec or we cut in the next meeting. But gradual cuts only.

He also said that the BSP is intervening in the foreign exchange market, focusing on preventing excessive volatility. Regis anticipates the BSP to cut its policy rate by another 25bps in December, in line with Jefferies’ expectation of a 25bps cut at the Fed’s Dec meeting

 

Inflation picks up to 2.3% in October. Headline inflation rose to 2.3% in October from 1.9% in September. This is in line with consensus forecast of 2.3% and at the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 2.0-2.8%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose by 0.2%, largely due to higher food prices, which rose by 0.5% MoM. This increase was largely driven by upticks in the prices of vegetables, corn, fruits/nuts, and meat, which were partly attributed to weather disturbances.

Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the fourth consecutive month by 0.5%, bringing the cumulative decline to 1.4%. Low base effects from the implementation of price ceilings on rice in Oct 2023 pushed up the YoY growth rate to 9.6% in October from 5.7% in September. Outside of food, inflation was tempered by lower electricity prices (-0.7% MoM) and petrol prices (-0.2%); but admittedly, these two items could pick up again in succeeding periods.

Core inflation remained stable at 2.4%, the same as in September, with core non-food inflation easing to 2.6% from 2.8%

Brings average YT-October inflation rate to 3.3%, on track to meet the BSP’s risk-adjusted inflation forecast of 3.1% – in line with Regis’ FY24E of 3.1%. Regis does not expect this to derail our expectations of a policy rate reduction (25bps) by the Bangko Sentral (BSP) in Dec.

Trade deficit widens to US$5.1bn in September. The country’s trade deficit widened to US$5.09bn in September. This is the highest level since March 2023 and was wider by 16% MoM and 43% YoY. Imports rose 10% YoY, 2% MoM to US$11.3bn. Strong rebounds on imports of consumer goods (+21% YoY) and raw materials (+20%). These more than offset the 11% drop in fuel imports and the slack in capital goods imports (+1%). Exports dropped 8% YoY, 7% MoM to US$6.3bn. Electronics exports fell 23% YoY to US$3.15bn; This was broadly the same level in the past 9 months. Brings YT-September trade deficit to US$39.4bn, flat YoY.

 

Government records budget surplus of P6bn in October as revenues rebound. The government recorded a budget surplus of P6.3bn in October, reversing the deficit of P34bn in Oct 2023. This was due to strong revenue collections. Revenue collections in October were 23% YoY higher, with tax revenues up 17% YoY and non-tax revenues up 82% YoY. BIR collections rose 19% in October, accelerating from the 13% pace in 9M24 due to higher collections of VAT, personal/corporate income taxes, documentary stamp tax, excise tax on tobacco and percentage taxes. Customs collections rose 11.5% YoY in Oct, much faster than the 5% pace in 9M24. Expenditures rose 11%, despite the 6% YoY decline in interest payments. The latter was attributed to “timing issues.” Primary spending rose 14% YoY, accelerating from the 5% pace in 3Q24 and 9% pace in 9M24. The rebound in primary spending was attributed to (a) the release of the first tranche of gov’t employee salary adjustments, the (b) release of FY2022 performance-based bonus for the Department of Education, as well as (c) higher spending on public works, transportation, and social/health projects. Brings YT-October budget deficit to P964bn, lower by 5% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. As of 9M24, the budget deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at 5.14%.

BOP posts US$724m deficit in October; End-October GIR settles at US$111bn. The BSP reported that the country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a deficit of US$724mn in October. This was a reversal of the US$3.5bn surplus in Sep 2024 and the US$1.51bn surplus in October 2023. This brings YT-October BOP position to a US$4.4bn surplus, higher than the US$3.2bn in the same period last year. BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn in FY24, equivalent to 0.3% of GDP. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-October stood at US$111.1bn, slightly lower than the US$112.7bn in end-September. GIR level is equivalent to 8.0 months’ worth of imports. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$104bn in end-2024.

Remittances rise 3.3% YoY in September. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 3.3% YoY to US$3.01bn in September due to acceleration in land-based remittances from the USA (+4.3% in Sep vs 2.5%% in 8M24). Sea-based remittances growth remain sluggish at 1.5%. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 2%, slowest paces in ten months. This brings YT-September remittances up by 3% YoY to US$25.2bn. This is broadly in line with the BSP’s target growth rate of +3% for the year.

 

Government keen on retaining rice tariff cut. The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) is recommending keeping the present rice tariff rate of 15% for now, according to Senator Cynthia Villar during plenary deliberations on the proposed 2025 budget of the Department of Agriculture. Officials cited foreign exchange rate concerns, which would impact rice prices.

Under Executive Order 62, the tariff rate shall be subjected to a review every four months, with the next review set in February 2025. Agriculture Secretary Tiu Larel Jr. said that “a lot bought “palay” (rice paddy) at a high price of P35 per kilo in July-Aug” and they will need about six months to sell to avoid incurring huge losses, which explains the gradual decrease in the retail price of rice.

Senate approves P6.35tn 2025 Budget. The Senate approved on third reading the bill containing the P6.352tn proposed national budget for fiscal year 2025, which is 10% YoY higher than 2024 and is equivalent to 21.9% of GDP. Senate will consult with the Lower House during Bicam regarding (a) P39bn cash aid program (removed by Senate) and (b) reduction in the budget of the Office of the VP to P733mn from P2bn. Senate increased the budgets for the AFP Modernization, calamity fund, ASEAN Summit in 2026, air traffic management but no details on magnitude of increases were provided.

Some key items of the 2025 budget: Debt service (i.e. interest payments) will increase by 27% YoY; Primary spending (excluding debt service) will increase by 8% YoY, driven largely by the 17% increase in allocations to LGUs; Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively.

Government infrastructure spending up 12% YoY in 9M24. Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 12% YoY to 1.43tn in 9M24. This was equivalent to 6.1% of GDP, better than the 5.9% outturn in the same period last year and the government’s year-end target of 5.6%. The government is targeting to spend P1.47tn this year, up 4% YoY. Higher-than-expected spending was attributed to accelerated infrastructure spending of the DPWH especially for carry-over and ongoing projects, and the direct payments made by development partners for foreign-assisted rail projects of the DOTr. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP.

 

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth slightly picks up to 11% in September; Domestic liquidity (M3) growth mildly decelerates to 5.4%.Preliminary data showed that outstanding loans of universal/commercial banks rose by 11% YoY in September, mildly accelerating from the 10.7% pace in August. Lifted by faster loan growth in manufacturing, utilities, trade, and real estate. Consumer loans grew by 23.4%, mildly decelerating from the 23.7% rate in the previous month due to slower growth in auto loans (18.6% from 19.3%). Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth slowed to 5.4% in September from 5.5% in August.

Vehicle sales pick up in October, rising by 4.9% YoY, 1.2% MoM. CAMPI/TMA reported industry-wide vehicle sales grew to 40,003 units in October, up 4.9% YoY, 1.2% MoM. Note that the Philippine International Motor Show was held during the month. This brings YT-October sales up by 8.9% YoY to 384,310 units.

Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) maintained its market share at 46.4% in YT-October, suggesting that it sold 178,435 units during the period, up by 10% YoY. Implied unit sales for October alone at 19,347 units, up by 7.5% YoY, +5% MoM. TMP appears on track to beat its target sales of 215k units (+7%), as it only need to sell 18.3k units per month for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales for the rest of the year only need to average 13.7k units per month.

TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).
