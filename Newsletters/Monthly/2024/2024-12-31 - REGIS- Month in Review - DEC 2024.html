REGIS REPORT

31 December 2024
 

While the PCOMP's 1.3% decline in December to 6,528.79 further narrowed the benchmark's FY gain to 1.2%, the year 2024 nonetheless saw an end to a four-year losing streak for Philippine equities.

Cyclicals led the December drop with the Financials (-4.3% MoM) and Property (-4.9% MoM) sector indices down the most as investors continued to weigh the evolving rate outlook, with higher terminal rates for the Fed seen limiting the space for looser monetary conditions for the BSP.

For the year though, banks were still largely favored as the more liquid market proxies for the Philippines, and with more resilient and visible earnings. Financials (+24.1% YoY) was the second best-performing sector index, only following Services (+29.7% YoY) that was lifted by PLUS (+239.4% YoY), CNVRG (+92.6% YoY), and ICT (+56.4% YoY). On this note, gaming saw very divergent performances for PLUS and BLOOM (-53.5% YoY), as online gaming revenues surged and took share from Entertainment City casinos that slumped amid slower-than-expected tourist arrivals and the POGO exit.

Following a strong 3Q run, Property (-16.7% YoY) fell towards year-end as Trump’s re-election clouded the rate outlook. The sector was only behind Mining (-21.7% YoY) as this year’s worst performer as nickel miners dropped amid the global nickel supply glut. Also, developers underperforming held conglomerates back from any further re-rating, dragging the Holdings (-7.6% YoY) sector index. Meanwhile, consumer names were mixed amid the high inflation environment.

On the macro front, inflation was slightly higher at 2.5% in November, from 2.3% in October. This was in-line with forecasts and took YT-November average inflation to 3.2%. Although food inflation accelerated to 3.4% in November (from 2.9%), rice prices continued to head lower. As of early December, the price of well-milled rice had already dropped to P40/kg, down 5% MoM and down 15% QoQ from P47/kg in September. On rates, the BSP delivered a 25-bp rate cut to 5.75% in its mid-December policy setting meeting, just as the Street expected. The central bank also raised its risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2025 to 3.4% from 3.3% while 2026 remains unchanged at 3.7%. BSP Gov. Remolona had stated that further cuts are expected in 2025, and potentially as early as its first meeting for the year that is usually held in the third week of February.

Net foreign outflows clocked at US$104m in December, bringing FY 2024 net outflows to US$408m. This marks the seventh consecutive year that foreigners were net sellers of Philippine shares, with the last net foreign inflow year in 2017. 

Also, ADTO weakened further to US$67m in December, taking FY 2024 ADTO to just US$75m.

The currency appreciated by 1.4% MoM to 57.843 in December, tempering the FY 2024 depreciation of the peso to 4.4%. Meanwhile, the PH10Y yield climbed 18bps to 6.176% in December (and 18bps YoY).

LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Market breadth improved in December with 17 advancers outnumbering 13 decliners. BLOOM (-16.4% MoM) was the biggest laggard for the second straight month as the POGO exit continued to dampen the outlook for Entertainment City casinos. Also among the worst performers this month were property developers ALI (-8.4% MoM) and SMPH (-4.7% MoM), as well as Big 3 banks BDO (-6.2% MoM), MBT (-5.6% MoM), and BPI (-3.7% MoM) as broad sentiment on cyclicals remained weak amid shifting cues on rate cuts.

On the flipside, YTD-underperformers NIKL (+17.5% MoM) and WLCON (+10.0% MoM) were top gainers in December thanks to bargain hunting, though they still ranked among the top 5 largest decliners for FY 2024, down by 34.2% YoY and 30.5% YoY, resp. due to poor earnings and expectations that this would continue into 2025. Rounding up that list are BLOOM (-53.5% YoY), JGS (-46.1%), and URC (-33.2%) that were all weighed down by weak results.

Meanwhile, top performers for the year were a mixed bag. CNVRG (+92.6% YoY) staged an impressive comeback, fully regaining its sharp loss from the previous year thanks to improved earnings and optimism over its dividend outlook. ICT (+56.4% YoY) remained a market darling with strong and visible earnings, while MBT (+40.4% YoY) outperformed its Big 3 peers after lagging in the re-rating over the prior year. CNPF (+35.5% YoY) was the top consumer play in the PCOMP for the second year in a row as its earnings performance bested peers that saw high inflation dampen demand and compress margins. GLO (+27.0% YoY) rounds up the top 5 biggest gainers for 2024, also on a rebound year after its large underperformance the year prior. 

Thematically, banks remained largely in favor this year (Financials +24.1% YoY) as investors still flocked to the sector for its resilient results and better liquidity. This was the second best-performing sector for 2024, only behind Services (+29.7% YoY) that was lifted not just by CNVRG and ICT but more so by PLUS (+239.4% YoY!). With online gaming taking off strongly this year and taking share from traditional integrated resorts / casinos, PLUS' gaming revenue and earnings have now eclipsed BLOOM's. Consequently, PLUS closed out the year with a market cap of over $2bn that is now more than double that of BLOOM due to their divergent share price moves.

Following a short-lived 3Q rally, Property (-16.7% YoY) sharply fell in 4Q as the monetary policy outlook quickly turned with the Trump re-election, clouding earlier expectations for a more favorable 2025 set-up for the residential and office segments. The sector was only behind Mining (-21.7% YoY) as the worst performer this year with nickel miners slumping on the global nickel supply glut.

Consumer names saw mixed performance for the year as the high inflation environment impacted the sector in different ways. Companies catering to lower- to lower-middle-income segments such as CNPF (+35.5% YoY) and PGOLD (+14.7% YoY) outperformed while middle- to upper-middle-income plays such as URC (-33.2% YoY) and WLCON (-31.6% YoY) lagged.

 
MACRO NEWS

Monetary board cuts policy rate 25bps as expected. The BSP's Monetary board reduced its policy rate by 25bps to 5.75%, in-line with consensus expectations. BSP also reduced its risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2025 slightly higher to 3.4% from 3.3%, while 2026 remains unchanged at 3.7%. This is based on the inflation outlook continuing to lean to the upside due to potential upward adjustments in transport fares and electricity but may be offset by the impact of lower import tariffs on rice. Given the within-target inflation outlook, Governor Remolona states that further cuts are expected next year but with 100 bps being "too much" and 0 bps being "too little." He also stated that barring any big surprises, the BSP will continue its easing cycle as any small surprises will not warrant a change in current policy direction.

Inflation inches up to 2.5% in November. Headline inflation inched up to 2.5% in November from 2.3% in October. This is in-line with consensus forecast of 2.5% and came in at the midpoint of the BSP's forecast range of 2.2-3.0%. On a MoM basis, CPI rose 0.4%, primarily due to higher food prices which increased by 0.7% MoM. The increase was mainly driven by price upticks in vegetables and fish as several typhoons hit the country in the month of November. Rice CPI sequentially dipped for the fifth consecutive month by 1.5%, which brings the cumulative decline to 3.0%. Prices of well-milled rice stood at P42-52/kg for local and P45-54 for imported on Dec 3 (vs. P43-54 for local and P45-55 for imported rice on Oct 31) based on the Dept of Agriculture’s data. Outside of food, petrol prices which saw a slight decrease of 0.2% last month—saw an increase of 1.2% in November due to the weaker peso and global oil supply concerns. Core inflation and core non-food inflation both remained relatively stable at 2.5% and 2.7% respectively, as both saw a small increase of 0.1% from last month. The latest print brings average YT-November inflation rate to 3.2%, on track to meet the BSP's risk adjusted inflation forecast of 3.1%—in-line with Regis' FY24E of 3.1%.

Remittances rise 2.7% YoY in October. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.7% YoY to US$3.08bn. This was primarily driven by the acceleration in land-based remittances from Singapore up 10.4% YoY and 17.1% MoM. Sea-based remittances growth remain slow, only up 0.6% YoY. In PHP terms, remittances rose 3.6% for the month, an increase from September (+2.0%) but still the second slowest for the year. Bringing YT-October remittances up 3% YoY to US$28.3bn. Broadly in line with the BSP's target growth rate of +3.0% for the year.

Trade deficit expands to $5.80bn in October. The October trade deficit is the largest in 26 months since the US$5.99bn gap in August 2022 and was wider 37% YoY and 14% MoM. Imports rose 11% YoY, 5% MoM to US$11.96bn, primarily driven by increased imports of consumer goods (+29% YoY), as well as electronics and capital goods (both up +21% YoY). These overshadowed the 10% drop in fuel imports and other goods. Meanwhile exports dropped 5% YoY, 1.5% MoM to US$6.16bn as Electronics exports declined 23% YoY to US$2.86bn. The latest reading brings the YT-October trade deficit to US$45.21bn, +4% YoY.

Current account deficit widens to US$5.7bn in 3Q24. The BSP reported that the country’s current account deficit widened to US$5.67bn in 3Q24, up 10% QoQ/154% YoY and accounted for 5.2% of GDP. This was due to a 10% QoQ, YoY expansion in the trade deficit to US$18.5bn, on the back of lower exports and higher imports. Electronic exports fell 27% YoY to US$4.6bn, which the BSP attributed this to a shift in international demand for new electronic products (e.g., AI, EVs, solar panels, batteries, and high-end semiconductors). Higher imports were driven by the 31% YoY expansion in iron and steel imports to US$1.4bn, as well as miscellaneous manufactures, such as magnetic tapes suitable for computer use, which rose by 15.1% to US$1.5bn. Bringing YT-September current account deficit to US$12.93bn, up 77% YoY and equivalent to 3.9% of GDP. This is trending higher than the BSP’s FY24 projection of a US$4.7bn current account deficit (1.0% of GDP).

November balance of payments deficit hits US$2.3bn. The country's overall balance of payments (BOP) for the month posted a deficit of US$2.3bn, higher than both of the deficits of US$724mn in Oct 2024 and the US$216mn deficit in Nov 2023. Bringing YT-November registered a surplus of US$2.1bn, lower than the US$3.0bn surplus recorded the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-November stood at US$108.5bn, a slight drop from the US$111.1bn in end-October. The GIR level is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$106bn in end-2024.

 

Dollar reserves slip 2% at end-November.  The Philippines' gross international reserves (GIR) saw a 2.4% MoM decrease to $108.5bn from $111.1bn at the end of October. This was primarily due to net foreign currency withdrawals to settle foreign currency debt obligations and payment of expenditures. The level of dollar reserves by end-Nov was enough to cover about 4.3x the country's short-term external debt and also equivalent to 7.8x months' worth of imports of goods and payments. The BSP expects the country's GIR to settle at $106bn by end-2024. Foreign investments dipped by 2% MoM to $91.bn as of end-Nov from 93.1bn end-Oct. Foreign exchange declined 18.2% MoM to $1.75bn from $2.14bn end-Oct. Buffers in the form of gold were valued at $11.03bn, down 2.9% MoM from $11.35bn at end-Oct.

Government records budget deficit of P213bn in November.The government recorded a budget deficit of P213bn in November, 130% higher than the deficit of P93bn in November 2023. Revenue collections in November were flat YoY (-0.6%), with tax revenues up 13% YoY, offset by a significant decline in non-tax revenues of 68%. BIR collections rose 18% YoY in November, accelerating from the 14% pace in 10M24 due to higher collections of VAT, personal/corporate income taxes, documentary stamp tax, and excise taxes. Customs collections dropped slightly by 2% YoY in November, compared to the 5% growth in 10M24. Expenditures accelerated by 27% in November, as both significant primary expenses and interest payments were incurred in the month. Primary spending rose 26% YoY, faster than the 10% pace in 10M24. The continued increase in primary spending was attributed to (a) larger tax allotments disbursed to local government units (LGUs), (b) higher spending on road, defense infrastructure, and social/educational projects, and (c) personnel services requirements. Interest payments were up 37%, as coupon payments were made to domestic debt and global bond issuances, alongside forex fluctuations. This brought the YTD November budget deficit to P1.2tn, higher by 6% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP. As of 9M24, the budget deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at 5.14%.

President signs 2025 Budget into law; vetoes P194bn worth of line items. President Marcos Jr. signed into law the General Appropriations Act (GAA) for 2025 last December 30, amounting to P6.326tn, 9.7% higher YoY and equivalent to 22% of GDP. This was lower than the original proposed budget of P6.352tn (-0.4% vs previous) following the veto of P194bn worth of line items approved by Congress. Items vetoed were: P26.065bn worth of projects under the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH); P168.240bn allocated under “Unprogrammed Appropriations”.  The Department of Education has the largest share of the budget at P1.053tn (+8.7% YoY), followed by DPWH at P1.034tn (+3.6% YoY).  Secretary of Finance Ralph Recto said that the 2025 budget will be supported by P4.64tn (+5.0% YoY, 16.2% of GDP) worth of revenues. This implies a budget deficit of P1.68tn (~5.8% of GDP), higher than the government’s "emerging" 2024 target of P1.33tn (~5% of GDP). Note that the "emerging" 2024 budget deficit target of P1.33tn is lower than the programmed 2024 budget deficit target of P1.48tn (~5.6% of GDP) due to higher non-tax revenues.

Infrastructure spending rises 11% YoY in 10M24. Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) grew by 11% YoY to P1.28tn in 10M24. The government raised its target to P1.54tn, up 8.5% YoY (from P1.47tn, up 4%) and equivalent to 5.8% of GDP. This growth was driven by the accelerated implementation of infrastructure activities, including both ongoing and carryover projects of the DPWH and foreign-assisted railway projects of the DOTr, financed from prior year’s unprogrammed appropriations. For 2025, the government is targeting to spend P1.54tn, equivalent to 5.4% of GDP.

Government debt rises 0.8% MoM to P16.02 trillion as of end-October. The national government's outstanding debt rose to P16.02tn in end-October, 0.8% (+P127bn) higher than the end-September level of P15.89tn. Primarily driven by valuation impact of peso depreciation against the USD from P56.01/USD at end-Sept to P58.19/USD end-Oct. Domestic debt declined slightly by 0.4% (-P46bn) MoM to P10.89tn due to the net redemption of government securities. External debt rose 3.5% (+P173bn) MoM to P5.13tn due to net foreign loan availments of P20.5bn as well as foreign exchange movements. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of 60.6% by year-end and is seeking to reduce the ratio further to 56.3% by 2028. As of end-Sep 2024, the government's debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 61.3% vs. 60.9% end-Jun 2024.

Consumer loans of the Philippine banks grew 16.5% YoY to P2,916.8bn. While this was slower than the 19.7% pace in 2Q24, it was still up a respectable 3.9% QoQ. Also, the 16.5% growth was faster than the 11.2% pace for total loans. That said, non-consumer loans accelerated to 9.8% YoY, consistent with banks’ strong corporate loan growth in 3Q. Consumer loans now account for 22.0% of total loans, a new record high. Encouragingly, NPL ratio for the sector improved to 5.67%, down 15bps QoQ

 

 

The slowdown was seen across all segments, but was largely driven by mortgages, which saw growth decelerate sharply from 13.5% in 2Q24 to 8.1% YoY in 3Q24. Balances were still up 2.2% QoQ though. NPLs for the segment were broadly stable at 6.78% up a tad from the 6.72% in the previous quarter.

Motor Vehicle (MV) loans grew 17.3% YoY (was 18.7% in 2Q). Car loans were still up 18.7% YoY (vs 20.3%), while motorcycle loans were down 2.6% YoY, the 7th consecutive quarter of YoY contraction. MV NPLs improved to 5.36%, down 36bps QoQ, thanks largely to 4-wheelers, whose NPLs dropped 38bps QoQ to 4.70%. Motorcycle NPLs were still high at 16.97%.

Credit card growth stayed elevated, growing 27.7% YoY, though slowing a bit from the 28.8% in 2Q. NPLs here improved to 4.35%, down 21bps QoQ.

International arrivals reach 5.31mn as of end-November, behind target. Data from the Department of Tourism (DoT) exhibited that tourist arrivals for the month of November declined slightly 2% YoY and MoM to 432k. This was 63% of 2019 average. Korean tourists remain as the top source of visitor arrivals but visitors from Korea was flat both YoY and MoM. Chinese tourists declined 19% YoY, marking five consecutive months of YoY decreases. The DoT stated that visa liberalization delays and geopolitical issues may have impacted projections, such as the DoT anticipated 2mn Chinese visitors with the e-visa system implementation which was suspended by the DFA. Bringing YT-November tourist arrivals to 5.3mn tourist arrivals, up 9% YoY and 71% of 2019 average. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, which is up 41% YoY or 93% of 2019 levels. 2023 tourist arrivals hit 5.5mn or 66% of pre-covid.

VAT refund program for non-resident tourists signed into law. Republic Act (RA) 12079 will establish a VAT Refund System for goods purchased at accredited retail outlets, provided that these goods are taken out of the country within 60 days and meet a minimum transaction requirement of P3,000. An estimated 30% increase in tourist spending is projected with the new law in place, and this is expected to benefit both large-scale industries and SMEs. The National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) projected that the law will raise P3.3bn to P5.7bn in additional revenues as well as create 4,400-7,100 jobs a year from 2024 to 2028.

 

POGO firms to cease operations by end-December.PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco said that POGO licenses will be canceled by the end of the year and the industry is on track to cease operations by end-December. Recall that during his State of the Nation Address in July 2024, President Marcos announced that a ban on offshore gaming would be effective by the end of the year. Mr. Tengco said there are now only 17 POGO firms operating, from 48 companies earlier this year. We estimate that the POGO industry occupies about 4% of Metro Manila office stock (equivalent to about 600,000sqm). POGO firms are predominantly located in the Manila Bay area; thus, we expect Manila Bay area property to be the most negatively affected by the ban. Colliers estimates that after the ban is fully implemented, Manila Bay area office and residential vacancies will rise from c. 30% currently to 50%. Although the developers we cover generally have minimal direct exposure to POGO office tenants or mainland Chinese residential buyers, a few of them have meaningful exposure to Manila Bay area property: 27% of SM Prime’s NAV is from its coastal redevelopment project in the Manila Bay area; and 10% of Megaworld’s NAV is from its land in the Manila Bay area.

PAGCOR raises 2024 GGR target to P350B. The Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corp (PAGCOR) raised its 2024 gross gaming revenue (GGR) target from P336B to P350B. This would represent an increase of 23% from the 2023 figure (P285bn). 9M24 GGR grew 29% to P265B and represents 76% of the P350B GGR target set by PAGCOR for the full year. The 9M24 GGR breakdown is as follows: PAGCOR operated: +16% YoY to P12bn; Licensed casino: -5% YoY to P149bn; Online gaming: +218% to P103bn.

Marcos, Sara Duterte ratings continue to fall in latest Pulse Asia survey. The approval ratings of President Marcos saw a decline by 2 percentage points to 48% in November from 50% in September. The decrease mainly stems from the Visayas (48% from 52%) and Mindanao (14% from 26%), while his approval ratings in Luzon and NCR stayed relatively stable at 65% (from 61%) and 51% (from 52%), respectively. In terms of approval ratings by socioeconomic classes, Class E declined the most to 35% (from 47%), Class D slightly to 51% (from 52%), while Class ABC rose to 39% (from 35%). Vice President Sara Duterte's approval ratings slumped 10 percentage points to 50% from 60%. The significant drop was driven by all three regions, with Visayas down to 51% (from 71%), Mindanao to 80% (from 93%), Luzon to 40% (from 46%), and NCR to 34% (from 36%).
