REGIS REPORT

01 August 2024
 

The PSEi recovered in July and posted its first monthly gain since February, +3.23% MoM to 6619.09, as investors increasingly priced in an imminent 25bp rate cut in the forthcoming BSP policy meeting in August, and  potential 50bp total rate cuts before the year ends. This was helped by increased expectations that the US Fed is en route to take a dovish turn in policy for the remainder of the year, with futures now pricing in potentially up to 3 rate cuts through the 3 remaining Fed meetings this year.

On the ground, inflation in June eased to 3.7%, lower than 3.9% street forecast and 3.9% prior. This was helped by a 5.7% YoY easing in electricity, gas and fuel costs. On a MoM basis, CPI was flat as it was tempered by declines in utilities (-1.2% MoM) and transport costs (-0.3%). The headline CPI number would have been lower if it weren’t for the food inflation which remained elevated at 6.1% YoY (and 0.6% MoM), driven in large part by rice which was up 22.5% YoY (but 0.1% MoM). With the enactment of the rice tariff reduction however, rice prices could start to ease in the coming months.

The easing in CPI, along with some dovish pronouncements from BSP Governor Remolona, helped propel the benchmark from the 6300/6400 levels. Shares however found some resistance at 6800 in the latter part of July amid the President’s announcement to ban POGOs in the country. POGOs will be given until year-end to unwind operations. Subsequent to the initial announcement, PAGCOR Chairman Al Tengco clarified that the ban will cover all licensed online gambling operations targeting offshore users, which are officially referred to as internet gaming licensees (IGL).  If a full ban does push through, it should be negative for the property sector, particularly for the office leasing market.

Another key area of focus, particularly in the latter part of July, is the start of the 2Q earnings reporting season. Thus far, results from the three biggest lenders look generally inline, notwithstanding higher NPLs from BDO and BPI (MBT’s was lower). However, the same cannot be said about the consumer sector (although only two reporters so far) with RRHI and WLCON both showing weaker-than-expected results. Nonetheless the recovery prospect for demand in the coming quarters still looks intact, as the imminent easing in inflation is expected to provide a boost to consumer demand. With the POGO ban announcement and a mixed start to results season thus far, the benchmark once again encountered resistance at the key 6800-line, leading it trim gains and ultimately settle July at the 6600-level.

Along with the equity benchmark’s recovery, the PHP similarly posted its first monthly gain since February, +0.42% MoM to 58.365. This should remove concerns over the BSP cutting rates ahead of the Fed, and as such further strengthens the odds of an August rate reduction at home.

Key data
 

Average daily turnover for the month barely improved to US$86m vs US$82m in the previous month and just like in June, the absence of liquidity resulted in sharp moves. Nonetheless, momentum for the month was to the upside with the index recovering from four straight months of underperformance, sending the benchmark to retest key 6800 levels. This was met by profit takers, however. Foreigners also turned net buyers in the market after four straight months of outflows, optimistic of a potential rate cut in the near term. Net foreign inflow stood at US$60m for the month. Foreigners were more active, taking 51% of the turnover split.

  • Foreigners turned buyers in SM after two months of heavy outflows related to a decrease in its MSCi weighting. The stock is the top foreign bought name this month for US$25.9m as foreign investors are buying the name as market proxy sending its share price back to pre-MSCi announcement levels. The rest of the conglomerate space continued to see foreign outflows but at a much lighter pace vs the month prior. AEV (-8.6%) though has not recovered from its deletion from the MSCi; the stock is still trading near its 52-week low level.
  • Sector wise, Banks saw the biggest inflow this month with both BDO and BPI topping the net foreign bought names. Foreign buying in the sector accelerated after the release of inline 2Q numbers. The sector managed to finish the month with gains despite the selling pressure late in the month.
  • Consumer names meanwhile saw the same trend as in June. Foreign interest remained in URC given its cheap valuations, while foreigners continue to sell JFC (-US$12.1m) and WLCON (-US$3.1m).

 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The breadth of the benchmark was skewed to the positive in July with 21 winners vs 9 losers.

 

Notwithstanding generally weak results announced by WLCON (-2.23% MoM) and RRHI (+2.71% MoM), a handful of consumer names were actually among the best performers in the PSEI in July with PGOLD (+8.68%, MoM), CNPF (+7.31% MoM), and URC (+7.01% MoM). An even bigger gainer outside the index however was SEVN (+15.53% MoM), which we have a BUY rating on. It further added to its +31% gain in June and extended its YTD gain to over +60%.

 

The banking sector also tracked the general rise in the market with, buoyed primarily by BDO +6.86% and its peers BPI +1.85% and MBT +1.7%.  The sector at large however did suffer some volatility in the latter part of the month as investors parsed through their results, though profits were largely in line. 

 

Despite the POGO ban announcement, the property sector also finished the month in the green (+2.26% MoM), albeit at a lesser pace than the main index, supported by gains in main plays ALI  (+3.51% MoM) and SMPH (+1.77% MoM). REITs also saw continued gains with RCR (+7.62% MoM) and AREIT (+7.25% MoM) carrying their momentum from June, as investors locked in their 6%-7% gains with interest rates widely expected to come down through the remainder of the year. That said, there were some negative spots in the sector, particularly those with a substantial exposure to the Manila Bay office leasing market such as DD (-13.55% MoM) and its REIT DDMPR (-10.34% MoM). Regis estiamtes the POGO industry occupies 6% of Metro Manila office space, but a disproportionately large 30% of the Manila Bay office space. As such if a POGO exodus does occur, the office vacancy rate in Metro Manila may rise to 22% from 16% currently, and in the Manila Bay area it can rise to as high as 50%.

 

The best performing stock in the benchmark was ACEN (+9.8% MoM) which actually started to rally through the latter part of the month (in contrast to the PSEi’s finding a resistance at the 6800-level). ACEN's 2Q is expected to be an especially strong quarter given the company’s new capacity at the start of 2Q and its net seller exposure to the electricity spot market.

 

One power name that has already reported results is MER (+6.56% MoM) whose 1H core income surged +21% YoY to P23.2b, well ahead of both Regis and street forecasts. Electricity volume growth in 2Q remained strong at +8.5%, which was unsurprising given the heatwave in April-May. The surprise driver of the blockbuster profits from its unregulated businesses (generation and RES), which delivered P6.2b in profits for 2Q alone. 

 

On the opposite end, the worst performing stock was BLOOM (-13.04% MoM), which settled the week at a new 52-week low. Sentiment on the name appears to have been dragged down initially by data from PAGCOR showing that GGR of licensed casinos in the country was flat QoQ and down -4% YoY in 2Q. While BLOOM itself is yet to report results, Okada Manila’s 2Q filing also showed that its GGR was virtually flat, which suggests a potentially soft 2Q as well for BLOOM. Another issue for BLOOM is that through the month there were several headlines over new casinos looking to start operations through the next 1-2 years, which could mean increased competition. Lastly, another notable headwind is the POGO ban which could curtail GGR coming from Chinese nationals affiliated with POGOs.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Lower energy prices drive June inflation lower to 3.7%. Headline inflation eased to 3.7% in June from 3.9% in May, lower than Street estimates of 3.9% and at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.4%-4.2%. On a MoM basis, CPI was flat, moderating slightly after rising +0.1% in May. Electricity CPI down by 13% YoY, 6.3% MoM due to implementation of staggered payment scheme to temper impact of higher generation costs in May. While operation of transport equipment eased to 3.5% YoY from 5.2% YoY on lower gasoline prices. Food inflation however rose to 6.1% from 5.8% due to faster increases in vegetable and meat prices, while rice inflation remained elevated at 22.5% YoY (+0.1% MoM).

Core inflation was steady at 3.1%, with non-food “core” CPI also steady at 3.3%. This brings 1H24 average inflation rate to 3.6%. Note that the BSP’s baseline inflation forecast for 2024 at 3.3%. BSP Governor Remolona said that even if inflation for the upcoming month of July breaches its target band of 2-4%, it won’t derail the monetary board’s planned rate cut on Aug 15. Governor Remolona previously said that the BSP could cut rates by 50bps this year, 25bps in August and another 25bps in 4Q. The next monetary board meeting on August 15.

GIR settles at US$104.7bn in end-June. Preliminary data showed the country’s GIR settled at US$104.7bn in end-June, down by 0.3% MoM (-US$315m), which represents 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The MoM decline is attributed to national government’s foreign currency payments and lower gold prices.

Remittances rise 3.6% YoY in May. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.6% YoY to US$2.58bn in May. In PHP terms, remittances expanded by 7.4% YoY. This was driven by higher remittances from Singapore (+5%), USA (+5%), Saudi Arabia (+4%), and UAE (+5%). This brings 5M24 remittances up 3% YoY to US$13.37bn. Note that the BSP is targeting remittances to grow by 3% YoY this year (suggests FY24E: US$34.5bn).

Government debt rises to P15.35tn in May. The national government’s outstanding debt reached P15.35tn in end-May, up 2.2% MoM (+P330bn). The increase is largely due new debt issuances and the impact of local currency depreciation on FX debt. Domestic debt increased by 1.3% MoM (+P134bn) to P10.44tn largely due to the net issuance of government securities. While external debt rose to P4.9tn, up 4.2% MoM (+P196bn) due to net foreign loan issuances and the upward revaluation of USD debt. For 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio is expected to settle at 60.6%, slightly higher than 2023’s 60.2%. After 2024, the government aims to gradually lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 56% by 2028.

Budget deficit narrows in June; 1H24 deficit widens by 11% YoY to P614bn. The national government recorded a lower budget deficit of P209bn in June, down 7% YoY. Revenues grew 11% YoY due to 74% YoY increase in non-tax revenues while expenditures rose by 3% YoY. Tax revenues grew 3.5% YoY, with BIR collections up 4.7% YoY and Customs collections up 0.7% YoY. The surge in non-tax revenues were due to undisclosed items unrelated to GOCCs dividends (-31% YoY) and privatization proceeds. Primary spending (excluding interest payments) grew 2.3% YoY. In 2Q24 alone, the budget deficit grew 22% YoY due to 18% YoY jump in spending (primary spending up 16% YoY). Prelude to 2Q24 GDP; favorable base effects have likely boosted GDP for the quarter. Brings 1H24 budget deficit up 11% YoY to P614bn; Primary deficit in 1H down 12% YoY.

BOP posts US$155mn deficit in June; End-June GIR rises to US$105bn. The country’s balance overall balance of payments position posted a deficit of US$155mn in June. This was a reversal of the US$1.997bn BOP surplus in May 2024 but lower than the US$606mn BOP deficit in June 2023. Officials cited government debt payments of foreign currency loans. This brings 1H24 BOP surplus to US$1.4bn, lower than the US$2.3bn surplus in the same period last year. Meanwhile, the final end-June GIR level rose to US$105.2bn in end-June from the US$105bn in end-May, which represents 7.7 months’ worth of imports.

Trade deficit widens by 5% YoY to US$4.6bn in May. Preliminary data showed the trade deficit widened to US$4.60bn in May, up 5% YoY. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was slightly lower than the US$4.73bn deficit in April. Imports were flat at US$10.93bn - despite a 21% YoY increase in fuel-related imports, imports of consumer goods and raw/intermediate goods were flattish (0%/1%) while capital goods imports fell 12%. Exports declined by 3% YoY to US$6.3bn due to lower electronic exports (-5%). This brings 5M24 trade deficit to US$20.59bn, down 13% YoY.

Government infrastructure outlay at P1.506tn in 2025. For 2025, infrastructure and other capital outlays (excluding subsidies to government-owned corporations and infrastructure transfers to local government units) are expected to jump by 3.04% to P1.28tn. The overall infrastructure outlay (i.e. including subsidies to government-owned corporations and transfers to LGUs) will decline slightly to P1.506tn in 2025 from P1.51tn in 2024. Infrastructure outlay is equivalent to c. 5-6% of GDP. 

 

NCR wage board approves P35 minimum wage hike. The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved a P35 hike in the daily minimum wage of workers in the National Capital Region (NCR). The new minimum wage for non-agri sector in NCR will rise by 5.7% to P645 (from P610), and for agri sector workers by 6.1% to P608 (from P573), effective July 17. This hike was much lower than wage petitions from labor groups, which ranged from P597 to P750. Recall that the government implemented a cumulative wage hike of 13% in NCR in 2022/2023 (vs. cumulative headline inflation of 12%). Gov’t estimates the wage order will directly impact 988,243 minimum wage earners in NCR. We note that the NCR wage hike will likely set the tone for possible wage hikes in other regions in the coming months.

FDI drops 37% YoY to US$556m in April. Foreign direct investments (FDI) net inflows amounted to US$556m in April, down 37% YoY. Equity capital placements in April came largely from Japan (47% of total), USA (21%), Malaysia (11%), and Singapore (9%). These were channeled primarily to manufacturing (36% of total), real estate (26%), trade (13%), and financial sectors (10%).

This brings YT-April FDI to US$3.5bn, up 18.7% YoY. Top sources of equity capital placement were Netherlands (63%), Japan (22%), and USA (6%). These were channeled to financial/insurance (67%), manufacturing (18%), and real estate (7%). The BSP expects FDI inflows to reach US$9.5bn in 2024, up from US$8.9bn in 2023.

Government to hike debt expenses by 25% YoY in 2025 budget. The government reportedly plans to increase the share of debt expenses in the proposed P6.352tn 2025 budget, according to the Philippine Star. This would mean that the allocation for debt expenses will be P876.7bn or 13.8% of 2025 budget. Under the 2024 budget (i.e. GAA), the allocation for debt expenses was P699.2bn, or 12.1% of total GAA. Interest payments under the 2025 budget will amount to P848bn (+26.5% YoY) while net lending will be flat at P28.7bn. Note that principal payments are not included in the calculation of debt expenses.

Budget Department submits 2025 P6.35tn budget proposal to Congress. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) submitted its proposed P6.35tn 2025 budget to the Lower House. The budget is 10% higher than the approved 2024 budget and is equivalent to ~22% of GDP.

Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively. The allocation for debt payments is up 27% YoY, while the local government units is up 17% YoY.

Under its proposal, the government's debt stock is expected to increase by 8.1% to P17.35tn from the projected P16.06tn debt at the end of 2024. The bulk comes from the outstanding domestic debt which is expected to rise by 9.64% to P11.98tn by end-2025 from P10.92tn by end-2024. The outstanding external debt is expected to rise by 4.76% to P5.38tn by end-2025 from P5.13tn by end-2024.

The NG’s debt as a share of GDP stood at 60.2% at the end of the first quarter, while the government aims for a 60.3% debt-to-GDP ratio by year-end. This suggests that the 2025 NG debt will be roughly 60% of GDP. The government aims to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 51% by 2028 (vs. trough of 39.6% in 2019).

Senate ratifies bicameral report on 12% VAT on foreign digital service providers. The Senate adopted the bill imposing the 12% value-added tax (VAT) on digital service providers without a physical presence in the Philippines (e.g. Amazon, Shein, Taobao). The reconciled version of the bill reinforces the Bureau of Internal Revenue’s (BIR) power to “impose and collect VAT on digital services and transactions”.

The Department of Finance (DoF) set withholding tax rates for companies falling under the P3mn VAT threshold under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) law to protect small online businesses. Nonresident digital service providers and electronic marketplaces must register with the BIR for the remittance of VAT on their services. Under the bill,  5% of the revenues from digital service VAT (around P900mn) will be used to support the digital creative industry. The bill is expected to generate as much as PHP18 billion on its first year.

 

Highlights from Pres. Marcos' 3rd SONA. President Marcos delivered his State of the Nation Address (SONA) on July 22 in front of the government’s assembled officialdom, which included the Cabinet and members of both Houses of Congress. 


Among the key priorities Marcos outlined were (1) ongoing agricultural programs to reduce food prices, (2) proposed changes to the power sector law (“EPIRA”), (3) healthcare programs and (4) “continuous’ implementation of infrastructure projects.  The proposed changes to EPIRA could, in our view, pose some risk to some of the power companies, including Meralco (MER PM).

He reserved the latter (last) part for two topics which elicited by far, the biggest reactions, most likely be design. It’s notable that this administration’s policies on these particular issues are directly contradictory to those promoted by the previous government:

  • He reiterated his administration’s stance on the South China Sea (West Philippine Sea), expressing the aim of pursuing a peaceful solution to the competing claims without giving up the country’s rights. The previous government pursued a policy that can arguably be described as appeasement. Frankly, it looks like the new government policy aims will be difficult if not impossible under the current environment.
  • Marcos announced he was banning POGOs, the offshore gaming firms that have mushroomed in the country, and whose operations have dominated recent headlines because of the criminal activities associated with them. The previous government conferred official recognition on POGOs in exchange for a share of revenue of their gaming activities. Marcos’ policy reversal would have significant repercussions on the presence of foreign nationals (mostly from China) and demand for property, particularly in certain locations.


PAGCOR clarifies POGO ban covers IGLs. PAGCOR chair Al Tengco says President Marcos’ ban on online gambling firms called “POGOs” covers internet gaming licensees (IGL). Clarification was being made due to supposed ambiguity of Marcos’ directive during the SONA. The online gaming firms targeting offshore gamblers are now called IGLs. The clarification should remove doubt that all licensed POGOs (IGLs) will be wound down within the year.

Bulacan special ecozone bill lapses into law. Republic Act 11999 (RA 11999) or the Bulacan Special Economic Zone and Freeport bill lapsed into law on June 13. This bill creates the Bulacan EcoZone (BuZ) that covers the airport city project of San Miguel Aerocity (subsidiary of San Miguel Holdings) in Bulacan. The Bulacan Special Economic Zone and Freeport Authority (BEZA) will manage the BuZ. The newly-enacted law is seen to create US$200 billion in exports, according to Rep. Joey Salceda, chairman of the House ways and means committee. The project “will create massive revenue gains in income taxes and property taxes, so the forgone revenue from tax incentives will get offset.” The country’s domestic product could be boosted by no less than P130bn annually, land value gains will be over P226bn through the RA. Recall that President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. had vetoed the measure creating BuZ in July 2022, citing defects such as the low tax base and lack of coherence with existing laws, etc. Salceda filed a new version in the 19th Congress to correct the deficiencies.

Pulse Asia survey shows 76% of Filipinos disapprove of government's performance on inflation. 76% of Filipinos (from 71% in 1Q24 survey) disapproved of how the government handled inflation. Only 5% of respondents gave their approval, while 19% were undecided. The same poll revealed that the government’s attempts at reducing poverty had the second-highest disapproval percentage (51%, from 1Q24’s 42%). Inflation levels are the only concern that majority (57%) of Filipinos want to hear from the President during his SONA on July 22. Inflation levels are also the issue that 72% of Filipinos find most urgent. Other issues are territorial defense (5%) and charter change (1%).

Marcos Jr. approval rating down 2ppt, Sara Duterte up 2ppt. In June, the approval and trust ratings of President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. declined by 2ppts and 5 ppts, respectively. This came from an approval and trust rating of 55% and 57%, respectively, in March 2024. The biggest drop in approval rating was from Luzon at 9ppts, while he received an increase of 14ppts from the National Capital Region. The approval rating also dropped by 13ppts among Class ABC.

Meanwhile, the approval rating of Vice President Sara Duterte went up by 2ppts to 69% in June. This came from an approval rating of 67% in March 2024. Duterte’s approval rating went up by 12ppts and 1ppt in NCR and Visayas, respectively. The approval rating increased by 17ppts in Class E. The survey was conducted from June 17 to 24, 2024 among 2,400 respondents, and has a margin of error of ± 2%.

 

Port regulator sees cargo volume rebound in 2H; eyes 10-13% growth for 2024. The Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) sees cargo volumes rising by 10-13% YoY this year. While January to May 2024 cargo volume growth was flat, PPA expects a rebound by the 2H due to the holiday season. Foreign cargo volumes are currently experiencing delays due to congestion at regional transshipment hubs arising from the conflict at the Red Sea. Recall that container volumes at ICTSI’s (ICT PM) Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) was flat in 1Q24 (664k TEUs). We forecast MICT’s volumes will grow by 5% YoY in 2024 to 2.9mn TEUs and we estimate MICT accounts for 23% of ICT’s consolidated volumes.


LPC: Office vacancy drops slightly in 2Q24; high-end residential prices rise. During its 2Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy fell 1ppt QoQ to 16%. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels.


Nationwide net office take-up rose 47% QoQ (32% YoY) to 270,000sqm. BPO net office take-up increased 58% QoQ (+13% YoY) to 104,000sqm. Net office take-up from traditional tenants (largely government agencies) climbed 74% QoQ (+284% YoY) to 146,000sqm. Meanwhile, POGO net office take-up fell 41% QoQ (down 73% YoY) to 20,000sqm.


LPC also said that secondary market prices of high-end Makati CBD and Fort Bonifacio condominium prices generally rose 5-10% YoY. It estimates that sales of Metro Manila condominiums in the primary market rose 7% QoQ (-16% YoY) to 7,377 units.


GGR of electronic games grows 230% YoY. PAGCOR reported that gross gaming revenue (GGR) in the Philippines grew 9% QoQ (+33% YoY) to P89.4bn. GGR of bingo and electronic games grew 31% QoQ and 231% YoY to P35.7bn, while GGR of integrated resorts was flat QoQ (-4% YoY) at P49.5bn and GGR of PAGCOR operated casinos fell 11% QoQ and 15% YoY to P4.2bn.


New vehicle sales rise 7.6% YoY in June; Toyota sales up 10.5% YoY. CAMPI reported industry-wide car sales rose 7.6% YoY to 39,088 units in June, mildly accelerating from May’s 5.5% YoY pace. On a MoM basis, industry car sales were down 2.9%. This brings 1H24 industry car sales to 226,279 units, up 11.8% YoY. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) cornered 46.1% of the market, selling 104,350 units in 1H24, up 11.5% YoY. For the month of June alone, TMP sold 18,093 units, up 10.5% YoY but down 3.1% MoM. 


Tourist arrivals rise 2% YoY in June; 1H24 arrivals up 12% YoY. Data from the Department of Tourism showed that tourist arrivals for the month of June grew 2% YoY, 3% MoM to 459k, 67% of 2019 average. Korean tourist remained the top source of tourists but saw arrivals dip by 5% YoY in June. Chinese tourists grew 4% YoY, sharply slowing down after rising 94% YoY in 5M24 (likely due to the recent PH imposition of stricter visa requirements and tensions in the West Philippine Sea). This brings 1H24 tourist arrivals to 3.03mn, up 12% YoY or 73% of 2019 average. The government is targeting 7.7mn tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY or 93% of 2019 levels. In 2023, tourist arrivals reached 5.5mn or 66% of pre-Covid level.

Banks’ NPL ratio soars to near two-year high. The banking industry’s nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio rose to 3.57% in May from 3.45% in April and 3.46% in 5M23, matching 7M22 ratio of 3.57%. May’s ratio is also the highest in 23 months (3.6% in 6M22). NPLs rose to P481bn +13/7% YoY / +3.1% MoM in May, while loan portfolio +9.3% / -0.3% MoM as of end-May to P13.9tn.

Past due loans dropped 1.6% MoM in May (from P618.05bn to P608.07bn). It YoY rise was 15.7% (from P525.51bn). Past due ratio in May was 4.37%, lower than 4.43% in April but higher than the 4.17% ratio in 5M23. Restructured loans stood at P295.89bn, up 1.9% MoM from P290.37bn but down 4.6% YoY from P310.3bn. Higher than April’s 2.08%, but lower than 2.46% 5M23. These borrowings accounted for 2.13% of the industry’s total loan portfolio.

Banks’ loan reserves inched up by 0.7% MoM in May (from P471.35bn to P474.88bn). Its YoY rise was 6.9% (from P444.03bn). Loan loss reserve ratio was up at 3.42% from April’s 3.37%, but lower than the 5M23 3.52%. Lenders’ NPL coverage ratio slipped to 95.81% from 98.07% in April, and 101.81% in 5M23.

Commercial banks' loan growth accelerates to 10.1% YoY in May; M3 growth accelerates to 6.5%. Preliminary data showed that commercial bank loans grew by 10.1% YoY in May, accelerating from 9.6% YoY in April. Loans for production activities grew by 8.4% in May from 7.8% in April due to faster growth in loans for manufacturing (+10%), retail (+11%), transport (27%), and real estate (+13%). Meanwhile consumer loan grew at a steady pace of 25.6% YoY, with credit card loans up 29% YoY and auto loans up 21% YoY. Domestic liquidity (M3) growth accelerated to 6.5% YoY in May from 5.6% YoY in April and was the fastest growth rate since November 2023’s 7.0%.

Marcos OKs new laws to enhance procurement, fight online scams.  President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed into law the New Government Procurement Act (NGPA) and the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA). The NGPA law updates the Government Procurement Reform Act (RA No. 1984), streamlining the procurement process from 3 months to just 60 days by standardizing procurement forms and institutionalizing electronic procurement. The NGPA features 11 new procurement modalities "that will afford greater flexibility for government agencies in choosing the manner of acquiring the best goods and services.” The NGPA also ensures best prices for Filipinos through the “Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid.”

The AFASA aims to combat the rising tide of online financial scams. The law mandates financial institutions to implement stronger safeguards to protect Filipino financial accounts. The AFASA also criminalizes money muling and social engineering schemes, and grants the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) authority to probe financial accounts involved in illegal activities.

Data from the Philippine National Police recorded 19,000 cybercrimes in 2023 (68.98% increase YoY). Majority of these cases were online scams, totaling 14,030 incidents (an increase of 7,208 cases or 94.64% YoY).
