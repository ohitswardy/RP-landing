REGIS REPORT

31 October 2024
 

The PSEi posted its first monthly decline in four months, -1.78% MoM to 7,142.96, as foreign inflows into Philippine shares substantially eased to just +US$21m in October, compared to the massive +$US346m windfall of the prior month.

The benchmark initially hit a fresh 4-year high of 7,554.68 earlier in October as sentiment was supported by a dovish CPI reading of 1.9% in September, which was not only substantially lower than the 2.5% Street forecast and the lowest reading in 4 years but was also below the BSP’s 2%-4% target range. This eventually set up the BSP to cut the benchmark rate for the second time in a row down to 6% during their mid-October policy meeting, as widely expected. There will be one more policy meeting remains before the year ends (December 19) and policymakers are again expected to trim the benchmark rate by another 25bps.

Despite the easing in monetary policy however, the 7500-handle however proved to be the key resistance level as shares drifted lower from there through the rest of the month. One potential factor behind the evident profit taking from the local market’s ~17% run over the past ~3 months is the lingering uncertainty over the impending US elections. 

Meanwhile, the broadly mixed start to the 3Q/9M earnings season so far also failed to help sentiment on the ground. Early results so far showed that Banks continued to report healthy growth and record profits, as expected, but are starting to see NIMs contraction for the likes of BDO and MBT. Meanwhile, the few consumer names that have reported so far (RRHI and WLCON) showed persistent weakness in their 3Q numbers, signaling potentially another downbeat quarter for the sector.

The PSEi notably broke below the 50-day moving average on the final trading session of October. Note that before these past few sessions, the technical level had last been touched when the index was still at the 6500 handle back in early August. As of end-October, the benchmark’s YTD gain now stands at +10.74%, down from its early October peak of +17.13%.

Along with the weakness in shares came an even sharper downturn in the PHP which similarly tumbled for the first time in 4 months, -3.7% MoM to 58.115, as investors flocked to the dollar amid signs of strength in the US economy over the past few weeks (before the massive payrolls miss that came out during the weekend) and an apparent flocking into safe havens ahead of the US elections.

Key data
 

Turnover was slower this month vs September, and October’s ADV only came in at  US$97m vs US$133m the month prior. Volumes practically thinned out during the latter half of the month as traders thread the market lightly, in search for a new catalyst with 3Q results just starting to pile in. The market meanwhile still finished the month at net foreign bought for US$22m despite the heavy outflows during the last week of the month, a week before the US elections. Foreign-local split pretty much even at 50/50.

  • The market finished the month net foreign bought despite the barrage of selling by foreigners during the last week of October. Market proxy AC was the top foreign bought stock, registering US$24.5m worth of net inflows, while SM fell out of the top 10 spot after last month’s strong foreign demand.

  • Banks were the main focus in October as the big 3 banks reported their 3Q earnings. Foreign appetite in the sector though was mixed, BPI saw US$15m worth of net inflows after 9M24 NPAT jumped 24% to P48b beating estimates while BDO saw outflows (-US$8.2m) after 9M24  just came out slightly ahead of consensus.
  • Property meanwhile saw net foreign selling on rising office vacancy and high condominium supply.  ALI is  the top foreign sold name  this month for US$24.7m while peer SMPH saw outflow of  US$3.6m.  Profit takers too weighed on ALI this month, sending its share price -10.7%. MER too reported good 3Q results and was taken positively by foreign investors. The stock saw US$9.3m worth of inflows, briefly sending the stocks share price to a new record highs.

  • Consumer names continued to be mixed despite inflation slowing down. Foreigners remained buyers in PGOLD, for the 5th straight month, while JFC was net foreign bought for 3 consecutive months. WLCON meanwhile fell -12% after 9M24 net profit fell 22%.

  • Other notable inflows this month were in: GLO +US$10m (9th straight months of inflow)  and MAC +US$2.3m, surging +22.4% MoM.

 

 
 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS

Breadth of the market was skewed to the negative this week with 23 index stocks in the red vs 7 in the green. Faring the worst in October was ALI -12.8% MoM, while its peer SMPH -7.82% posted a substantial decline as well. Note that ALI is set to hold its 3Q/9M results briefing this Wednesday (and SMPH press on November 11) but the recent Colliers report has already stoked concerns over the incoming results of developers at large. According to the property consultant’s latest data, office take-ups in 3Q turned negative at -33k sqm as POGOs vacated -57k sqm of space ahead of the expected ban on POGOs by year-end. Meanwhile on the residential side, unsold inventory grew to 70 months’ of sales in 3Q from 60 in 2Q.

Meanwhile, lenders were mixed notwithstanding their generally strong results, with BPI +3.39% MoM outperforming peers BDO -2.86% and MBT -5.3%. Looking at their 3Q/9M numbers, while all 3 names continued to show strong earnings growth, record profits, and healthy mid-teens loan growth, there were some noteworthy nuances: BPI saw an improvement in NIMs, supported by high-yielding consumer loans, while the BDO and MBT (*but muddled by swaps) both saw declines; and BDO achieved record results without having to book significant trading gains yet, while BPI recorded trading gains of over P2b for 3Q and MBT an even larger P6b.

Consumer sector was another mixed space amid some tepid results by early reporters RRHI and WLCON. RRHI -2.99% MoM announced that its 9M core income +8% YoY to P4.1b, below forecast, while 3Q core income alone was practically unchanged at P1.4b as group-wide SSSG slightly slowed to +0.5% YoY vs +0.7% in 1H, with several formats posting negative growth (department store -3.4%, DIY -5.8% and specialty -8.6%). Meanwhile WLCON -11.05% was among the biggest laggards in the benchmark in October after reporting that its 9M net profit declined by -22% YoY to P2.1b, behind both Regis and Street forecast. For 3Q alone, earnings fell by -33% YoY and -21% QoQ, dragged by weakness in both revenues and margins. SSS contracted by -8.5% YoY in 3Q, worse than -2% in 2Q; while GPM fell by -80bps YoY and -90bps QoQ. Prospects for the company moving forward remain tempered amid the ongoing oversupply of condominiums in Metro Manila and as new building permits remain low. With all that being said, there were a handful of consumer names that were among the few gainers in the benchmark in October with CNPF +9.8%, MONDE +6.65%, and PGOLD +3.33% ahead of their respective results announcements due over the next weeks (MONDE briefing this Thursday).

Telcos were broadly weaker with GLO -10.58%, TEL -6.23% and CNVRG -3.59%. Note however that GLO has started to see some signs of bargain hunting over the past couple of sessions after hitting oversold levels for the first time in almost a year and touching its lowest level in nearly 3 months of 2100/share (intraday), ultimately capping October at 2146. GLO results briefing is on Monday, November 11.

On the opposite end, leading the few index winners in October was MER +10.61% after the company recorded a +17% YoY growth in 9M core income (and a derived +10% for 3Q core alone), ahead of forecasts. An even more important matter brought about by management however was the regulatory decision on the current regulatory period (“5RP”). Although yet to be officially announced, the ERC apparently voted to treat 5RP as a lapsed period and thus fix the tariff to what it was for 4RP (=P1.35/kwh), which is in favor of Meralco. If this decision is indeed effected, Regis estimates an annualized positive impact to MER’s post-tax profit of ~P6b. For context the company is set to make over P40b this year.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

September CPI surprises to the downside, slows sharply to 1.9%. September CPI inflation came in at 1.9%, coming in lower-than-expected. Not only was inflation below Street guestimates (2.5% average), it also came in below the Bangko Sentral’s (BSP) target inflation band of 2 to 4%.

Food inflation (YoY) continued to retreat and was only 1.4% for the month (vs. 3.9% in Aug).  Rice was down only very slightly MoM but the YoY rice inflation was down principally due to base effects.

Monetary Board delivers widely expected policy rate cut of 25bps. The BSP’s Monetary Board reduced its polity rate by 25bps to 6.0%, in line with consensus expectations. BSP reduced its risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2024 to 3.1% (from 3.3% previously). It mildly raised its risk-adjusted inflation forecasts for 2025 and 2026 to 3.3% and 3.7%, citing potentially higher electricity prices and impact of higher minimum wages.

Given the better-than-expected inflation forecast for the year, Governor Remolona said another 25bps reduction during its last meeting in December is possible. Furthermore, Governor Remolona did not rule out a 100bps easing for 2025 but admitted that such magnitude is on the dovish side. Regis expects the BSP to cut its policy rate by another 25bps in December and 75bps in 2025. Jefferies expects the FED to reduce policy rates by 25bps in November and 25bps in December. For 2025, JEFF expects the Fed to cut rates by another 100bps.

GIR climbs to new record of US$112bn in end-September. Preliminary data showed that the country’s GIR rose by US$4.1bn (+3.8%) MoM to US$112bn at the end of September. Prior to this, the GIR peaked at US$108.8bn in Dec 2021. Cited the rise in the national government’s foreign currency deposits, which included proceeds from the issuance of US$2.5bn global bonds in August. Increase was also due to upward valuation adjustments in gold holdings and higher net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. BSP also gained US$1.2bn MoM from FX trades. The GIR is sufficient to cover 8.1 months’ worth of imports.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.4bn in August. Preliminary data showed that the country’s trade deficit widened by 7% YoY to US$4.38bn. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit in August was lower than the US$4.9bn deficit in July. Exports were flat YoY at US$6.75bn, as electronic exports fell 8% and offset the 12% improvement in non-electronic exports. Imports rose 3% YoY to US$11.1bn due to 10% YoY increase in capital goods imports and 5% higher raw material imports. Consumer goods imports were steady at US$2.24bn while fuel imports fell by 9%. Electronic imports rebounded, rising by 9% YoY and could lead to a rebound in exports in succeeding periods. This brings YT-August trade deficit to US$34.3bn, down 4% YoY.

Remittances rise 3.2% YoY in August. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 3.2% YoY to US$2.89bn in August, mildly accelerating from 3.1% rate in July. In PHP terms, remittances rose by 5% YoY, moderating from the 6.5% pace in YT-July due to PHP appreciation. The change in school calendar (start of school year varied between July/August this year vs Aug/Sep last year) could have still distorted the growth rate. This brings YT-August remittances up by 2.9% YoY to US$22.2bn. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% YoY in 2024 (to ~US$34bn).

BOP posts US$3.5bn surplus in September. The country’s overall balance of payments posted a surplus of US$3.5bn in September, which was a reversal of the US$414m deficit in Sep 2023 and higher than the US$88m surplus recorded in August 2024, primarily due to the US$2.5bn global bond issuance. This brings YT-September BOP surplus to US$5.1bn, higher than the US$1.7bn surplus in the same period last year. The BSP is targeting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn this year, lower than the 2023 BOP surplus of US$3.7bn.

 

Budget deficit widens to P273bn in September. The government ran a budget deficit of P273bn in September, 8.9% YoY wider, due to a rebound in expenditures. Revenues grew 17.3% YoY, with tax collections up 8.5% and non-tax revenues doubling. BIR collections rose 15% due to the higher personal income taxes from government workers’ salary hike and higher collections from documentary stamp tax. BOC collections fell 3%, which was attributed to the double-digit negative growth in import duties partly due to tariff reduction of some commodities, as well as smuggling. Non-tax revenues doubled to P49bn due to the upfront fee from the new concessionaire of NAIA. Expenditures rose 13% YoY, as primary spending rose 15% YoY. Interest expenses rose by just 3% YoY. This brings YT-September deficit to P970bn, down by 1.3% YoY. The government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

BSP Governor eyes lowering RRR to zero within term; BSP removing fees on small transfers. BSP Governor Remolona, Jr. hinted at the possibility of lowering bank’ reserve requirement ratio down to zero before his terms ends in 2029. Discussions are ongoing to bring the RRR down further to 5%, but timing is still undetermined. The BSP is reducing the RRR by 250bps to 7% for commercial banks by October 25, which could raise NPAT by c. 4%.

In a draft circular, the BSP is eyeing to amend the Manual of Regulations for Payment Systems (MORPS) to eliminate transaction fees on electronic fund transfers for personal transactions, up to a specified threshold on the number of transactions, and on payments for micro-merchants. Micro-merchants are described to have monthly aggregate gross receipts that do not exceed P250k (c. US$4.5k). The draft circular also proposes to lift the moratorium on the increase of fee for InstaPay and PESONet. If the circular is approved, payment service providers (PSPs) will need to comply with the amended rules by April 1, 2025. Data from the BSP showed that the value of transactions done through InstaPay and PESONet climbed by 34.6% YoY to P9.45tn in 7M24.

Government debt rises 2% MoM to P15.89tn in end-September. The national government’s outstanding debt rose to P15.89tn in end-September, 2.2% (+P343bn) higher than the end-Aug level of P15.55tn due to net issuances of domestic and external debt. Domestic debt rose by 1.3% (+P145bn) MoM to P10.94tn due to net issuance of new government securities. External debt rose by 4.2% (+198bn) MoM to P4.96tn partly due to the issuance of US$2.5bn global bonds. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end and is aiming to reduce the ratio further to 51% by 2028. As of end-June 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 60.9%.

Government infrastructure spending drops in August. Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 12.9% YoY to P989.4bn in 8M24. Growth moderated sharply from the 17% pace in 7M24. This implies infrastructure spending for August alone fell by 8% YoY to P125bn. Officials cited project delays of major infrastructure projects, timing issues on encashment of payments to contractors, and adverse weather conditions.

To reach the government’s FY24E target of P1.47tn (+4% YoY), spending for the rest of the year could fall by 11% YoY. For 2025, the government plans to spend P1.53tn, up 4% YoY and equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. The government aims to spend 5-6% of GDP annually for infrastructure through 2028.

Government’s infrastructure budget for 2025 down by 0.3% YoY. Preliminary data showed that the country’s GIR rose by US$4.1bn (+3.8%) MoM to US$112bn at the end of September. Prior to this, the GIR peaked at US$108.8bn in Dec 2021. Cited the rise in the national government’s foreign currency deposits, which included proceeds from the issuance of US$2.5bn global bonds in August. Increase was also due to upward valuation adjustments in gold holdings and higher net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. BSP also gained US$1.2bn MoM from FX trades. The GIR is sufficient to cover 8.1 months’ worth of imports.

 

President Marcos signs law imposing 12% VAT on digital services. President Marcos, Jr. signed into law Republic Act 12023, which imposes a 12% VAT on foreign digital service providers and a 5% VAT on registered foreign players providing services to the government. Law covers online search engines, online marketplaces, cloud services, online media and advertising, online platforms, and any digital content providers. The law exempts digital services sold to educational institutions and requires nonresident digital service providers to register for VAT payments if their gross sales or receipts for the past year have exceeded P3m. The government expects to collect P105bn in additional revenues over the next five years from this measure. Senate President Francis G. Escudero said there are currently no pending revenue-generating bills except the measure imposing an excise tax on single-use plastic.

Commercial banks’ loan growth accelerates to 10.7% in August. Loans of universal/commercial banks grew 10.7% YoY in August, slightly faster than the 10.4% pace in July. Due to faster growth in manufacturing, trade, utilities, and real estate. Consumer loan growth moderated to 23.7% from 24.3%. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3) growth slowed to 5.5% in August from 7.3% in July.

Banks’ bad loan ratio flat at 3.68% in August. Preliminary data showed that the NPL ratio of Philippine banks (net of interbank) was steady at 3.68% in August. NPLs (net of interbank) rose to P512bn in August, up 15.8% YoY and 0.9% MoM. Total loans (net of interbank) rose by 10.3% YoY, 0.8% MoM to P13.91tn in August (+10.3% pace in July). NPL coverage ratio was at 94.11% in August from 94.32% in July. Past due ratio climbed to 4.42% in August from 4.40% in July. Past due loans rose by 0.9% MoM to P531bn. Restructured loan ratio was steady at 2.05%, with restructured loans up 0.7% MoM to P293bn.

Growth in vehicle sales moderates to 2.4% YoY in September. Vehicle sales rose by 2.4% YoY, 1.0% MoM to 39,542 units in September, according to CAMPI/TMA. CAMPI attributed the slowdown to absence of new vehicle models, as auto companies are likely preparing for the upcoming Philippine International Motor Show in October. This brings 9M24 vehicle sales up 9.4% YoY to 344,307 units.

67% of BPO firms implement AI initiatives. 67% of IT-BPO firms survey by the IT-BPO Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) said that they are using AI in their operations, including customer support, data entry and process, workforce management, and sales and marketing. 8% have reported declines in their workforce due to the use of AI, while 13% have reported gains. IT-BPM industry is expected to end the year with US$38bn in revenues and a workforce count of 1.82m, representing 7% growth from 2023. IBPAP President Jack Madrid expects growth to slow down in 2025. BPO firms occupy 50%-80% of the office portfolios of the developers Regis covers. 

Metro Manila condominium supply is equivalent to 29 months of sales. Leechiu Property Consultants said that there are currently 67,600 condominium units available for sale in Metro Manila. This is equivalent to 29 months’ worth of sales. LPC said that the unsold inventory level is typically closer to 12 months’ worth of sales. Several developers have experienced weakness in Metro Manila condominium sales and have reduced launch plans. The major developers have (nationwide) unsold inventory levels equivalent to 18 to 24 months of reservation sales.

 

LPC: Office take-up declines in 3Q24.During its 3Q24 briefing, Leechiu Property Consultants (LPC) revealed that Philippine office vacancy was flat QoQ (at 17%), while Metro Manila office vacancy was also flat QoQ (at 16%). Regis estimate that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels. Nationwide net office take-up fell 75% QoQ (down 47% YoY) to 68,000sqm - BPO net office take-up fell 51% QoQ and YoY to 51,000sqm, take-up from traditional tenants (including government agencies) declined 52% QoQ (up 400% YoY) to 70,000sqm, while POGO demand reversed from net take-up of 20,000sqm in 2Q24 to net closures of 53,000sqm.

Regional wage boards in three regions hike minimum wages. Regional wage boards have approved increases in minimum wages across three regions: Region 2 increased wages by 7% to P460-480, Region 3 granted ~10-13% hike in minimum wages to be implemented in two tranches: Oct 17, 2024 and April 16, 2025, while Region 12 hiked minimum wages by ~7% to be implemented in four tranches: Oct 17, Jan 1, April 1, June 1.

Ilocos Region wage board raises minimum wages by 8%. The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) of the Ilocos Region granted a P33 (~8%) daily minimum wage hike across all sectors. Minimum wages for non-agri sector was raised to P468 daily from P435 previously. For the agri sector, the daily minimum wage was raised to P435 from P402. New wages will take effect on November 7. To date, the government has raised minimum wages in seven (7) regions out of 17 regions; Increases have ranged between 7-8%. This should help boost consumption, particularly for the low-end. Regis’ top picks are: URC, MONDE, CNPF, PGOLD.

Colliers: net office take-up turns negative in 3Q24, unsold residential inventory level rises. During its 3Q24 briefing, Colliers revealed that Metro Manila 3Q24 net office take-up turned negative for the first time since 4Q21. Net office take-up was -33,000sqm, from +98,000sqm in 2Q24 (+17,000sqm in 3Q23). 57,000sqm of office space vacated by POGOs. As a result, Metro Manila office vacancy rose 0.2ppt to 18.5%.Due to the POGO ban, Colliers expects Metro Manila office vacancy to rise to 20.5% by end-2024. POGO firms still occupy c. 400,000sqm of office space in Metro Manila. Rental rates in most Metro Manila business districts fell c. 1% QoQ. Regis estimates that rental rates in most parts of Metro Manila are still 10%-50% below pre-COVID levels.

Meanwhile, Colliers estimates that the level of unsold residential inventory represents c. 70 months’ worth of presales (from c. 60 months as of 2Q24). This appears to be due to an increase in unsold inventory level and a reduction in residential take-up. 3Q24 residential take-up fell c. 20% YoY to 3,100 units (flat QoQ).

PAGCOR 3Q24 revenue from e-games falls 31% QoQ. PAGCOR reported that its revenue from e-games (such as e-Bingo and e-casino) fell 31% QoQ to P7.56bn (in 3Q24). This could imply weakness in the GGR of DigiPlus (PLUS PM), which had a 50% market share as of 2Q24. Meanwhile, PAGCOR revenue from licensed casinos grew 5% QoQ to P8.44bn. Since GGR of Okada Manila fell 8% QoQ in 3Q24, the GGR of other licensed casinos should show stronger improvement. Solaire North likely showed stronger GGR in 3Q24, since it only contributed for one month in 2Q24.
