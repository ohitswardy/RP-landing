REGIS REPORT

01 July 2024
 

The PSEi ended on a flattish note at 6,411.91, -0.33% MoM, after a volatile June, as shares managed to find support at the tail-end of the month after breaking crucial support levels and plummeting to its lowest level since November. The benchmark ultimately capped 2Q with a sharp -7.12% QoQ slump, reversing all its gains in 1Q, and ending the first half of the year with a slight -0.59% drop YT-June.

Shares climbed at the outset with the benchmark rising and fluctuating around the psychological 6,500-levels in the first few days of the month, supported a below-estimate CPI reading in May (3.9% vs 4% forecast) and the government’s initial announcement of its plan to cut rice tariffs, which bolstered the hopes for a dovish policy outlook ahead. Shares were stymied from there, however, as signs of strength in US economy, the Fed’s indication of only a single rate cut this year, and the weakness in PHP put the dovish policy outlook at home into question. Market activity on the ground also notably deteriorated with four sessions during the month being among the five lightest so far this year (turnover including blocks). At its lowest point, the benchmark tumbled to as low as 6,158.48 during the FTSE rebalancing on June 21, as the downbeat sentiment and the lack of bid-side liquidity on the board resulted to an outsize move of -2.93% on the day, the worst one-day drop since December 2022, notwithstanding the fairly modest outflow expectations (under $10m) leading into the event. At this level, the benchmark was down by as much as -4.14% nominally and -10.11% in USD terms YTD.

Shares then managed to find support from there with the benchmark gaining in all the remaining sessions, clawing back above the 6,400 line and trimming the monthly slide to just -0.33% by the end, helped by a dovish indication from BSP Governor Remolona that the Monetary Board may start cutting rates by 25bps in 3Q and cut a total of 50bps for the year, notwithstanding the Fed’s direction. Furthermore, the BSP reduced its inflation forecast for the year to 3.3% from 3.5% previously. Note that YTD-May inflation currently stands at 3.5%, which implies that policymakers see a meaningful slowdown in inflation for the latter half of the year. Our own view is that inflation may even slow to the 2% level starting August/September due to the imminent easing in rice prices amid the official enactment of the rice tariff cut from 35% to 15%. Furthermore, another source of optimism leading into 2H is that the PHP held up and even slightly strengthened vs the USD after the BSP’s dovish pronouncements, which could further solidify the odds of a rate cut at home starting August.

 
PSEi -0.3% MoM | 6411.91
 
Key data
 

It was a volatile month for the PSEi as investors struggled to find conviction given the lack of market catalysts.  Trading was dry for the most part, evidenced by the sharp decline in turnover, with June’s average only at US$82m/day vs May’s US$130m. The absence of liquidity also resulted to some sharp moves especially during the FTSE rebalancing when the index fell almost -3% (the largest single day drop since March 2021 ) despite the estimated outflow of only US$6m. Nevertheless, the index was quick to recover losses towards month-end as bargain hunters stepped in and helped push the index back to 6400 levels. Foreigners continued to be net sellers, bringing YTD net foreign outflow to US$529m. Foreign participation improved slightly to 48% vs 46% in May.

 

 

Index proxies SM and AC both saw the biggest outflows in June as overall sentiment towards the market remained weak, dampened further by the ongoing tension in the West Philippine sea. SM finished the month lower -4.5% MoM after its underperformance in May  due to a decrease in its MSCi weighting. AC too fell -2.2% MoM and printed a new 52-week low, trading at key support levels.  Foreigners were also largely sellers in AEV but the selling was met by the company’s buyback program as the family continues to accumulate the name after it got deleted from the MSCi sending its share price higher +10.7%.  Outside conglos, consistent foreign selling was also seen in property names,  as the timing of rate cuts remains uncertain. However, both SMPH +5.0% and ALI +8.6% managed end the month with gains as local bargain hunting ensued.

  • ICT continued to see foreign demand (+US$15.7m), and is now net foreign bought for the 5th straight month. The stock saw some volatility but ultimately finished the month at highs with the help of foreign buying. Foreigners also bought into GLO, sending its share price to a new 52-week high with its mobile revenue improving and Mynt contributions ramping up. Banks meanwhile finished the month two-way  as foreign investors sold  BDO (-1.4% MoM) and buying into MBT (+8.9% MoM).
  • Consumer were likewise mixed. PGOLD and URC started to see some foreign interest given the weakness in their share price.  Foreigners were sellers of WLCON and JFC.

 

 

 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

While the PSEi’s performance during the month was flattish (-0.33% MoM), its breadth was skewed to the negative with 19 laggards outnumbering only 11 winners.

Best of the winners was AEV, +10.7% MoM, bouncing from its sharp sell-off in May and a near 3-year low after its deletion in the MSCI standard cap index, which took effect at the close of end-May. Note that the company was also actively buying back shares throughout the month, netting ~62m shares bought this June. That said, the broader Holding Company sector was a big laggard this month, -3.5% MoM, dragged by an otherwise across the board weakness among its members: JGS, -15.97%, LTG, -6.97%, AGI, -6.58%, DMC, -6.39%, SM, -4.54%, SMC, -2.43% and AC, -2.18%. There is some sense that, at least for some names, investors could be preferring to own the subs instead of the parents, like in the case of big laggard JGS -15.97%, the 2nd most battered stock in the index this June, where its sub URC outperformed the market with a +3.93% MoM rise.

On the opposite end, bright spot this month was the Property sector +4.77% MoM, supported by gains in large caps ALI +8.57% and SMPH +5.01% amid optimism for lower interest rates, particularly after the BSP’s dovish signal in the latter part of the month. We also highlight SMPH’s Pasay coastal development project which was the topic of the call we hosted for the company last week. Comparing the management’s latest numbers to ours, Regis’ key takeaway is that the project may be even more lucrative than we expected. On the cost side, SMPH looks to spend Php40k-Php45k per sqm, which is lower than our own estimate of Php50k per sqm, but more importantly on the revenue side, management noted that selling 44 hectares will be sufficient to fully fund the entire project. This implies a selling price of Php325k-Php370k per sqm which is much higher than our own assumption of Php250k per sqm.

Another relevant topic of discussion relating to the sector was the enactment of Property Valuation Reform Law, which is expected to mostly increase real property taxes. In our view however, the impact of this on developers should be minimal as property taxes accounted for only 2%-7% of the 2023 pre-tax profit of companies under our coverage. Furthermore, note that the increase in property taxes will be capped at 6% on the first year of implementation, and hence we estimate that the profit hit should be less than 0.5% on that first year. For the succeeding years, we expect the reduction in profits to be less than 5%.

Outside of those main property plays, REITs also helped prop up the sector: RCR +5% MoM, FILRT +4.95% and AREIT +3.92% as investors look to be locking in their 6.5%-7.5% yields at current levels with imminent rate cuts in mind.

Meanwhile the Banking sector finished virtually unchanged, -0.01% MoM, supported primarily by MBT +8.86% which was the 2nd best performing stock in the benchmark this June. Its peers BDO -1.38% and BPI -0.92% posted moderate losses, but the standout laggard in the sector this month was non-PSEi SECB which plummeted -14.65% to its lowest level since mid-2011. Note that at current levels, SECB now only trades at 0.33x P/B.

Consumer sector was mixed this month with JFC +4.44% MoM and URC +3.93% outperforming their peers MONDE -16.07%, the worst performing component of the index this June, CNPF -14.27% and PGOLD -3.97%. Outside the PSEi however, the standout consumer stock this June was SEVN which surged by another +15.53% MoM, adding to its +31% surge in May. Recall that we initiated a BUY recommendation on the stock back in April with a target price of Php135/share, which implied a 65% upside then. SEVN shares hit a new 52-week high of Php132 and settled the month at Php131.7.

The telco space was also mixed this month with TEL -2.91% MoM trailing behind GLO +6.6% and CNVRG +6.88%, which both marked new 52-week highs and were among the 3 best performing stocks in the benchmark this month. We continue to harp about GLO as we deem that Mynt’s (GCash) strong profits justify a re-evaluation. We estimate that GLO’s 38% owned Mynt produced ~$46m in profits for 1Q24, 1.4x the prior year. On that pace, Mynt could make close to $200m in profit for 2024, and as such a $5b valuation is, in our view, justifiable if not conservative. On top of that, GLO’s dividend yield looks attractive as well at nearly 5%. We have a BUY rating on GLO with a street-high target price of Php2750, which represents 30% upside from current levels.

Lastly, Mining was the most battered sector this month, -8.22% MoM, dragged by an across-the-board selloff in pure-play miners FNI -16.33%, PX -11.3%, NIKL -8.4% and APX -4.9%, amid the softening in metal prices this June. That said, mixed power and mining play (and officially under Industrial, not Mining index) SCC largely resisted the selloff and ended the month -0.59%.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation accelerates to 3.9% in May. Headline CPI rose 3.9% YoY in May, slightly faster than the 3.8% print in April. This was however a tad lower than consensus forecast of 4% and came in at the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.7%-4.5%. The mild uptick was due to faster transport inflation (+3.5% YoY in May vs. +2.6% in April) and a milder deflation in electricity CPI (-8.5% YoY in May vs -11% in April). Food inflation slowed to 5.8% from 6.0% as rice CPI dipped 0.2% MoM - the first MoM decrease in seven months, resulting in moderating rice inflation to +23.0% in May from 23.9% in April. Core inflation slowed further to 3.1% in May from 3.2% in April, with non-food “core” CPI moderating to 3.3% from 3.2%.

Headline inflation averaged 3.5% in 5M24, trending lower than the BSP’s risk-adjusted inflation forecast for 2024 is 3.8%. The BSP said the risks to the inflation outlook continue to lean toward the upside in the near-term due to the possible impact of adverse weather conditions on farm output. Nonetheless, the BSP expects full-year average inflation to settle within the target range of 2-4% for 2024 and 2025. Next BSP policy meeting is scheduled on June 27.

BSP maintains policy rate; signals rate cut in Aug. The BSP kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.5% at its June policy meeting. The central bank also downgraded its inflation outlook citing the imminent reduction in rice tariffs and its second-round effects. It is now looking at a baseline inflation rate of 3.3% (from 3.5%) for 2024 and 3.1% (from 3.3%) for 2025, and a risk-adjusted inflation rate of 3.1% for 2024 (vs 3.8% previously) and 3.1% for 2025 (vs. 3.7% previously). Note that YT-May average inflation rate is at 3.5%, with inflation for May alone at 3.9%

BSP Governor Remolona said they are now “less hawkish” and that the Monetary Board is “on track” to cut rates when it meets on August 15. The BSP could cut rates by 25bps in 3Q and another 25bps in 4Q. The central bank is “occasionally” intervening in the FX market, as it does not want the PHP to depreciate “too sharply.” Mr. Remolona thinks the peso’s effect on inflation is “not very large”, citing the estimated “pass-through” of only 0.36% per 1% depreciation of the peso.

Import tariff on rice lowered. President Marcos signed EO 62, which, among other things, reduces the import duty on rice from 35% to 15% effective from early July until end-2028. In a concession to farmers’ groups and officials of the Department of Agriculture, the EO provides for a review of the tariff every 4 months. Lower rice prices could bring headline inflation to under 2% by Aug/Sept – all else held equal – and give the BSP elbow room to start policy rate cuts from 6.5%. 

April trade deficit mildly lower YoY at US$4.76bn. The country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.76bn in April, slightly (-1%) down YoY but was much larger than the US$3.44bn deficit recorded in March. We suspect the sequential rebound in the trade deficit figure was largely due to timing of Holy Week. Note that while most ports are generally open year-round, customs offices are often shut during the holidays. Imports grew 13% YoY and 15% MoM to US$11bn - most major type of goods accelerated: raw materials (+14%), capital goods (+10%), consumer goods (+16%), and fuel (+8%). Electronic imports also rose by 9% YoY. Meanwhile, exports also grew by 26% YoY and 1.4% MoM to US$6.2bn. This brings YT-April trade deficit to US$16.3bn, lower by 16% YoY.

Current account deficit falls 61% YoY in 1Q24; BOP posts US$238mn surplus. The BSP reported that the country’s current account deficit fell 60.6% YoY to US$1.7bn in 1Q24, equivalent to 1.6% of GDP. Officials cited 15% YoY reduction in trade in goods deficit to US$14.7bn, which resulted from higher volume of exports and lower prices of imports. BPO export revenues amounted to US$7.1bn in 1Q24, up 2.1% YoY. This brings 1Q24 Balance of Payments (BOP) to a US$238mn surplus, lower than the US$3.5bn surplus in 1Q23. The BSP raised its 24E BOP surplus forecast to US$1.6bn from US$700mn previously due to a lower current account deficit forecast of US$4.7bn from US$6.1bn previously.

BOP reverses to US$2 billion surplus. The Philippine’s BOP position shifted to a surplus of US$2bn in May, reversing the US$439mn deficit recorded in May 2023. This marks a turnaround from the US$639mn shortfall in April and is the biggest surplus since January 2023 (US$3.08bn), reflecting inflows mainly from the national government’s net foreign currency deposits with BSP. This includes proceeds from ROP Global Bonds and net income from BSP’s foreign investments.

The cumulative BOP position has now reverted to a surplus of US$1.6bn. However, it is 44.3% lower than the surplus in the same period last year. Personal remittances rose to US$12.01bn from January to April (2.8% increase), while cash remittances increased by 2.8%. The BOP position reflects increase in GIR level to US$105bn, which is 6.1 times the country’s short term external debt and 3.8 times based on residual maturity. The foreign exchange buffer represents an external liquidity equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. Note that the BSP raised its BOP surplus forecast to US$1.6bn for this year.

 

VP Sara Duterte resigns from Cabinet post. Vice President Sara Duterte resigned from her post as Secretary of the Department of Education and as vice chairperson of the National Task Force to End Local Communist Armed Conflict (NTF-ELCAC, effective July 19.

Remittances rise 3.1% YoY in April. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose 3.1% YoY to US$2.56bn in April. In PHP terms, remittances grew 6%, fastest growth rate since May 2023. Land-based remittances rose 3.2%, driven by Singapore (+5.7%), Saudi Arabia (+6.8%), Qatar (+5.9% ), and USA (+2.8%). Meanwhile, sea-based remittances rose 2.8% YoY. This brings YT-April remittances up 2.8% to US$10.78bn. BSP sees remittances growing by 3% this year, suggesting remittances could reach US$34.5bn. To reach this, remittances for the rest of the year should average US$2.96bn per month.

Palace proposes P6.352 trillion 2025 budget. Marcos administration is proposing another record budget of P6.352tn for 2025, up 10.1% from the 2024 budget of P5.768tn. 2025 proposed budget is equivalent to 22% of GDP. The budget will largely focus on social sector (education, health, social protection) and agriculture. This also builds in a higher budget allocation for LGUs, which will be higher by 18.7% YoY to P1.03tn due to increased tax collections in 2022. There will also be an expansion in the conditional cash transfer program of the government, as well as a higher allowance of P10,000 for teachers.

The economic team is set to present the NEP to the Cabinet on July 2 and submit it to Congress on July 29 (a week after SONA). It was emphasized that the budget will build on the gains from the first two years of the Marcos administration. The team is less optimistic on the inflation trend (hiking assumption from 3% to 4% from 2-4% in April).

Infrastructure spending jumps 18% in 4M24. Infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose 18% YoY to P410bn in 4M24. This implies that in April alone, infrastructure spending rose 39% YoY, 27% MoM to P143.4bn, amid the implementation of public works and transport sector projects. The government is targeting infrastructure disbursements to reach P1.47tn in 2024, up 3% YoY and equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

President Marcos signs Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform into law. President Marcos signed the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform into law. The reforms will take effect within two years. It seeks to standardize the property valuation used by the national government and local government units. The national government will be required to update the Schedule of Market Values every three years. Note that the values used for computing real property taxes can be 10-20 years out of date in some areas. Although Local Government Units will have to follow the Schedule of Market Values, they will have the power to reduce the tax rate on the said values. In the first year of implementation, the increase in real property taxes will be capped at 6%. Regis estimates that real property taxes account for 2%-7% of pretax profit of the developers under coverage. 

FDI net inflows up 23% YoY in March, 42% YoY in 1Q24. Foreign direct investments (FDI) posted a net inflow of US$686mn in March, up 23% YoY as non-residents’ net investments into debt instruments increased 19% YoY to US$465mn, while net investments in equity capital (ex-reinvestment) rose 67% to US$157mn.

Equity capital placements were primarily from Japan, Singapore, and USA and these were largely invested in manufacturing, financial/insurance, and real estate industries. This brings 1Q24 FDI to US$3bn, up 42% YoY. Note that the BSP is targeting FDI net inflows of US$9.0bn this year, slightly higher than 2023’s FDI of US$8.9bn.

LEDAC eyes approval of 28 priority bills by June 2025. National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) said the Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) agreed to prioritize the passage of 28 bills before the end of the 19th Congress in June 2025. 18 bills are considered “top priority”, the key ones among them:

  • Reforms in Philippine capital markets, which seeks to facilitate capital raising for companies through the stock market and boost the value of pension funds by reducing taxes on stock transactions and equalizing dividend tax
  • Bill amending the “Right-of-Way” Act to hasten the implementation of infrastructure projects
  • Rationalized mining fiscal regime, which seeks to impose margin-based royalties and a windfall profit tax on mining companies
  • CREATE MORE bill, which seeks to further lower taxes on domestic and foreign companies

April NPL, Past Due rates blip up; loans accelerate to new high since 2019. Loan growth (ex-interbank) in April shot up to 11.6% YoY (was 10.2% in March). That’s the fastest YoY growth rate since April 2019 (12.2%).

NPL and Past Due (PD) rates both blipped up, despite the larger loan denominator. Even bad-der-er, the deterioration was driven by the larger universal/commercial banks (UKBs). Asset quality actually improved MoM at the Thrift Bank (TB) and Digibank segments.

System-wide NPL (ex-interbank) ratios were up 6bps MoM to 3.53% (from 3.47% in March). In nominal terms, NPLs jumped 3.4% MoM to P480bn, nearly all of which (>97%) came from UKBs. NPLs for the UKBs were up 8bps MoM to 3.15%.  Surprisingly, NPLs for Digibanks dropped sharply to 17.69% in April, from 25.33% in March. Past Due (PD) ratios blipped up 13bps MoM to 4.43%, with the PHP value spiking 5.0% MoM to P618bn. This time, UKBs accounted for 101% of the sector’s PD increase MoM. Worryingly, the PD ratio is now at a near-2yr-high (since May 2022, at 4.44%). To the upside, Restructured Loans (RLs) continued to improve, down 7bps MoM to 2.08%, with the nominal value down 1.4% MoM.

 

Vehicle sales rise 5.5% YoY, 7.9% MoM in May. CAMPI reported car sales rose 5.5% YoY, 7.9% MoM to 40,271 units in May, which brings 5M24 car sales up 13% YoY to 187,191 units. CAMPI targets industry car sales to reach 468,300 units (+8% YoY) but also expressed optimism that car sales could reach 500k units this year (+16% YoY). Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 86,257 units in 5M24, up 12% YoY. This implies that for the month of May alone, TMP sold 18,677 units (+4.5% YoY, +4.3% MoM). To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.4k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 17k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

Indonesia cuts access to gambling websites from the Philippines. Bloomberg reported that Indonesia will cut access to online gambling sites from Cambodia and the Philippines. Note that online gambling is banned in Indonesia. Indonesia and other countries might put pressure on the Philippine government to ban offshore gaming activities conducted from the Philippines. Note that POGO firms occupy about 6% of Metro Manila office stock. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for: 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 43% of Megaworld, and 28% of Robinsons Land.

Department of Energy eyeing P31tn clean energy investment by 2040. The country needs to invest P20-31tn in renewable energy investment by 2040 to achieve its targets. By 2040, the country wants to achieve 5% energy savings, 10% electric vehicle penetration and 50% RE share in the generation mix, according to Energy Undersecretary Rowena Guevara.

Based on the demand, the target is to achieve a total capacity of over 52.8 gigawatts of RE, with 27 GW of solar, 16.6 GW of wind, six GW of hydro, 2.5 GW of geothermal and 360 megawatts of biomass. 

IT-BPM industry likely to hit 7% goal. The Information Technology and Business Process Management (IT-BPM) industry is on track to achieve 6.5%-7.5% growth in revenue and headcount this year, according to IT and Business Process Association of the Philippines (IBPAP) President Jack Madrid. Under the IT-BPM industry roadmap 2028, the industry is projected to hit 1.84mn (+8% YoY) in headcount and US$40bn (+12% YoY) in revenues. Mr. Madrid admitted that achieving 7% growth means the industry will just be hitting the “baseline scenario” and not the aggressive targets. Based on 7% growth targets, the industry may end the year with 1.82mn in headcount and around US$38bn in revenues. In 2023, the industry employed 1.7mn individuals and generated US$35.5bn in export revenues. Under the roadmap, the industry is targeting to reach 2.5 million jobs and generate $59 billion in revenues by 2028. BPO firms occupy about 60%-80% of office space of the major listed developers. Office leasing (including stakes in REITs) accounts for 49% of Filinvest Land’s NAV, 43% of Megaworld’s, and 28% of Robinsons Land’s.

Gov't eyes PPP auction of big-ticket railway projects in 1Q25. The government is hoping to auction off the O&M contracts for the following railway projects by 1Q25: MRT-3 – No indicative amount given, Metro Manila Subway – P72bn, and North-South Commuter Railway – P164bn.

Employers seek delay in minimum wage hike. In a public hearing, the Confederation of Wearable Exporters of the Philippines asked the NCR wage board to delay a proposed P100-750 wage hike. The group asked for a one-year moratorium on wage hikes to allow exporters to fully recover and preserve jobs until orders stabilize by 2025. The NCR wage board is set to release its decision on or before July 20, and is expected to set the tone for the wage increases in other regions. In 2022 and 2023, the government has implemented cumulative wage hikes of +20-22% across regions. In NCR alone, the cumulative wage hike was 13% over the same period. The cumulative headline inflation rate was 11.8% over the same period.
