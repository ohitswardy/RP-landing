REGIS REPORT

01 March 2024
 

The Philippines closed 4.5% higher in February, building on its 3.0% gain in the prior month to take it to +7.7% YTD.  Foreign inflows lifted the market, even as valuations remained near the lowest in over a decade (11x forward PER). The index closed the month just below the 7000-index level.

CPI in January was at 2.8%, falling below the December 2023 print of 3.9% and beating Street estimates of 3.1%.  The headline inflation number was pulled lower by food inflation, which moderated to +3.5% from 5.4% in December, as vegetable costs (-20.8%) helped to offset elevated rice prices. Rice prices recorded sustained increases at +22.6% from +19.6% in December. 

The decline in CPI was not a surprise as the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) had guided for a drop in early 2024 due to YoY base effects. The high base in early 2023 should likely help keep headline CPI low for February. Still, a re-acceleration in inflation beyond 1Q remains a possibility, as the base effects fade and as vegetable prices rebound. 

The BSP kept its policy rate (RRP) of 6.5% unchanged at its Monetary Board meeting. The move was widely expected given the BSP’s own pronouncements and the US Fed’s moves to keep rates steady. The BSP also lowered its inflation forecast for this year, reducing its baseline forecast to 3.6% from 3.7%. Expectations that cuts in the Fed funds rate would be pushed back due to the resilience of the US economy, among other things, have translated to prospects of higher rates domestically to endure for longer.

The opposing views on Constitutional amendment held by the two Houses of Congress hogged the headlines in February.  The Senate approved, on second reading, a bill raising the minimum wage across the country by P100 (US$1.8) per day. That figure is equivalent to a 16% increase in Metro Manila and a whopping 32% increase in southwestern Mindanao. In response, some members of the House of Representatives is proposing their own wage hike proposal to the tune of P400/day.  Wage increases of this quantum, though improbable, would likely be damaging not only to firms employing many low-skilled workers but also to inflation and inflation expectations.

The government officially declared the SMC SAP & Co consortium as the winning bidder of the NAIA airport rehabilitation project. The concessionaire will operate the airport for 15 years (plus probable 10-year extension) and is mandated to increase its capacity from 33m to 62m passengers per annum. The consortium is committing to spend P123bn for the airport and offered to give up 82% of (1) aircraft parking and landing fees and (2) non-aero revenues to the government for the concession. The consortium’s lead shareholder is San Miguel, which holds a 33% share.

The NAIA project is the first PPP project of this administration and involves the rehabilitation and upgrade of the ageing airport. The government though, is lining up 15 new PPP projects for approval this year.  These are on top of the ones already or about to be launched such as TPLEX extension and Laguindingan airport.

Key data
 

Trading activity fell in February with average turnover dropping to US$95.3m vs US$103.9m the month prior ($88m ex MSCi and PSEi rebal), as most investors struggled to find their footing given the mixed 4Q results and the Fed’s stance on cutting rates. The PSEi though managed to finish the month with a new 52-week high, with foreigners consistently net buyers in the market the whole February (20 straight days). Net inflow for the month stood at US$128m, the highest since July last year. Foreign participation finished at 50%, flat MoM.

 

  • Market continued to see foreign demand. The month’s inflow stood at US$128m -- the highest monthly inflow since July 2023. A couple of index names printed new 52-week highs propelled by strong foreign buying and helping push the index back to 6900 levels. Banks saw the biggest inflow as 4Q results showed loan growth picking up. BPI and BDO were the two top foreign bought names at US$35m and US$28m, respectively. MBT too finished the month with decent foreign inflows of US$5.2m and rallied +8.6% after reporting solid results and declaring a P3.50/share div.
  • Conglos overall saw foreign buying with SM the top foreign bought name in the sector, with US$25.6m in net inflows, after profits beat FY23 estimates. GTCAP also saw decent inflow of US$9.2m on the back of the outperformance of key subsidiaries MBT and auto business Toyota. AC likewise finished the month net bought but failed to secure monthly gains as traders sold the stock despite its upweight in the MSCi.
  • Property names were mixed. ALI saw inflow of $17.2m this month while SMPH  fell -7.7% on the back foreign selling. Consumer names also saw mixed flows. URC continue to see foreign selling despite a slowing inflation number. The stock is now net foreign sold for the 11th straight month, with foreign supply capping its share price. MONDE snapped four straight months of outflows, pushing the stock to rally 13.5% this month.
  • ICT led this month’s gainers with a +18.9% jump on the back of foreign demand. The  stock printed a new all time high this February.



 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The PSEi’s 298-index point advance in February was broad-based, with 19 of the 30 index members ending higher.

ICTSI (+18.9% MoM) was the top index gainer for the month, likely as investors anticipated a solid set of operational results for 4Q23.

ICTSI announced it reached a settlement with the labor union in Portland for US$20.5m. ICT will accept the amount as settlement for a lawsuit it filed against the union, which initiated a work stoppage at ICTSI’s Portland operations at that time, disrupting regular operations. ICT subsequently terminated its concession agreement in Portland in 2017 due to those labor issues.

ICTSI was also the top mover, contributing 85.8 points (or 28.8%) of the PSEi’s total rise for the month. 

After logging a 5.1% gain in January, continued buying interest in Monde (+13.5% MoM) propelled a stronger surge in February. Positive sentiment has been underscored by market share recovery for its domestic business as well as easing input costs.   Large cap Jollibee (+7.8% MoM) likewise had a strong performance during the month, as did Universal Robina (+4.9% MoM) which bounced back from its 5.7% decline in January. 

Retailers, meanwhile, were a mixed bag.  SSI Group (+29.4% MoM) gained significantly. Robinsons Retail (+6.4% MoM) also gained. The share price managed a rebound from the 11% fall in the prior month despite mildly soft numbers from core operations for 4Q/FY23. Core retail operations underperformed mildly, with revenues and EBIT up 7% and 2%, respectively, for FY23. For 4Q23, SSSG across the group decelerated (+1% vs +2.5% in 3Q) as inflation weighed on consumer demand.

Other retailers Puregold (-1.1% MoM) and Wilcon (-1.4% MoM) closed lower, however.

Banks continued to capture buying interest in the Phils, as elevated rates are expected to be supportive of NIMs while lending growth has started to accelerate. BPI (+13.1% MoM) advanced markedly, contributing 23% of the PSEi’s overall move during the month. A solid set of results for FY23 underpinned the positive sentiment for the stock. Headline profit for 2023 was P51.7bn, up 31% YoY. Loan growth jumped to 10.5% YoY, compared to 8.8% in 3Q23, while NIMs expanded 50bps YoY.

Metrobank (+8.6% MoM) also gained, with a surprise dividend declaration of P5/share for the year, equivalent to 8.5% yield and much higher than Regis expectation of P3/share.  Metrobank reported in-line profits for FY23 (+29% to P42.2bn). Loans grew 8%, driven by consumer loans, which expanded 16%. 

BDO (+5.5% MoM) likewise reported in-line profits (NPAT for FY23 at P73.41bn, +29%). Profits included a one-off, non-cash P6.1bn gain, which the bank used to accelerate loan loss provisions, accelerate depreciation of some IT assets, and write off some deferred tax assets. Loan growth accelerated to 9% (from 7.5%), while NIMs slightly softened (-6bps QoQ).

Smaller caps Security Bank (unch) and Union Bank (unch) were flat. 

Aboitiz Equity Ventures (+9.3% MoM) led the conglomerates during the month.  AEV and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP) completed the US$1.8bn acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Philippines Inc. (CCBPI), the exclusive bottler of Coca-Cola in the Philippines. The Philippines is Coca-Cola’s second largest market in Southeast Asia.  

JG Summit (+7.05% MoM) and GT Capital (+5.6% MoM) also gained.  GT Capital’s auto unit Toyota saw January sales rise by 20% YoY to 16k units, running mildly better vs Regis FY24 assumptions (206k). 

SM Investments (+4.4% MoM) reported FY23 profit of P77bn, up 25% and beating Regis and Street estimates on solid earnings from units BDO and SM Prime. For the retail segment, revenues rose 10% for the year while income grew 11%. 

Ayala Corp (-0.15% MoM) subsidiary Integrated Microelectronics (-0.4% MoM) disclosed that it is electing a new CEO effective May.  The new CEO, Lou Hughes, will replace long-time CEO Arthur Tan.  Meanwhile, AC says it is nearing the divestment of its minority stakes in the operator of the LRT-1 as well as Manila Water (+8.1% MoM), which will enable it to reach the US$1bn in divestments.  AC has so far managed to sell US$600m+ of assets under its divestment initiatives after previously announcing a target of raising US$1bn through asset sales by 2023.

Metro Pacific is keen to acquire Ayala Corp’s 35% stake in the LRT-1 operator.  MPI owns a 35.8% stake in LRT-1 while Ayala Corp owns 35%. MPI also reiterated plans to list its toll road investments after they are merged with San Miguel’s (-5.5% MoM). MPI CEO and President Manuel V. Pangilinan said the IPO can take place this year.  Such a listing could be among the biggest in recent memory; the EBITDA of combined Philippine operations is at least P50bn.

The government officially declared San Miguel’s consortium as the winning bidder of the NAIA airport rehabilitation project. The concessionaire will operate the airport for 15 years (plus probable 10-year extension) and is mandated to increase its capacity from 33m to 62m passengers per annum. The consortium offered to give up 82% of (1) aircraft parking and landing fees and (2) non-aero revenues to the government for the concession. It will keep passenger service charges. This compares to the payments of (1) P30bn upfront and (2) a P2bn annuity to the government, and P122bn in “investments” in the airport.

Meralco (+7.7% MoM) led the utilities. Meralco’s preliminary indications show that Jan 2024 unit sales are going to be ~10% higher YoY. While the YoY comparison looks better because of a soft base in Jan 2023, this latest figure is still a positive indicator for demand. Meralco’s reported income for FY23 came in at P38bn, up 34% YoY. The full-year profit appears to be mildly ahead of Street estimates (P36bn),  though 4Q results were sequentially weaker than the prior periods due to the generation business.  Distribution utility volumes rose 4.2% in 4Q (FY23 volumes +4.4%). 

Aboitiz Power (-2.6% MoM) closed lower. AP raised its stake in STEAG State to 85% (from just under 80%) for US$11m.  STEAG State runs a 210MW coal-fired power plant in northern Mindanao under a BOT contract with the government until 2031.

Semirara’s (+4.2% MoM) FY23 headline profit of P27.9bn (-30% YoY) came in below our forecast (P33bn) and consensus (P31.5bn), even though revenues (P77bn) were within expectations.

Telcos Globe (0.4% MoM) and PLDT (+3.2% MoM) ended the month with gains.

Globe’s core income for FY23 came in at P19.1bn, down 1%, and below expectations. While the bottom line was a disappointment, the mobile business showed positive signs. Mobile revenue for 4Q23 was up a surprising 8% YoY.  Mynt’s profit contribution continued to grow, and capex fell 30% and is set to drop another 20%+ this year.

PLDT and ABS-CBN (-0.4% MoM)  announced that PLDT’s purchase of Sky Cable from ABS-CBN will not proceed.  The termination of the deal, after approval from the competition authority, comes after years of back-and-forth negotiations and three announcements about a transaction.  PLDT was acquiring Sky for its broadband business, which had around 300,000+ subscribers. Meanwhile, it was also disclosed that PLDT agreed to settle for US$3m the US class action lawsuit filed against it over the capex overrun issue in 2022.

Converge (-2.9% MoM) consolidated after its 10.6% rise in the previous month. The company is investing P150m (US$2.7m) for its pay TV business. This follows the collapse of the Sky Cable deal between PLDT  and ABS-CBN.

Ayala Land (+2.6% MoM) gained, after a 1% drop in January.  ALI is embarking on a P13bn, 3-year redevelopment of four of its flagship malls which account for 35% of its mall space and 50% of mall revenue.  Malls account for around 25% of ALI’s EBIT. Meanwhile, the company reported in-line earnings for FY23, up 32% to P24.5bn, in line with consensus and ahead of REGIS forecast. The company has started delivering on its plan to reduce land bank, consuming 1,000 hectares in 2023. It plans to use another 800 hectares annually until 2028, which would reduce land bank by c. 40% over six years.

Meanwhile, SM Prime (-7.7% MoM) corrected, in the wake of its 4.1% gain in the prior month. SM Prime’s 4Q23 net profit rose 22% YoY (-8% QoQ) to P9.9bn. Full year earnings of SM Prime grew 33% to P40bn, in line with REGIS and consensus forecasts, driven by the predictably robust performance of its core mall business.

Robinsons Land (+2.4% MoM) rose, while Megaworld (-1.5% MoM) ended lower. FY23 earnings of Megaworld grew 29% to P17.3bn, ahead of REGIS and consensus forecasts, as revenue from every segment outperformed.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Headline inflation slows to 2.8% in January. Headline CPI grew 2.8% YoY in January, slowing from 3.9% in December. This came in better than consensus forecast of 3.1% and was at the lower-end of the BSP’s target range of 2.8%-3.6% for the month due to moderation in food items to 3.4% from 4.4% the previous month. Vegetable and meat prices continued to decline while inflation for fish slowed. These offset the sustained increases in rice prices (+22.6% from +19.6% in Dec). Lower commodity prices still aiding in the decline of transport and electricity inflation.

On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.6% MoM, slower than past seasonal trends (+0.9%). Core inflation moderated for the 10th consecutive month to 3.8%, with the core non-food category also declining further to 3.6%. The latest inflation print is consistent with the BSP’s outlook that inflation will likely moderate in Q1 2024 due largely to negative base effects and the easing of supply constraints affecting key commodities. Regis is forecasting inflation will moderate to 4.6% this year from 6.0% in 2023 (consensus FY24E: 3.7%). The first Monetary Board meeting for 2024 is scheduled on Feb 15.

BSP maintains policy rate. The Monetary Board left the target reverse repurchase rate at 6.5% for the third straight meeting. Meanwhile the BSP adjusted its risk-adjusted inflation forecasts downwards. The 2024 “risk-adjusted” forecast was lowered to 3.9% from 4.2% previously while “baseline” forecast was lowered to 3.6% from 3.7% while the 2025 “risk-adjusted” forecast was raised to 3.5% from 3.4% while “baseline” forecast was kept at 3.2%. BSP said there is “scope for a rate cut” this year as soon as they see a firmer inflation downtrend. Inflation might continue to ease this quarter due to base effects, but it might pick up above the 2-4% target in the second quarter. Inflation will go back to the target in the second half. They also stated that higher-than-expected minimum wage increases in the Philippines could reverse slowing inflation.

GIR settles at US$103.4bn in January. Preliminary data showed the GIR level settled at US$103.4bn as of end-Jan 2024. This is lower by US$347mn (-0.3%) MoM from end-Dec 2023 level of US$103.8bn. The  gov’t cited debt payments and decrease in price of gold. The GIR is equivalent to 7.7 months’ worth of imports. The BSP expects the GIR to end at US$102bn this year.

Remittances rise 3.8% YoY in December; FY23 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn. Remittances coursed through banks rose 3.8% YoY to US$3.28bn in December. This was the fastest pace since Dec 2022 and the absolute amount was also a new high. In PHP terms, remittances were up 3.7% YoY in Dec. Growth accelerated in almost all key remittances sources, except for Kuwait, Singapore, and UAE. Remittances from Kuwait fell for the 8-straight month. Meanwhile, remittances growth from Singapore and UAE slowed to 2.4% and 3.8% in Dec from 3.3% and 9.3% the previous month. These were more than offset by stronger growth rates in most countries. For FY23, remittances grew by 2.9% YoY to US$33.5bn, broadly in line with the BSP’s forecast growth rate of 3%. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% in 2024.

January BoP swings to deficit. Preliminary data showed the balance of payments swung to a US$740mn deficit in January. Reversed surpluses of US$3.08bn in Jan 2023 and US$642mn in December. It was also the largest deficit recorded in 11 months. The government cited outflows arising from the national government’s payments of foreign currency obligations. The GIR dipped 0.5% MoM to US$103.3bn in January.

FDI net inflows rise 28% YoY to US$1bn in November. Preliminary data showed foreign direct investments (FDI) posted net inflows of US$1.05bn in November, up 28% YoY and 60% MoM. This was due to the 58% YoY and 78% MoM jump in nonresidents’ net investments in debt instruments to US$897mn. Investments in equity capital (ex-reinvestment of earnings) fell 53% YoY to US$85mn while reinvestment of earnings dropped 8% YoY to US$66mn. Cumulative 11M23 FDI net inflows stood at US$7.6bn, lower by 13% YoY.

First investment of Maharlika Investment Fund will be in power sector. Maharlika Investment Fund CEO Joel Consing said that the fund’s first investment will be in the power sector. President Marcos previously said that the government was studying a proposal to nationalize National Grid Corp of the Philippines. Mr. Consing said that in three years, each sector should not comprise more than 15% of the fund.  

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth steady at 7.0% in December; M3 growth moderates to 5.9%. Preliminary data showed commercial banks’ loans grew by 7.0% YoY in December, unchanged from Nov ’23 (7.0%). Outstanding loans for production activities rose by 5.5% in December from 5.7% in November due to slowdown in real estate loans (10.9% in Dec from 20.8% in Nov); wholesale and retail trade (7.2% from 9.6%), electricity (6.2% from 12.8%). Consumer loans increased by 23.5% in December from 23.6% in November. Credit card loans grew 29.6%, slowing from 31.0% in Nov. Auto loans up 16% YoY in Dec, broadly unchanged from 15.6% YoY in Nov. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3 growth) slowed further to 5.9% YoY in December from 7.0% in November.

Banks’ end-2023 NPL ratio drops to 3.23%. Preliminary data showed Philippine banks’ NPL ratio fell to 3.23% as of end-Dec from 3.41% in end-Nov. Gross NPLs stood at P447bn in end-Dec, up 12% YoY but down 1.6% MoM. Loans (net of interbank) amounted to P13.5tn, up 10.1% YoY and 3.8% MoM (faster than November loan growth of 9.3% YoY). NPL coverage ratio at 102.13% in end-Dec ’23, down from 107% in end-Dec ’22 but higher than 101.47% in end-Nov ’23. Past due loans rose 14% YoY to P547bn, bringing past due ratio to 3.95% from 4.22% in end-Nov ’23 and 3.79% in end-Dec 2022. Restructured loans dropped 8.3% YoY to P302bn, accounting for 2.19% of total loans (down from 2.61% in end-Dec ‘22 and 2.29% in end-Nov ’23).

Maharlika fund is in talks for a potential JV for rural telco towers. Maharlika Investment Corp. (MIC) President Rafael Consing discussed the potential of a JV with foreign partners to build telecommunication towers in rural areas. No foreign companies have been named due to nondisclosure agreements but rural areas have already been identified for construction. Clients of the JV tower company will primarily be incumbent telco players. The project is said to be in the pipeline of investments for next year.

Senate approves P100/day wage increase on 3nd reading.The Senate approved on third reading a bill calling for a P100 across-the-board minimum wage increase for workers in the private sector. The Bill exempts businesses with less than 10 employees, establishments with less than P3mn capital, and barangay micro enterprises. If implemented, this could result in a 22-24% increase in minimum wages for the private sector across all regions. The current minimum wage in the National Capital Region is P573-610/day (~USD10-11). The House of Representatives is looking to pass a bill raising minimum wages by up to P400/day.

House committee approves bill banning POGO. The House Games and Amusements panel approved a bill banning Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators due to crimes linked to the sector. However, PAGCOR Chairman Alejandro Tengco is in favor of allowing them to continue operating if they are properly monitored and regulated. He said that since the number of POGO firms (now called Internet Gaming Licensee) has been reduced to 75 (from nearly 300 in 2019), the number of crimes has dropped substantially. He also said that licensees now come from more countries, such as Malaysia, Singapore, United States, and European countries. Separately, the Local Government Unit of Pasig shut down POGO, E-games, and E-bingo establishments in the city. A City Ordinance in 2022 gave the gambling establishments until the end of 2023 to wind down their operations in Pasig. Regis estimates that c. 5% of POGO office space is located in Pasig City.

 

House approves Bill reducing stock transaction tax. The House of Representatives approved on third reading House Bill 9277 or the Capital Markets Efficiency Promotion Act. The Bill seeks to reduce stock transaction tax from 0.6% to 0.1%. It would also lower the dividend tax for non-resident investors from 25% to 10%. It also seeks to remove the 7% gross receipts tax on net trading gains by banks and financial institutions. 

Government pushes P839bn water infrastructure projects. Finance Undersecretary Catherine Fong said that the government wants to implement 44 infrastructure flagship projects worth P839bn aimed at boosting water resources. The largest is the P115bn Central Luzon-Pampanga River Floodway Control Project (funding through Official Development Assistance). The other big ticket item is the P102bn Paranaque Spillway Tunnel Project (funding through Japanese ODA loan). All projects are awaiting government approvals and are part of the Marcos administration’s Build Better More program of P8.78tn infrastructure projects.

E-games revenue hits P58bn in 2023. PAGCOR said that revenue from E-games grew over 92% to P58bn in 2023. This comprises e-games, e-bingo, specialty games, and sports betting (does not include POGO). In 2022, GGR from Integrated Resorts in PAGCOR Entertainment City hit P146bn.

Korean tourists jump to post-Covid high in January. Tourist arrivals jumped 24% YoY and 15% MoM to 574k in January 2024, sustaining the big recovery in December 2023. The latest data showed a reversion to 83% of pre-Covid, a new post-covid high due to a 38% YoY, 15% MoM recovery in Korean tourists, which at 174k was already 5% above 2019 average. Chinese tourists jumped 3.5x YoY and 76% MoM to 33k, reverting to 23% of pre-Covid levels. Improving tourism data should bode well for the current account and GDP.

Department of Transportation launches Swiss Challenge for Laguindingan International Airport. The Department of Transportation has started seeking comparative proposals to develop and improve the Laguindingan International Airport in Misamis Oriental. The proposals will serve as competitive challenge to Aboitiz InfraCapital Inc.’s unsolicited proposal in 2018 for the takeover of Laguindingan Airport. The National Economic and Development Board last month approved negotiated parameters, terms, and conditions for the P12.75bn airport project. 

DOE sets 4,399MW green energy auction program in August. The Department of Energy will conduct the Third Round of the Green Energy Auction (GEA-3) this August with 4,399MW of the following renewable energy up for bidding. 699MW from impounding Hydro with a target Delivery Commencement Period (DCP) from 2028-2030. 3120MW from Pumped-Storage Hydro with a DCP from 2028-2030. 380MW from Geothermal with a DCP beginning 2026-2028. 200MW from Run-of-river Hydro with a DCP from 2026-2028. GEA-3 will cater to non-feed-in-tariff eligible renewable energy. No price caps will be set and developers will have to submit price offers pending the review of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).

DOE, DENR sign MOA to speed up offshore wind projects. The Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) signed a memorandum of agreement (MOA) that will simplify the issuance of an environmental compliance certificate (ECC) for offshore wind energy projects. MOA covers pre-development and exploration activities. Once DENR issues an interim ECC, developers can push through pre-development activities and will not need to undergo consultations with LGUs and communities yet. Other renewable technologies do not have interim ECC, and the ECC is only required during the development stage.
