REGIS REPORT

02 January 2024
 

The Philippines climbed 3.6%  in December. The market rose along with global markets, which were propped higher by the Fed’s decision to hold rates steady.  Foreign buying into the market accelerated amidst decidedly improved liquidity. The market’s performance in December took the index to -1.8% for the full year. The performance in 2023 extended the losing streak for the PCOMP to four years.

The market had a strong open in 2023, surging in January (3.45%) as risk appetite for the EM improved on the back of benign US inflation. Foreigners resumed selling in February however, with the early days of the month marking the last time the PCOMP traded above the 7000- level for the year. The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank and Credit Suisse caused markets to be jittery early in the year. For most of the year however, it was the Fed and the direction of interest rates that shaped the markets. Things went further downhill in August for the PCOMP, with a disappointing 2Q GDP print spurring a 6.3% plunge for the month. The Philippines suffered a steep 5.5% drop again in October -- breaching the 6,000 Index level – on the back of a disappointing September inflation print and the BSP’s announcement of an off-cycle policy rate hike.  The sub-6000-index point close in October encouraged buying interest subsequently. Gains in the last two months of the year pared losses which peaked at 9.2% (YTD as of Oct 27).

Headline Inflation decelerated to 4.1% in November from 4.9% in October, fueling market optimism on interest rates. The November CPI came in better than consensus forecast of 4.3% and near the low end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.0%-4.8%. The deceleration came as a results of slower increases in food (ex-rice) and lower petrol prices. Rice price inflation was actually higher in November though, compared to the previous month (15.8% vs. 13.2%).

With the dip in inflation, a policy rate pause was widely priced in.  True enough,  the BSP moved in step with the Fed keeping the policy rate steady at 6.5%. The BSP cited that inflation risks “lean significantly to the upside”, but it expects 1Q24 inflation to fall within its target band of 2-4% before rising again in 2Q24.

The outlook for the headline CPI inflation number over the next few months will appear good because of the high base of comparison in early 2023. The key risk by March and beyond, would be commodity prices, particularly rice. Rice prices have been on the rise again -- with export prices out of Vietnam climbing to new highs and those out of Thailand bouncing. Meanwhile, El Niño is forecast to bring droughts in 1Q24, pointing to consumers having to brace for potentially higher rice prices in 2024.

The government is looking at extending, by another year, reduced tariffs on a number of key agricultural products, including rice, corn and pork. Allowing the temporary reduction of tariffs to lapse would immediately show in the inflation print. With the Philippines dependent on imports to meet the supply shortfalls, the landed cost of imports have an outsized impact on prices domestically. A temporary reduction in the 35% tariff on rice imports from Vietnam and Thailand could significantly diminish the pressure on prices but the administration has so far refused to allow it.

Auguring well for the local currency, the 3Q23 current account deficit fell 65% YoY and 48% QoQ to US$2.1bn on the back of lower commodity prices as well as higher contributions from tourism, BPO revenues, and OFW remittances . This brought the 9M23 current account deficit to US$11.0bn, down 40% YoY. The BSP also raised its BOP forecast to a US$1.1bn surplus, from a deficit of US$100m, due to higher foreign loans taken by the government. The BOP surplus has helped to keep the peso relatively stable in 2023 with a performance of +0.6% to PHP55.385/US$. The BOP should continue to be supportive, with the BSP expecting a surplus of US$400m this year.

Key data
 
 

Turnover improved in December to US$89.0m (+25% MoM) skewed mostly in the middle of the month after the Fed signaled that rates may have peaked and began discussing for a potential easing by next year. BSP also kept policy rate unchanged which added confidence to the market. Focus still remained in Banks and Property  given their sensitivity to interest rates alongside some consumer names with inflation slowing down. Conglos AC and SM also saw demand as investors bought into these two names as market proxies when the PSEi rallied. Foreign participation was much lower this month, taking 49% of the split vs 53% the month prior.

But the two still managed to finish the year with decent inflow of around $30m each.

While ICT continues to see foreign demand for the 3rd straight month and propelled the stock to a new all time levels this December.
  • Despite slowing inflation numbers, Consumer names overall saw outflows this month with URC the top sold for $14.9m.   • ACEN was also top sold with an outflow of US$8.1m. It fell as low as -26% this month before closing the month -8.8%. Other notable names this month are SMC and AREIT with both ending the month with inflows mainly due to a small upweight in the FTSE index.

 

 

 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The 3.6% rally of the PSEi during the month was broad based, with gainers beating losers 11:4.

The top index mover was ICTSI (+14.4% MoM). The stock surged as investors priced in a more stable global economy and a recovery in global trade on the view of lower interest rates in 2024. The company also recently secured a major client at its port in Melbourne, which has been the biggest drag to group volumes/revenues in 2023. Management believes that the upcoming vessel calls of the new client could allow them to recover all the volumes it lost by Jan 2024. Moreover, the Philippine port regulator also approved a 32% hike in storage fees, with a 150% surcharge on reefers, across all international ports in the country.   The stock was one of the top performing index names in 2023, advancing by 23.4%.

Cebu Air (+3.2% MoM), meanwhile, gained in December but ended the year down 15.1%. This marks the fourth straight year of declines for Cebu Air. While the airline has reversed losses from the pandemic, fleet availability issues and tempered capacity guidance for next year have weighed on sentiment.

The property sector extended its gains in December, seeing inflows with the outlook that interest rates have peaked and are set to come off beginning 2024. Ayala Land (+10.4% MoM) led the sector and was among the top gainers for the month.

AREIT (+11.9% MoM) announced a series of transactions wherein AREIT will acquire properties from related parties Ayala Land and ACEN (-8.8% MoM). The three main transactions are: 1) AREIT will purchase a hotel from Ayala Land for P1.2bn in cash; 2) AREIT will acquire P21.8bn worth of office, mall, and hotel assets by issuing 642m shares to Ayala Land; and 3) AREIT will acquire P6.8bn worth of industrial land by issuing 199m shares to ACEN.

SM Prime (+1.9% MoM) likewise gained on follow though buying post the lifting of a ban on its coastal development project. The project accounts for a significant chunk of its NAV (23% of Regis NAV). 

Robinsons Land (+9.8% MoM) played catch-up in December. Lance Gokongwei will take over as President and CEO of the company starting January 8, with current president Frederick Go appointed as Cabinet Secretary for the new Office of the Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs (OSAPIEA). He will serve as the Chairperson of the Economic Development Group, and will supervise the Department of Finance (DOF), National Economic Development Authority (NEDA), Department and Industry, among other agencies.

For the year, Ayala Land led with a gain of 11.9% after declining by 16.1% in 2022. ALI outperformed SM Prime which registered a drop of 7.3% this year – having outperformed (+4.7% in 2022) previously. Investors have also discounted the Paranaque reclamation project which has yet to be granted national government approval.  With a surge late in the year, Robinsons Land ended 2023 up 6.6%, recovering part of its marked declines in the previous years (-22.1% in 2022).  Undoubtedly the view on interest rate direction has been helping boost property names.

 

The view on rates has also triggered rotation from the banks which had done well previously.  Largest most liquid bank BDO (-2.2% MoM) saw profit-taking in December and was one of the few index decliners for the month.  The stock is still up a significant 23.5% for 2023, however.  

BPI (+0.5% MoM), whose merger with Robinsons bank was approved by the BSP this month, ended slightly higher.  BPI likewise outperformed the index for the year, accumulating a gain of 10.9%. The merger is expected to increase BPI's asset base by 6-7%, making it the 2nd largest bank. It also gives BPI access to the vast ecosystem of customers, suppliers, etc of the Gokongwei Group. BPI will be the surviving entity after the merger. The Gokongwei group will own 6% of BPI after the merger.

Meanwhile, Metrobank (+2.7% MoM) was up on laggard interest during the month, paring its YTD decline to 5.0%.

Conglomerates were mostly higher in December, with large cap SM Investments (+6.5% MoM) the second biggest index mover for the month. Ayala Corp (+2.6% MoM) likewise gained, cementing its 9.7% climb in the previous month.   Ayala Corp’s healthcare unit, AC Health, acquired a “significant minority stake” in retail pharmacy, St. Joseph Drug (Joleco Resources, Inc.).  St. Joseph Drug has 112 stores, which will add to AC Health’s Generika chain of 742 and bring it closer to its goal of 1,000 stores by 2025.

Mid caps Alliance Global (+15.1% MoM) and DMCI (+6.2% MoM) surged, as did GT Capital (+8.8% MoM) whose automobile unit Toyota continued to gain market share. For the month of November, Toyota sales grew 11% YoY (+1% MoM) despite industry vehicle sales slowing down. GT Capital led the holdcos in 2023, with a gain of 35.6%. San Miguel (-5.5% MoM) followed with a gain of 9.8% for the year. The rest were down, with SM and AC recording losses of 3.1% and 2.0%, respectively. Large index names Aboitiz Equity Ventures (-4.7% MoM) and JG Summit (-0.9% MoM) were down a steeper 22.7% and 24.2%, respectively.

Manila International Airport Consortium, comprised of Aboitiz Equity, Ayala Corp, LT Group, Alliance Global, Filinvest Development Group (+5.3% MoM), and JG Summit and US-based Global Infrastructure Partners submitted bid documents for the NAIA PPP project.  Three other consortia submitted bids 1) Asian Airport Consortium - Cosco Capital (+0.9% MoM), Asian Infrastructure Management Corp, Philippine Skylanders International, and PT Angkasa Pura II, 2) GMR Airports Consortium - House of Investments (unch), Cavitex Holdings, GMR Airports, 3) SMC SAP & Co Consortium – San Miguel Holdings, RMM Asian Logistics, RLW Aviation Development, Incheon International Airport Corp. The P170.6bn project aims to raise passenger capacity to 62m annually from 32m today over the 15-year concession period (extendable by 10 years). The Department of Transportation aims to award the concession in 1Q24.

 

The top index decliner in December was ACEN (-8.8% MoM) with sentiment continuing to be dampened by weak operating results. ACEN is also one of the top index losers this wear, dropping 42.1%, partly brought on by a placement in the stock early in the year (335m shares at Php6.70 by Mermac) and its deletion from the MSCI index during the rebal in February. AC holders entitled to receive ACEN shares as part of a property dividends announced in 2022 also saw delays in receiving these shares, serving as overhang on the stock.

Meralco (+10.8% MoM) subsidiary MGen Renewable Energy (MGreen) paid the remaining balance of P8.9bn to acquire common and preferred shares of solar power developer SP New Energy Corporation (-9.6% MoM).  This would give MGreen a 31.4% economic stake and 50.5% voting stake in SPNEC.  Manuel V. Pangilinan, CEO of PLDT, Meralco, and Metro Pacific, was elected as Chairman, President, and CEO of SPNEC.

Meanwhile, SPNEC will buy out Prime Infrastructure in its joint venture.  SPNEC will pay P6bn to take full control of what would have been a 50-50 solar joint venture (“Terra Solar”) to provide Meralco with 850MW of power. Terra Solar is building what is to be the world’s largest solar farm.  The capacity of the farm will be 3,500MW upon completion and it will be complemented by 4,000MWh battery storage facilities.

Prime Infrastructure, meantime, signed a 15-year Terminal Lease Agreement with First Gen (+4.8% MoM) for the latter’s LNG storage and regasification terminal in Batangas City. Prine Infrastructure has control of the Malampaya gas field, which supplies gas to First Gen.

The telcos underperformed in December.  PLDT (-0.7%) was down mildly.  PLDT CEO Al Panlilio retired effective end of the year, because of “health reasons”. Manny Pangilinan will resume his role as interim CEO.  Globe (+0.6% MoM) turned over another 120 telecom towers to Frontier Towers for P1.5bn.  This latest closing brings the total amount raised from tower sales to ~P55bn to date.  In total, Globe signed deals for the sale of over 7,500 towers for P96bn.

Converge (+2.1% MoM) managed to close the month up, but ended the year down a significant 47.2%, with the share price reflecting the slowdown of broadband against lofty market expectations.

In terms of news flow, the consumer sector was quiet in December.   Large caps Jollibee (+10.9% MoM) and Universal Robina (+4.6% MoM) fared well while losses were seen in retailers Puregold (-1.1% MoM), Robinsons Retail (-1.1% MoM), Wilcon (-1.2%) and SSI (-18.9%). 

For the year, CNPF (+1.8% MoM) delivered a hefty gain of 20.0%, thanks to its inclusion in the PSEi.  Other names reporting strong gains were San Miguel Food and Beverage (up 31.8% in 2023), Shakey’s Pizza (up 28.1%), and SSI Group (+45.5%).  On the flipside, large losses were seen coming from D&L (-20.1%), Monde (-24.4%), Puregold (-22.9%), Robinsons Retail (-26.4%), and Wilcon (-29.2%)

Among the consumer large caps, Jollibee closed 9.3% higher for the year, while URC dropped 13.1%.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases further to 4.1% in November. Headline CPI rose at a slower rate of 4.1% YoY in November from 4.9% in October. This is below consensus estimate (4.3%) and near the low-end of the BSP’s forecast range (4.0%-4.8%). Softening due to slower increases in food (e.g. vegetables, meat, fish, sugar) and oil prices. Offset by an acceleration in rice inflation (15.8% in November from 13.2% in October). 

On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2%, accelerating from -0.2% in October due largely to higher rice prices. Non-food items decelerated further to +0.05% from +0.20% in October. 

Core CPI also eased to 4.7% from 5.3%, with core non-food item portion also rising at a slower rate of 4.2% (vs. 4.6% in Oct). Average headline inflation rate in 11M23 at 6.2%. 

BSP keeps policy rate steady at 6.5%. The BSP held its key policy rate at 6.5% for the second consecutive meeting, in line with consensus. It said that the risks to the inflation outlook still lean significantly to the upside. The upside risks include transport charges, electricity prices, and oil prices

New inflation forecasts:
2023 – 6.0%, from 6.1% previously
2024 – 4.2%, from 4.4% previously
2025 – 3.4%

Inflation is expected to return to the target 2%-4% range by 1Q24. The BSP then expects it to accelerate in 2Q24 (due to El Nino and the delayed impact of wage hikes) before returning back to the range in 3Q24. BSP Governor Remolona said that “The Monetary Board continues to see the need to keep monetary policy settings sufficiently tight to allow inflation expectations to settle more firmly within the target range”

Remittances rise by 3% in October. Cash remittances coursed through banks rose by 3% YoY to US$2.998bn in October. In PHP terms, remittances for October slipped by 0.6% YoY. This was driven by higher land-based remittances from Singapore (+12.5% YoY), Saudi Arabia (6.6%), and the UAE (+10.5%). Additionally, this brings 10M23 remittances up 2.8% YoY to US$27.49bn. The BSP is forecasting remittances to rise by 3% this year.

End-November GIR settles at US$101.3bn. Preliminary data showed the GIR rose to US$101.3bn in end-November from US$101.0bn in end-October. This represents 7.5 months’ worth of imports. This reflected mainly upward valuation adjustments in the value of the BSP’s gold holdings and net income from investments abroad. The BSP is forecasting a GIR level of US$99.5bn by end-2023.

Current account deficit shrinks to US$2.1bn in 3Q23. Preliminary data showed the current account deficit narrowed by 48% QoQ, 65% YoY to US$2.1bn in 3Q23. The 3Q23 current account deficit equivalent to 2.0% of GDP, down from 3.8% of GDP in 2Q23 and 6.5% in 3Q22. The lower YoY deficit was due to lower commodity prices, tourism recovery, higher BPO revenues and overseas remittances. BPO export revenues rose by 4% YoY to US$7.4bn in 3Q23 and 7.6% YoY to US$21.3bn in 9M23. This brought the YT-September current account deficit to US$10.95bn, down 40% YoY. The BSP raised its BOP surplus forecast for 2023 to US$1.1bn from US$100m previously due to higher inflows from the government’s foreign loan availments.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.18bn in October. Preliminary data showed the country’s trade deficit widened by 26% YoY to US$4.18bn in October. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was also higher by 17% compared to September’s deficit of US$3.58bn. Imports fell by 4% YoY to US$10.54bn, slowest monthly decline in 7 months. Fuel and raw material imports fell by 5% and 7%, respectively, due to lower commodity prices. Capital goods imports also fell by 5% but consumer goods imports rose by 5%. Meanwhile, exports collapsed by 17% YoY to US$6.36bn due largely to base effects (Exports peaked at US$7.71bn in Oct 2022). However, Oct exports appear to be recovering relative to the monthly average in 9M23. This brings YT-October trade deficit to US$44.07bn, down 12% YoY.

Balance of payments swings to US$216m deficit in November. Preliminary data showed the country’s balance of payments swung to a deficit of US$216m in November after recording a US$1.5bn surplus in October. Recall October BOP surplus was boosted by the government’s issuance of US$1.24bn global bonds. The BOP deficit in November, however, is lower than the US$756m deficit in November 2022. This brings 11M23 BOP position to surplus of US$3.0bn, a reversal of the US$7.9bn deficit in the same period a year ago. The BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.1bn by year-end, a reversal of the US$7.3bn BOP deficit in 2022.

 

Philippine rice imports as of mid-December hits 3.2m MT. The country’s rice imports as of mid-December have reached 3.22m MT according to the Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI). In 2022, total rice imports reached 3.826m MT. Vietnam remained the country’s top source of imported rice, accounting for 87% (2.813m MT) of the total import volume as of December 14. Vietnam FOB rice spot price has climbed to US$666/MT (vs. average of US$652/MT in November).

Congress ratifies P5.77tn 2024 budget.Congress ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee’s report on the P5.77tn 2024 budget. The Bicam approved a P450bn increase in unprogrammed appropriations to P731.4bn. Unprogrammed appropriations are put on standby in case of additional priority programs or projects when revenue collection exceeds targets. No material changes to the proposed budget of the Cabinet.

FDI net inflows fall 42% YoY to US$422m in September.  Net foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows fell 42% YoY to US$422m in September. This brings 9M23 net FDI to US$5.88bn, down 15.9% YoY. Bulk of gross equity capital placements came from Japan, Singapore, USA, and Germany and these were invested in manufacturing (50%), real estate (14%), and financial/insurance (12%).

Budget deficit narrows 25% YoY to P93bn in November as primary spending falls. The government recorded a budget deficit of P93bn in November, down 25% YoY due largely to (a) 10% drop in primary spending and (b) timing differences of VAT collections. Revenues rose 2.8% YoY on higher dividend remittances and government share from PAGCOR. Meanwhile, tax revenues dropped 9% YoY due to shift in the deadline for VAT collections (i.e. from monthly to quarterly beginning Jan 2023). Expenses fell 4.7% YoY due to a 10% YoY drop in primary spending. This brings 11M23 budget deficit to P1.11tn, down 10% YoY.

BIR slaps 1% withholding tax on online merchants. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has officially imposed a 1% creditable withholding tax on one-half of the gross remittances of online platform providers to their partner sellers or merchants. The Withholding tax imposed, however, will only apply to online merchants and online sellers with cumulative gross remittances of more than P500,000 for the past taxable year. The tax will apply to online shopping, food delivery platforms, platforms for booking of resort, hotel, motel, inn, house, condominium unit, bed space, room for rent and other similar lodging accommodations, and other service or product marketplaces. The BIR said the withholding tax imposed shall be in addition to the existing withholding tax obligations being imposed on e-marketplace operators. Last year, the digital economy contributed P2.08tn, equivalent to 9.4% of GDP. Of this, e-commerce had the highest growth at 26.5%, with its share to the economy reaching 20% or P416.12bn. Data showed that there are roughly two million entities involved in online selling as of last year.

President Marcos signs EO to extend lower import tariffs on rice, pork, corn. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has approved the extension of reduced tariffs on rice, corn, and meat products until the end of December 2024. The government cited looming negative impact of dry weather on the price and production of rice and corn and the ongoing prevalence of African Swine Fever that restricts meat trade in some exporting countries.

 

Tourist arrivals revert to 64% of pre-Covid in November. Tourist arrivals in November reached 440k, equivalent to 64% of 2019 average. This is up from 407k in October (59% of pre-Covid) due to higher visitor arrivals from Korea, USA, and Japan, which were 71%, 75%, 46% of pre-covid levels, respectively. Chinese tourists still lagging, at just 15% of pre-Covid levels.

MWSS approves second tranche of water rate hike. Water regulator, MWSS, approved an increase of P6.41 (+17%) per cubic meter (cum) for Manila Water (MWC PM), and a hike of P7.87 (+20%) per cum for Maynilad Water. The latest adjustment in water rates is the second tranche of the approved tariffs for the 2023 to 2027 period, as well as two thirds of CPI. Recall that in the 2022 rate rebasing, MWC and Maynilad were granted “R” tariff adjustments of +73% and +38%, respectively, which will be spread out over five years. Regis valuation/profit estimates assume these tariff hikes. MWC is a 22%-owned unit of Ayala Corp. (AC PM) and accounts for 3% of its NAV; AC trades at 26% discount to its market-based NAV of P869/sh. Maynilad is a 25%-owned unit of DMCI (DMC PM) and accounts for 25% of its NAV; DMC trades at a 24% discount to its market-based NAV of P12.30/sh.

Loan growth accelerates to 7.1% in October. Loan growth for universal and commercial banks (UKBs) accelerated in October to 7.1% YoY, from 6.5% in September. The improvement was seen nearly across the board, especially for loans “for production”.

Construction loans jumped 10.1% YoY, from 5.9% in September. Wholesale and retail trade loans sped up to 9.3% YoY, up from 6.6% Real estate loans grew 8.3% YoY, compared to 5.0% the prior month. The construction and real estate loans could be indicative of increased capex and/or government spending. Meanwhile, the wholesale/retail loans might mean traders are (finally) building up inventory for the holiday season.

On the consumer (ex-mortgage) side, growth slowed to 22.8% YoY in October, from 23.5% the previous month. However, the drag came from salary loans, which decelerated sharply to 6.5% YoY. Meanwhile, credit card growth was steady at 30.4% YoY, while motor vehicle loans sped further to 14.4% YoY (from 13.4%).

Senate to ratify FTA with South Korea by January. The Philippine Senate aims to ratify the Free Trade Agreement with South Korea when Congress resumes session in January. International agreements and treaties require a concurrence of two-thirds of the Senate’s members for ratification. South Korea accounts for 5% of PH exports and 7% of imports. The FTA aims to eliminate tariffs of agricultural and industrial products. Under the deal, the 5% import duty on South Korean cars will be lifted while duties on electric and hybrid vehicles from South Korea will be eliminated within five years. Cars from South Korea (i.e. Hyundai and Kia) accounted for 2% of 2022 car sales. 

Maharlika to start as national development fund. The Maharlika Investment Fund will start as a national development fund in the first five to seven years according to Maharlika Investment Corp President and CEO Joel Consing. Maharlika has an initial fund of P75bn, which will eventually rise to P125bn. Once the fund generates excess funds, then it can go into other investment opportunities, including fixed-income instruments, domestic and corporate bonds. Mr. Consing aims to convert unutilized government land into mega ecoozones for FDI; ecozones will focus on agriculture and forestry, as well as renewable energy. These plans are still subject to the approval of MIC’s board of directors, the members of which are expected to be announced soon by Malacanang.

Palace says four new Maharlika directors sworn in. Four new directors of the Maharlika Investment Corp (MIC) have been sworn in:
(1) Vicky Castillo Tan – A long-time Asian Development Bank officer, serving in various capacities from 2002-2021
(2) Andrew Jerome Gan – a seasoned corporate executive, currently serving in a audit/compliance firm Capital Markets Integrity Corp
(3) German Lichauco - a seasoned corporate litigator, has been a director and corporate secretary of several firms in the country
(4) Roman Felipe Reyes - current director of various entities including Converge ICT
