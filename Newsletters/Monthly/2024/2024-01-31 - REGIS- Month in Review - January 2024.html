REGIS REPORT

31 January 2024
 

The Philippines kicked off the year with a 3.0% surge, taking the PSEi to 6646.44 as of end January. The rally extended the PSEi’s two-month ascent, and was fueled by prospects of rate cuts by the US Federal Reserve. On the ground, the lower inflation print boosted hopes of a more dovish stance from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).  Net foreign inflows helped to push the market.

December inflation decelerated to 3.9% from the 4.1% pace in November. The inflation print came in lower than consensus estimates of 4.0% and at the lower end of the BSP’s estimated range of 3.6-4.4%. The drop was thanks largely to easing food inflation, which slowed to 5.4% from 5.7% in November.  December marked the third straight month of slowing inflation, from the 6.1% recorded in September 2023 when food inflation reached 9.7% as rice prices soared.

Rice continued to be a factor to December inflation, however.  Rice inflation for the month rose to 19.6% from 13.5% as regional export prices continued to climb on a YoY and sequential basis. Nonetheless, CPI inflation is likely to dip further next month (Jan 2024) on base effects.  These base effects are likely to fade into 2Q however, and this will likely keep the BSP wary of cutting rates too soon particularly with the threats from El Niño.   The first monetary Board meeting for the year is set for 15 February.

Even so, BSP Governor Eli Remolona seems to have slightly softened his stance on monetary policy, saying the BSP was in a “neutral territory at this point.” This is a change from his statements in December when he said the BSP was still “hawkish”. The BSP Governor has also brought up the possibility of further cuts in banks’ regulatory reserve requirement (RRR).

Philippine GDP grew 5.6% in both 4Q23 and for full year 2023.  The latest print was a slowdown from the 3Q23 growth of +6.0% but is better than Street expectations (5.2%). Private Consumption was solid at 5.3% while Capital Formation grew at a faster clip of 11.2%. Government Consumption contracted (-1.8%) in 4Q due to the budget constraints.  Meanwhile,  net imports (exports) were a drag (+2.3%) as exports contracted again.

The new leaders of the economic team of the administration, Frederick Go and Ralph Recto, laid down their priorities soon after assuming their positions earlier in the month.  Ralph Recto, a congressman and a former senator, took his oath as Secretary of Finance.  Frederick Go, the former CEO of Robinsons Land, meanwhile, took his oath as a “Special Assistant” to the President, and will supervise three of the main Cabinet Departments relating to the country’s finances and economy, including the Finance Department. Among the priorities outlined were the restoration of powers of investment promotion agencies and the streamlining of tax regulations for businesses under various incentive schemes. Go also aims to reduce equity trading friction costs with a reduction in the stock transaction tax to 0.1%. Five priority sectors were also identified: (1) Mining, (2) Semiconductors / Micro-electronics, (3) Agriculture, (4) Steel and (5) Pharmaceuticals.  The tax on junk food and sweetened beverage is not among the new team’s initiatives, with Secretary Recto saying that the proposal is being shelved.

Along with other regional currencies the PHP showed weakness in January.  The PHP depreciated 1.6% MoM to close at PHP56.295/US$.  Corresponding with this weakness was the strength of the USD. The DXY gained 1.9% in January as markets re-evaluated the speed and magnitude of Fed rate cuts against the state of the US economy. Also providing pressure to the currency was the trade deficit, with the November number showing a drastic (26% YoY) widening due to the persistent weakness in exports. 

Political news during the month could not have helped sentiment for the currency either.  Politics has come to the forefront as the signature drive to amend the Constitution under a “People’s Initiative” (PI) reached the 12% threshold that would trigger a plebiscite. The People’s Initiative has revealed in-fighting within the coalition that supported the Marcos candidacy.   The Duterte camp has shown objections to the People’s Initiative, with the Duterte family launching personal attacks against President Marcos and calling for his resignation.

The single amendment proposed by the PI seeks to change a provision that would allow Constitutional changes to be proposed by Congress voting as one body (rather than as two Houses – Senate and House of Representatives – voting separately). This change would massively dilute the power of Senators (many of whom oppose Constitutional amendments) and allow for bigger and more substantive changes to the Constitution.

Key data
 

Average turnover improved to US$103.9m, up +16% MoM, an early optimism on rate cuts and improving macro numbers fueled interest in the market. The month also saw a couple of placements – in Ayala Corp (US$97m) and AREIT (US$100m). Excluding the two, ADV stood at $95m, still better than the previous month. Foreign and local split was pretty even at 50%, similar to the month prior.

  • Foreigners continued to be net buyers in the market and inflow for the month stood at $80m, a large improvement over the US$18m last December.  Gains in big caps propelled the index to 6600  and heavy weights BDO (+US$41m)  and SM  (+US$20m) both led the top foreign bought names. Buying eventually spilled to mid-cap GTCAP (+US$20m)  towards the middle of the month.
  • Foreigners generally turned net buyers in banks save for BPI (-$9m) after some rotation from the sector in the previous month. Property names continued to see foreign interest given the peak rates scenario. ALI (+$19m) and SMPH (+$12m) saw decent foreign buying.
  • Despite inflation cooling, foreigners continued to sell consumer names with URC (-US$15m) the top sold name again for the 2nd straight month. The stock has been net foreign sold for the 10th consecutive month and is currently trading near its 52-week low. JFC just like last month  bucked the trend and printed a new 52-week high this January on continued foreign demand.

 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

The lower interest rate outlook resulted in across-the-board gains, with index gainers beating losers 7:3.

 

Banks benefitted from strong market sentiment. The big three banks led, with large liquid BDO (+11.1% MoM) contributing most to the PSEI’s overall gain in January (60.5 points to overall 196-point move).  BPI (+6.6% MoM), whose merger with Robinsons Bank (RBC)  became effective on Jan 1  was finalized was the second top contributor to the index’s gain (32.1 index points).   

In contrast to the big banks, Union Bank (-11.2% MoM) dropped markedly as its 4Q23 profit dropped 57% on massive provisions.  The bank likewise announced a P10bn rights issue, to pump more capital into UnionDigital.

Meanwhile, Metrobank (+11.3% MoM)  surged, on strong laggard interest.

 

Metrobank parent GT Capital (+12.8% MoM) likewise rose sharply.  Its auto unit Toyota Motor Philippines reported record sales in 2023 of 200k units (+15% YoY).  Toyota’s sales were solid in December, rising by 13% YoY to 19,551 units.  The auto industry association CAMPI also conveyed optimism that industry car sales could reach 500k units in 2024 from 430k last year.

Other mid cap conglos also outperformed. DMCI (+9.9% MoM) and Aboitiz Equity (+8.7% MoM) ended higher. Also gaining were Cosco (+8.7% MoM), LT Group (+5.8% MoM) and San Miguel (+5.8% MoM).  SMC jumped after winning bids to supply 1,200MW and 1,800MW of power to Meralco. These new contracts would replace two contracts that SMC either reneged on or walked away from as the previous contract prices were low and did not reflect current fuel costs. The prices for these new supply contracts (~P7/kwh) are 70% higher than they would have been (~P4/kwh) under the terminated deals.

Meanwhile, large cap market proxy SM (+3.2% MoM) rose along with the market. Ayala Corp (-0.3% MoM) ended the month slightly lower, however.  Mitsubishi Corp.’s placed 8.6m shares in the company.

 

The consumer sector was mixed, despite the better-than-expected inflation print.

Large cap Universal Robina (-5.7% MoM) succumbed to heavy selling pressure despite Finance Secretary Ralph Recto’s pronouncement that the proposed tax on junk food and sweetened beverages was being shelved. 

Robinsons Retail (-11.1% MoM) also dropped. Other retailers however had a better month, with Puregold (+3.3% MoM), Philippines Seven (+4.9% MoM), Wilcon (+4.8% MoM), and SSI Group (+16.7% MoM) all delivering gains.

Jollibee (+1.0% MoM) meanwhile managed to close the month higher. Jollibee disclosed that it is infusing more money into the vehicle that holds the international operations of Tim Ho Wan, the dimsum chain founded in HK. The fund size of the vehicle, Titan Dining, will be raised to SGD450m from SGD350m and JFC’s stake will rise to 92%. JFC aims to have 100 Tim Ho Wan stores in China, from the current 19.

Monde (+5.1% MoM) offered a glimpse into its operating performance in 2023. The APAC BFB business continued to perform strongly in 4Q23. The segment posted all-time high volumes during the quarter, rising 5% YoY  (2% QoQ). Market share in instant noodles was 67.5% in 4Q23 up slightly vs 3Q23 (67.3%). The core income of MONDE implied in the statement would be ~P7.5bn for FY23 -- a shade higher than Street estimates. FY23 sales grew high single-digit YoY (Regis FY23: +4%), while gross margin is in line with 2022 (31.3%).

Century Pacific (+6.5% MoM) also continued to be in favor.

 

Bloomberry (+13.6% MoM) outperformed after gaming regulator PAGCOR said that it expects 2024 gaming revenues for the industry to reach P336bn in 2024, a growth of 17% from a record-setting 2023 performance. GGR jumped 33% in 2023 on the back of strong contributions from integrated resorts (P207bn) and  electronic games (P58bn). Regis expectations for IRs are in line with the regulator’s outlook for 2024, and Bloomberry is expected to extend its revenue streak on the back of increased play from existing players, the return of tourists and the new property.

 

Despite reports that Ayala Land’s “ultra high-end” project has been seeing strong interest, Ayala Land (-1.0% MoM) shares succumbed to profit-taking, after rising 24% the previous two months.

Meanwhile, SM Prime (+4.1% MoM), which underperformed the market in December, rose.

 

Converge (+10.6% MoM) led the telcos, after its 47% plunge in 2023 attracted laggard interest.  Talk that GCash (Mynt) could do a listing by 2H24 drummed up interest for parent Globe (+1.0% MoM). Meanwhile, PLDT (-0.5% MoM) chairman and CEO Manny Pangilinan is eyeing a permanent replacement for Al Panlilio within the year. Panlilio retired end of 2023 due to “health reasons”. Pangilinan was only supposed to assume the CEO position on an interim basis when Napoleon Nazareno retired in 2015. But his interim tenure ended up lasting longer than Panlilio’s term as CEO (two years).

 

After being the top index mover last month, ICTSI (-1.5% MoM) shed in January after demands from a labor union and unsuccessful bidders cast doubts on the company’s ability to take over South Africa’s largest container port in Durban.  Meanwhile, attacks at the Red Sea have disrupted shipping line routes connecting Europe and Asia, igniting concerns over a potential drop in volumes at ICT’s ports.  During the month, the company also sold a small port in Jakarta, Indonesia but also won a similar sized port contract in Iloilo, Philippines. 

 

After terminating bulk water contracts in Pangasinan and Cebu City in December, Manila Water (-3.3% MoM) signed a new contract with the local government of Cebu City, which has a much higher tariff than its previous terminated agreement. The company also announced that it is in talks with the local government of Pangasinan about possibly crafting a new agreement.   

In the wake of its 10.8% rise in the previous month, Meralco (-9.3% MoM) fell despite reports of congressman Rep Joey Salceda filing a bill to extend its distribution franchise by another 25 years.  The company also guided for electricity sales growth of 4.5% in FY24, which is just a tad below real GDP growth but in line with historical trends.

Aboitiz Power (+0.5% MoM)  conveyed a bullish outlook on electricity demand, expecting consumption to increase 6.6% in 2024E. This is higher than the 5% growth in peak demand in 2023. To help address the tightness in the electricity market, the company plans to build at least 4,600MW worth of renewable energy (RE) capacity over the next decade. Of this, the company already has close to 1,000MW in the pipeline, with a combination of geothermal, solar and wind.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

4Q23 and FY23 GDP up 5.6%. 4Q23 GDP settled at +5.6% YoY, slowing down from +6.0% in 3Q23 and +7.1% in 4Q22. This was mildly better than consensus forecast of +5.2% for the quarter. Majority of the heavy lifting was done by private consumption and a strong rebound in capital formation.

Private Consumption grew slightly to +5.3% in 4Q from +5.1% in 3Q. This was propelled by sustained strong growth in discretionary spending: restaurants/hotels (+16% in 4Q), transport (+12%), and recreation (+7%). However, food spending remained soft for the fourth straight quarter, up by +0.5% YoY (below historical trend: +5.1%). Capital formation rose sharply to +11.2%, after being lethargic for the past two quarters. This was due to the (a) turnaround in durable equipment, which expanded 14.6% YoY in the quarter, as well as (b) inventory re-stocking. Government consumption, however, contracted -1.8% from +6.7% in 3Q. Domestic demand was strong, accelerating to 6.1% in 4Q after posting below trend growth rates in 2Q/3Q. Net imports dragged overall GDP growth by 1.2ppts, largely due to a 12% YoY drop in goods exports. 

On the supply side, growth accelerated for the services and agriculture sectors, which more than compensated for the slowdown in the industrial sector. Industrial sector growth moderated to +3.2% from +5.6% in 3Q, as the manufacturing sector barely grew during the quarter. 

FY23 GDP growth averaged +5.6% YoY, falling below the government-set target of 6-7% but was broadly in line with consensus estimates (5.5%). For 2024, Regis expects GDP growth to accelerate to 6.0% (Street: 5.7%).

Inflation cools further to 3.9% in December; 2023 average inflation rate at 6.0%. Headline inflation slowed to 3.9% YoY in December from 4.1% in November. This is slightly better than consensus: 4.0% and came in near the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.6-4.4%. Inflation of food and non-food items decelerated to 5.4%/2.6% in December from 5.7%/2.9%. Headline inflation averaged at 6.0% in 2023, higher than 5.8% in 2022. On a MoM basis, headline CPI rose 0.2% in December, broadly the same rate as in November. Rice CPI grew at a faster MoM rate of 3.5% in December from 2.7% in November and accounted for bulk of the acceleration in food items (+0.7% MoM in Dec vs +0.2% in Nov).

Non-food items dipped -0.2% MoM, slowing further from +0.1% in Nov thanks to lower electricity prices and fuel prices. The average MoM change for the whole of 2023 was +0.3%, slower than +0.6% in 2022. Core inflation (excluding volatile food and fuel items) decelerated to 4.4% from 4.7%. Regis anticipates the BSP will begin cutting rates in 2Q24 (50bps), with the rest of the cuts (100bps) by 2H24.

Cuts in RRR, not just rates, possible – BSP. A cut in the policy rate is possible in 1H24 but not likely. According to BSP Governor Eli M. Remolona Jr. While the data on inflation and other economic indicators are “looking good” and that “we like the trend so far” he is not encouraging further talk of rate cuts anytime soon. The BSP’s 24E inflation forecast is 4-4.2%, citing supply shocks from El Nino as key risks. Remolona previously signaled that they will consider easing monetary policy only when the CPI is firmly within the mid-range of the target or around 3%. He also said that a cut in the RRR within the year is very possible. The first Monetary Board policy meeting for 2024 is scheduled on Feb. 15

GIR drops MoM to US$102.45bn in December. GIR hit US$102.45bn in December. This is down from US$102.72bn in November due to payment of maturing foreign loans but up from US$96.15bn in 2022. This is equivalent to 7.7 months of imports.

BSP to limit its intervention in forex market; Monetary policy on “neutral territory”. The BSP plans to implement a new framework regarding intervention in the foreign exchange market later this year. BSP Governor Remolona believes that the BSP has been “intervening a bit too much" and since intervention should only been done during times of stress, it should be done infrequently. Meanwhile, expectations that inflation could have returned to within its 2-4% target band for December is giving Governor Remolona scope to refrain from further rate hikes. 

“The BSP is no longer behind the curve in taming prices” and describes its policy rate on “neutral territory at this point”. Gov Remolona has repeatedly signaled that the BSP is unlikely to pivot to policy easing soon as price risks persist, including El Nino’s effects on food and energy costs. The BSP’s key interest rate is at a 16-year high of 6.5%. Its first monetary policy meeting for 2024 is on Feb 15.

Remittances rise by 2.8% YoY in November. Remittances in November grew by 2.8% YoY to US$2.72bn. Remittances were down 9.3% MoM due to seasonality and In PHP terms, remittances dipped by 0.3% YoY. Land-based remittances rose 2.9%, lifted by higher remittances from Saudi Arabia (5.6%) and UAE (9.3%); Sluggish growth from the USA (+1%) persisted. Sea-based remittances continued to gain pace, rising by 2.6% YoY in Nov, fastest growth rate since April 2023’s +2.7%. This brings YT-November remittances are up by 2.8% YoY to US$30.2bn. The BSP is forecasting remittances to rise by 3% in 2023/2024.

 

Finance chief Recto calls for study of carbon pricing instruments. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto to impose carbon tax and the study of carbon pricing instruments. He specifically identified the development of a carbon tax and emissions trading system (ETS) as crucial steps towards achieving a low-carbon economy. The DOF will lead technical group working on carbon pricing, with government agencies, multilateral banks, and development partners.

Philippines secures 5-year rice supply deal with Vietnam. During President Marcos' two-day visit to Vietnam, among the deals signed was a memorandum of understanding on Rice Trade Cooperation. The agreement entails a steady supply of 1.5 to 2mn metric tons of white rice at "a competitive and affordable price" from Vietnam's private sector to the Philippine private sector.

The country sources 90% of its rice imports from Vietnam, importing 2.4mn tons of rice worth US$1.5bn in the nine months to September 2023.

Department of Transportation to launch Swiss Challenge for AEV’s Bohol airport proposal in 1Q. The Department of Transportation is eyeing the conduct of a Swiss challenge of Aboitiz Infracapital’s P4.53bn unsolicited proposal to upgrade, operate, and maintain the Bohol international airport in 1Q23. Negotiations with the proponent are at the tail end already so the Swiss challenge could be launched this quarter. Aboitiz Infracapital is a wholly-owned unit of Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV PM).

 

Earlier this month, the department also announced that it plans to launch the Swiss challenge for AEV’s P12.75bn unsolicited proposal for the Laguindingan airport. The Bohol airport project will add to the government’s PPP pipeline of “live bids”, which currently includes big-ticket projects such as P171bn NAIA airport PPP and a Swiss challenge for San Miguel’s (SMC PM) P27.5bn TPLEX extension project.

Government eyes US$5bn from global bonds. National Treasurer Sharon Almanza said the programmed global bond issuance for 2024 is equivalent to US$5bn, almost the same level as in 2022 (US$5.26bn). However, the Treasury could not say for now what would be the first issuance for the year.

BIR raises VAT-free ceiling on housing. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has raised the VAT-free ceiling on housing units to P3.6m, from P3.2m previously. Regis expects this to be mildly positive for Filinvest Land and Vista Land. This development should be neutral for other developers Regis covers.

FY23 tourist arrivals reach 5.45m (66% of FY19). The Department of Tourism reported that there were 5.45m international visitor arrivals in 2023, which is 66% of 2019 levels. Tourist arrivals in 2023 more than doubled from the 2.6m reported in 2022. South Korea remained the top source (26% of total) of tourist arrivals at 1.44m, reverting to 72% of 2019 levels. USA came in second at 903k (17% of total and about 85% of pre-Covid levels). Chinese tourists only reached 264k, or 15% of pre-Covid levels. Implied December 2023 tourist arrivals at 565k, 82% of pre-Covid levels. The government is targeting 7.7m tourist arrivals in 2024, up 41% YoY and about 93% of 2019 levels.

 

 

Oath-taking of Ralph Recto and Frederick Go to be held. The Presidential Communication Office announced that Batangas Rep. Ralph Recto will take his oath as Finance Secretary. Mr. Recto is a House Deputy speaker, a former three-term senator, and former NEDA Secretary. Mr. Frederick Go will also take his oath as Special Assistant to the President for Investment and Economic Affairs. Mr. Go was President of Robinsons Land (RLC PM) for 17 years.

House panel endorses CREATE MORE. The House Ways and Means Committee endorsed to the members the CREATE MORE (CREATE to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy). The Bill seeks to amend Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act, reconciling “disparities between the CREATE Act and its implementing rules”. A committee report will be taken up at the Ways and Means panel next week before it is debated at the plenary.

Proposed amendments include:

  • Reduction in the corporate income tax to 20% for those under the enhanced deduction regime from 20-25%
  • Continuation of duty exemptions, VAT exemption on importation, and VAT zero-rating of local purchases for domestic and export companies, even those inside ecozones and freeports
  • Empowering the President to modify, craft and grant incentive packages, without recommendation of the Fiscal Incentives Review Board
  • Introduction of a “simplified and streamlined” tax refund system for registered business enterprises
  • IT and BPO sector will be allowed to “conduct business under alternative work arrangements

DOF shelves plans to impose new taxes on junk food and sweetened beverages. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto culled the proposed junk food and sweetened beverage tax from the list of proposed new taxes. Citing that such taxes will continue to fuel inflation. On the other new taxes proposed, Sec. Recto is supportive of imposing taxes on single-use plastic and digital services. The proposed hike in taxes on motor vehicle users may also be tempered.

DOF expects P126bn revenue loss from PIFITA (Tax Package 4) over 5 years. During the hearing of the Senate Committee on Ways and Means, the DOF said that the Passive Income and Financial Intermediary Taxation Act (PIFITA) could result in lower tax revenue of P126bn from 2025 to 2029 (average of P21bn annually). Many of the changes will be gradual (lowered by 1ppt annually or 0.1% annually in the case of the stock transaction tax).

New finance chief joins BSP’s monetary board. Newly appointed Finance Secretary Ralph Recto has formally assumed the role of the government’s representative to the seven-member Monetary Board. Recall that Mr. Recto was the former House deputy speaker before his appointment as the Secretary of the Department of Finance last Jan 12. He replaced former finance secretary Benjamin Diokno, who was appointed to a six-year term as a private sector representative in the Monetary Board.
