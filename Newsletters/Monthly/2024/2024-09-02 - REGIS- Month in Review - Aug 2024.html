REGIS REPORT

02 September 2024
 

The PSEi gained for the 2nd month in a row, +4.21% MoM to 6897.54 in August, as investors rejoiced the BSP’s decision to ease rates for the first time since late 2020 and anticipate a dovish turn in global monetary policy with the Fed expected to cut rates by as much as 100bps before the year ends. Shares initially suffered weakness in the earlier part of the week as investors digested a faster-than-expected uptick in the latest CPI reading and an as-expected acceleration in 2Q GDP growth, which supported a case for a possible rate hold in the BSP’s mid-August policy meeting.

Data showed that inflation accelerated to 4.4% in July from 3.7% in June, marking the highest reading since October 2023 and exceeding the 4.1% consensus forecast, driven primarily by electricity, food and transport prices. However that CPI is expected to ease moving forward as effects of the rice tariff cut may start to trickle in and the unfavorable base effects on rice CPI may start to fade as well.

Meanwhile, GDP growth accelerated to +6.3% YoY in 2Q from +5.8% in 1Q, matching the consensus forecast. On the demand side, government spending was the key driver, growing by +10.7%, marking a rebound after a -7.1% slump in 2Q2023. However, private consumption grew by a slower pace of +4.6%, below pre-pandemic levels, which at least in isolation still supported the case for a potential monetary policy easing.

Amid these data points, BSP Governor Remolona projected mixed signals regarding their policy move leading into the event, commenting at one point that the CPI turnout lessens the chances of a rate cut. Consensus still favored a cut but the individual entries in the Bloomberg survey showed that economists were split on the matter with 13 favoring a cut, against 10 seeing a hold. That said, the monetary board ultimately decided to ease rates by 25bps to 6.25%, which marked the first rate cut in the country since November 2020. This made the Philippines a stand-out in the region as our close peers Indonesia and Thailand both decided to keep their interest rates steady in the following week.

With this tailwind, the PSEi finally broke past the crucial 6800 line which had been the sticky resistance level that held the market since early April. Chart-wise, the benchmark also formed a golden cross in its daily chart in the latter part of the month. That said, shares found a new resistance level at the psychological 7000-line which was tested during 3 separate occasions but remains to be convincingly broken. Note that the PSEi has yet to end a session above 7000 since January 2023. Shares slightly edged off highs and ultimately settled August at 6897, +4.21% MoM.

Alongside the rise in equities, the PHP sharply strengthened as well, +3.98% MoM to 56.150. This marked the peso’s biggest monthly advance since August 2001 and best level since March this year, which should bode well for the easing in inflationary pressures moving forward, and hence potentially strengthens the case for further rate cuts. As it stands, the BSP has indicated for 1 more rate cut before the year ends. There are two more scheduled policy meetings left for the year: October 17 and December 19.

Other key events on the ground during the month include the conclusion of the 2Q earnings season and the MSCI rebalancing. Regis’ tally of results shows that aggregate earnings of PSEi members grew by +5% YoY in 2Q and +6% in 1H, in line with both Regis and Street forecasts. Excluding SMC, SCC, DMC and NIKL, results of PSEi members grew by +10% YoY in 2Q and +13% in 1H, also in line with forecasts. As for the MSCI rebalancing, there were no big changes in the standard cap index (no new additions/deletions), but the small cap had a new inclusion in PLUS which surged +33.45% MoM and a deletion in SECB -6.83%. Turnover during the month-end rebal session surged to US$237m, more than double the ~US$100m YTD average and the heaviest since the prior MSCI rebal on end-May when activity hit as high as US$388m. Outflow estimates for the event expected at least US$50m but actual net outflow of the market during the month-end session was much lighter at US$5.5m, indicating some signs of support from foreign investors.

Key data
 

Turnover for the month improved to US$107.7m vs US$86m in July, with volumes improving mid-month after the 25bp-rate cut of the BSP. The rate cut helped propel the PSEi to retest key levels (7000) late this month, eventually settling at 6897 to end the month +4.2%. Foreigners were net buyers in August for US$143m. The market saw 14 consecutive days of net foreign inflow during the month. However, they turned net sellers on the last trading day of August as MSCi held its quarterly index rebalancing end of the month, where estimated outflow for Phils was around US$50m. Foreigners were more active in August, taking 56% of market activity, vs 51% in July.

The market continued to see foreign demand with banks the most favored sector. All big 3 banks finished the month in the green, with largest, most liquid BDO the preferred name among the 3 with the stock closing +12% this month. BDO recorded strong net inflows of US$33m. Property meanwhile finished the month mixed, despite the rate cut, with foreign interested gravitating towards ALI over SMPH. Strong foreign demand lifted ALI’s share price to close +19.6% while SMPH still managed to finish the month with some modest gains. It however saw net selling of US$3.0m

ICT continued to be the favored, with foreigners buying into its growth story. The stock has been foreign bought (net) for the 7th straight month with the stock printing a new all-time high in August on strong foreign buying.

Renewed foreign selling sent URC’s share price falling 23.5%. The stock now traded near 52-week lows and is at risk of breaking its key support levels.  Outside of URC, the consumer space was mixed. JFC and CNPF saw decent foreign buying, while outflows still evident in EMI and RRHI.

Other notable names in August: CNVRG turned in a performance of +30% on foreign demand, while SECB saw an US$11m worth of outflow after its deletion from the MSCi small cap index.

 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

With the backdrop of easing rates, winners outnumbered laggards this August, 22 to 8.

Biggest gainer in the benchmark was CNVRG which surged +29.98% MoM to its best levels since March 2023. Its 2Q net income looked broadly in line with expectations but what was more encouraging was that its topline grew by 12%. The company stated that they added 99k new broadband subscribers in 2Q which, albeit lower than its 125k adds in 1Q, outperformed its rivals in the segment and accounted for nearly 2/3 of the industry adds. CNVRG's share price sharply outperformed GLO +2.23% and TEL -0.99%, which posted more tempered moves and trailed behind the broader market rally this time after previously outperforming over the past months when the index was in a holding pattern below 6800. CNVRG is now the best performing stock in the benchmark this year, +80.19% YTD (vs PSEi's +7.34%).

Property and banking sectors were generally strong in August as they both benefitted from a surge in inflows following the BSP’s rate cut. In the case of Property, the gains came despite a more tempered outlook on the industry amid the upcoming POGO ban that is expected to take effect after this year, and as the recent 2Q results showed that pre-sales continued to be largely mixed across developers. ALI’s reservation sales grew by +5% QoQ and +15% YoY in 2Q, while SMPH’s markedly fell by -48% QoQ and -58% YoY. Despite this, both names managed to beat the tape with ALI, which tends to be high-beta play for any rate cuts and renewed foreign interest, surging +19.66% MoM to its best level since late February, while SMPH +7.47%.

Meanwhile the gains in the Banking space came at the heels of being the bright spot in the 2Q results season. BDO +11.53% MoM fared the best, supported by sharp inflows. Note that it is among the most widely held and liquid names in the market, which passes filters of foreign funds and hence benefits as a kind of proxy for investors wanting to increase exposure to the Philippines. Its peers MBT +6.99% and BPI +2.8% also gained this week, similarly seeing inflows post rate cut. Outside of results, there was also a headline this month about the BSP Governor commenting that the current RRR ratio of 9.5% is too high, reiterating his intention to lower it in the future, which should bode well for lenders.  

Also, among the most widely-held and highly liquid names in the market, ICT was another top gaining stock this August, marking a new all-time high during the month and ending +11.29% MoM. Note that 2Q results from earlier in the month looked quite good as well, surpassing both Regis and consensus forecasts. ICT is now the 2nd best performing stock in the PSEi, +64.1% YTD.

In contrast, URC fared the worst, -23.53% MoM, as it was conspicuously dragged down by foreign selling while the broader market was being lifted by inflows towards the 7000 handle. Its results look tempered with 2Q revenues largely flat (and 1H up by just +3% YoY) but we continue to be positive on the broader consumer space, which we expect to have an earnings recovery cycle once inflation eases in the remainder of the year. URC also notably trailed behind its consumer peers with JFC +12.96%, CNPF +6.23%, PGOLD +5.51% and MONDE +3.05% all capping the month in the green. Next to URC, the second worst laggard in the benchmark was its parent JGS which slumped -15.47% to its worst level since late 2011.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

2Q24 GDP accelerates to 6.3%. GDP expanded by 6.3% in 2Q24, accelerating from the 5.8% (revised from 5.7%) pace in 1Q24 and 4.3% in 2Q23, in line with the consensus forecast of 6.3%. This brings 1H24 GDP to 6.0% (vs. Regis FY24E: 6% and government’s 6-7%).

On the demand side, domestic demand grew 7.9%, the fastest pace since 1Q23 following a strong rebound in government spending (+10.7%) and capital formation (+11.5%). Private consumption grew at a steady pace of 4.6%, but remained below pre-pandemic trends (5.4%). National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) Secretary Arsenio M. Balisacan described household consumption growth of 4.6% as “anemic.” Government spending rebounded by 10.7% in 2Q24, after declining by 7.1% in 2Q23. Capital formation went up by 11.5%, after construction activity rebounded (+16% YoY in 2Q24 vs 6.9% in 1Q24 and 2.5% in 2Q23). Public construction grew 22% in 2Q24 from 12% in 2Q23; Private construction rose 10% from 5%; Commercial construction grew 14%. Strong fixed capital formation was also aided by slower growth in inventory withdrawals. Net imports turned positive, reversing last year’s net exports and weighing on overall GDP, albeit mildly (-0.9ppts).

On the supply side, the industry sector grew at a faster rate of 7.7%, boosted by strong construction growth while service sector grew a solid 6.8%. Agriculture sector declined by 2.3%, primarily driven by the decline of Palay by 9.5%.

BSP cuts policy rate by 25bps to 6.25%. The BSP monetary board decided to reduce its policy rate (target reverse repurchase rate) by 25bps to 6.25%, in line with consensus estimates. Officials cited weak consumption in 2Q24 GDP, as well as expectations of inflation declining in the coming months. Headline inflation is projected to trend downward to within the government’s 2–4% target range despite the uptick in July. BSP Governor Eli Remolona said the cut may only be felt next year, given lag effects. BSP said that the balance of risks to the inflation outlook continues to lean toward the downside for 2024 and 2025 with a modest tilt to the upside for 2026. Moving forward, the BSP Governor Remolona signaled another 25-bp cut for the remainder of the year. Note the Monetary Board will next meet on Oct. 17 and Dec. 19.

Headline inflation accelerates to 4.4% in July. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that headline CPI rose 4.4% in July, accelerating from 3.7% in June. This was above consensus estimate of 4.1% but came in the lower end of the BSP’s forecast range of 4.0%-4.8%. Key drivers were electricity prices (-5.4% YoY in July vs. -13.7% in June), food prices (+6.4% YoY vs. 6.1%), and fuel prices for transport (+6.4% YoY vs. 3.8%). While rice inflation moderated to 20.9% in July from 22.5% in June, it remained elevated; inflation of other food items quickened (meat, fruits, vegetables, and corn). However, unfavorable base effects on rice CPI should start fading by August due to falling Vietnam rice prices and the rice tariff cut last July 5.

As of Aug 6, the Department of Agriculture indicated that prices of imported well-milled rice ranged P51-53/kg and regular milled rice ranged P46-48/kg in NCR. The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that NCR retail prices of well-milled rice averaged at P53.1/kg while regular milled rice averaged P45.88/kg in July 2024. In Aug 2023: NCR retail prices averaged P47.06/kg (well-milled) and P42.70/kg (regular).

Core inflation eased to 2.9% in July from 3.1% in June, with non-food “core” inflation steady at 3.3%. The average 7M24 headline inflation rate stood at 3.7%, within the BSP’s 2-4% target band. The BSP said that the latest inflation outturn is consistent with the BSP’s projections that inflation will temporarily rise above target in July but could follow a general downtrend beginning in August. 

Remolona says: BSP can hold rates ‘for the time being’. During a budget hearing, BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. said that “evolving inflation conditions show the BSP can hold its policy settings steady for the time being.” If price pressures continue to ease, it will be possible for the BSP to consider a less restrictive monetary policy stance. Previously, Governor Remolona said the BSP may cut rates by a total of 50bps this year, with 25bps set on Aug 15 and another 25bps in 4Q.

 

BSP has room for one more rate cut. After cutting interest rates by 25 bps last Thursday, the BSP said there is room for one more rate cut this year, according to BSP Governor Eli Remolona Jr. in an interview with CNBC. The next moves by the BSP will largely be driven by the inflation outlook. If inflation continues a downward path, the BSP can look to further remove the restrictiveness in the monetary stance to support a recovery in domestic demand and overall growth. BSP is scheduled to conduct two more policy reviews on October 17 and December 19. The GDP growth projection for the year is expected to be close to 6%, slightly higher than last year's growth rate of 5.6%.

Trade deficit widens by 9% YoY to US$4.3bn in June; 1H24 trade deficit down 10% YoY. Preliminary data showed that the trade deficit widened to US$4.3bn in June, up by 9% YoY. On a MoM basis, the trade deficit was lower than the US$4.71bn deficit in May. Exports slumped by 17% YoY to US$5.57bn due to a 24% decline in electronic product exports. Imports were at US$9.87bn, down 7% YoY. Fuel-related imports increased by 2%. However, imports of raw/intermediate goods declined by 10%, while capital goods and consumer goods imports fell by 9% and 7%, respectively. This brings 1H24 trade deficit down 10% YoY to US$25bn.

Remittances rise 2.5% YoY in June. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew 2.5% YoY to US$2.88bn in June. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 7.6% YoY. Strong growth rates seen from land-based remittances coming from Singapore (+4.6%), Saudi Arabia (+5.2%), UAE (+5.4%), Qatar (+6%), UK (+3.3%), and Canada (+3.3%), while sea-based remittances rose 2.1% YoY. This brings 1H24 remittances up 2.9% YoY to US$16.25bn, broadly in line with the BSP’s target of +3% YoY.

Budget deficit narrows to P29bn in July; Primary surplus up >3x to P51bn. The national government recorded a budget deficit of P28.8bn in July, lower by 40% YoY. Revenues grew by 11% YoY to P457bn, buoyed by a 16% YoY jump in tax revenues to P403bn. Disbursements/expenses rose 6% YoY to P486bn, following a 25% YoY jump in interest expenses to P79bn. Primary spending (i.e. excluding interest expenses) rose by 3% YoY in July, resulting in a primary surplus of P51bn, up 222% YoY. This brings YT-July budget deficit to P643bn, higher by 7% YoY. The government appears to be broadly on track to hit its fiscal deficit target of P1.48tn by year-end, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

Balance of payments position swings to a US$62m surplus in July; End-July GIR level rises to US$106.7bn. The country’s balance of payments registered a US$62m surplus in July, a reversal of the US$53m deficit recorded in July 2023 and the US$155m deficit in June 2024. Officials cited inflows from the BSP’s investments abroad and the national government’s net foreign currency deposits with the BSP. Meanwhile, the final GIR level for end-July stood at US$106.7bn, up from US$105.2bn in end-June. This is equivalent to 7.9 months’ worth of imports of goods.

Infrastructure spending rises 18% YoY to P721bn in 1H24. Overall infrastructure spending (including LGU transfers and GOCC subsidies) rose by 18.4% YoY to P720.5bn in 1H24. This exceeded the government’s 1H24 program of P671.3bn by 7.3% and is equivalent to 5.7% of GDP. Implies that infra spend for 2Q24 alone grew by 24% YoY to P454bn. The government aims to spend P1.47tn (+4% YoY) on infrastructure for FY24 (5.6% of GDP). However, this implies that infrastructure spending in 2H24 will drop by 7% YoY to P752bn. For 2025, the government plans to spend P1.5tn of infrastructure, up 4% YoY and equivalent to 5.4% of GDP. The government aims to spend 5-6% of GDP annually for infrastructure through 2028.

Government raising US$2.5bn in three-tranche bond offering. The government returned to the international bond market for the second time this year via a three-tranche sustainable bond offering: US$500m 5.5-year bond offer was priced at +75bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 4.375% (vs. initial guidance of T+110bps). US$1.1bn 10.5-year bond offer was priced at +95bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 4.75% (vs. initial guidance of T+125bps). US$900m 25-year bond offer was priced at +103.4bps over US treasuries or a coupon of 5.175%(vs. initial guidance of 5.5%).

Proceeds will be used for general purposes including budgetary support, of which US$900m 25-year bond will be used to finance/refinance assets in line with the Republic’s sustainable finance framework. The government plans to borrow US$5bn this year, of which US$2bn was raised from the issuance of global bonds last May. The National Government’s (NG) outstanding debt stood at P15.48tn as of end-June, equivalent to 62.4% of GDP. The government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end; It aims to lower the ratio further to 51% by 2028.

 

Commercial banks’ loan growth steady at 10.1% YoY in June; M3 growth mildly accelerates to 6.6%. The BSP reported that commercial bank loans grew by 10.1% YoY in June, based on preliminary data. This is the same rate of increase in May 2024. Loans to production activities decelerated slightly to 8.3% YoY from 8.4%. Consumer loans expanded by 25% YoY from 25.6% YoY in May. Loans to non-residents grew by 9.8% YoY in June from 8.1% YoY in May. Meanwhile, domestic liquidity (M3 growth) grew by 6.6% in June, mildly faster than the 6.5% in May.

Asset quality improves in June; loan growth accelerates to 12.5% YoY. Asset quality ratios for the Philippine banking system improved nearly across the board in June. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) dipped 7bps MoM to 3.59% from 3.66% in May. In PHP terms, NPLs were up 1.4% MoM to P502bn. Past Due (PD) ratio fell 9bps MoM to 4.29%, with the nominal amounts up 1.0% to P614bn. Restructured Loans (RL) improved to 2.05%, with absolute RLs down 0.8% MoM to P294bn. NPL cover was flattish MoM, with June coming in at 95.41%, 40bps lower than the 95.81% in May.

Vehicle sales rise 6% YoY in July; Toyota sales up 11%. CAMPI reported that new vehicle sales grew by 6% YoY, 0.6% MoM to 39,331 units in July. This takes 7M24 sales up 11% YoY to 265,610 units. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) cornered 46.21% of the market, selling 122,738 units in 7M24, up 11% YoY. In July alone, TMP’s sales rose 11% YoY, 1.6% MoM to 18,388 units. To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.5k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 16.6k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses). GTCAP trades at a 50% discount to its market-based NAV of P1,186 (vs. historical discount of 14%) and 5.4x FY24E PER.

BSP to lift ban on new digital banks in 2025. BSP will lift the moratorium imposed on the granting of digital banking licenses starting Jan. 1, 2025, but will only permit a maximum of 10 digital banks to operate in the country. The issuance of new digital bank licenses includes converting an existing bank’s license to a digital bank license. Six digital banks are currently operating in the country. Lifting the moratorium will only accommodate four additional licenses, coming from either new or converting banks. The BSP previously granted digital banking licenses to Maya Bank, Overseas Filipino Bank Inc., Tonik Digital Bank Inc., UNObank Inc., UnionDigital Bank and GOtyme Bank Corp.

Philippine ports foreign container throughput rises by 5% YoY in 2Q24. Philippine ports handled almost 1.4m foreign container TEUs in 2Q24, up 5% YoY, which marks a rebound from -1% YoY in 1Q24. For the quarter, ICTSI’s MICT grew volumes by 7% YoY to 738k TEUs, after posting flat growth in 1Q24. This takes 1H24 volumes of MICT to 1.4m TEUs, up 3% YoY. To reach our forecast of 2.9m TEUs (+5% YoY) for 2024, volumes for the rest of the year need to grow by 6% YoY to 1.5m TEUs.

Air passenger volumes rise 26% YoY to 29.5m in 1H24, 3% below pre-Covid levels. Data from the Civil Aeronautics Board showed that nationwide air pax volumes rose by 26% YoY to 29.5m in 1H24. This was 3% below pre-Covid levels. Cebu Air (CEB PM) sustained market share gains, with pax volumes up 27% YoY to 10.9m, exceeding 1H19 levels by 3%. International pax volumes grew 54% YoY to 13.8m in 1H24, 90% of 1H19 levels. Domestic pax volumes grew 8% YoY to 15.8m in 1H24, surpassing 1H19 levels by 4%.
