REGIS REPORT

02 October 2024
 

Philippine shares rallied for the 3rd month in a row as foreign buying further accelerated following the Fed’s decision to cut rates by 50bps.On the ground, shares also had a tailwind from the BSP’s surprise announcement of a significant 250bp cut in lenders’ reserve requirement ratio to 7%, set to take effect on October 25. While the cut itself did not come as a surprise, its magnitude was indeed unexpected and helped drive further gains in the Banking sector.

Net foreign buying in the overall market during the month stood at a heavy $346m, which has notably tipped the Philippines’ YTD net flow to the positive (by $23m) after hitting a bottom of -$534m in net outflows in June. Note that the Philippines has yet to see a positive annual net foreign flow since 2017.

That said, local shares however did see some signs of resistance at the tail-end of the month as focus among foreign investors shifted towards China where shares have been rallying sharply as of late, supported by a fresh batch of stimulus measures by the PBOC. Note however that Chinese markets will be shut for the first week of October in celebration of the People’s Republic of China’s 75th anniversary. With the largely risk-on sentiment on the ground through most of the month, the PSEi ultimately capped September with a sizable gain of +5.44% MoM to 7272.65 (albeit off the September 26 peak of 7458.74), touching its best levels since February 2022 and turning in its best monthly gain since November 2022.

On the macro front, data released earlier in the month showed that CPI eased to 3.3% in August, lower than 3.6% forecast and 4.4% in July. Meanwhile the PHP eked out another +0.17% MoM gain (to 56.045) following its sharp +3.82% rally in August. These, along with the Fed’s direction for further cuts, help support the case for further policy easing by the BSP. Note that expectations on the ground have recently shifted towards favoring two more rate cuts before the year ends, from a prior view of just a single cut after the last BSP policy meeting back in mid-August. The BSP will hold policy meetings in October and December. With this, the yield on the 10-year Treasury substantially eased to 5.76% by end-September from 6.1% in end-August, albeit above the 5.595% low recorded during the month.

Key data
 

Market turnover (daily ave) grew to US$133m from US$107m in August, with more activity spurred by the 50-bp Fed rate cut and the BSP’s surprise 250-bp RRR cut. The market also saw the biggest monthly inflow for the year of US$346m in September. This took YTD foreign flow into positive territory.  


  • Locals became more active, taking up 53% of the split.
  • Foreign demand surged in September as appetite for equities grew after the Fed’s 50bps rate cut and the surprise 250bps RRR cut by the BSP. The market was net foreign bought this month, virtually erasing all outflows earlier in the year and taking YTD inflow to US$18m.
  • Foreign investors were quick to buy into market proxies SM and AC alongside banks which will benefit from the RRR cut.
  • SECB was a big outlier after its deletion from the MSCi small cap index. The stock rallied 60%, with foreigners taking notice of its  cheap valuation. Both BDO and BPI meanwhile printed new all time highs, while peer MBT created a new 52-wk high.
  • Property names generally saw foreign inflows in September, witih ALI recording the biggest inflow of +US$35m.
  • Consumer names too saw good foreign demand. JFC was net foreign bought for the 2nd straight month while URC turned finally saw net foreign buying after huge outflows in the previous month.





 
 
LEADERS AND LAGGARDS
 

Banking shares fared well in September as investors priced in the clearly positive impact of the RRR cut announcement on lenders. The Financial sub-index gained +8.86% MoM, bringing its YTD gain to a hefty +32.13%. Regis estimates that the 250bp RRR cut could add 6-8bps on NIMs and increase net profits by 4%, based on Rafa’s FY2025E numbers. The three biggest lenders all advanced during the month with BPI +8.34% MoM, MBT +6.94% and BDO +3.4%, but what’s interesting is that several smaller cap, non-PSEi names posted much faster gains this time around: SECB +59.92%, CBC +27.5% and PNB +21.74%.

There was also notable rotation into Conglos during the month with the sub-index +8.54% MoM. Traditional Philippine proxy names like index heavyweights SM +8.02% and AC +10.99% were boosted by consistent foreign buying throughout the month, but again a handful of mid-caps saw larger gains: JGS +18.3%, GTCAP +16.99% and AEV +11.9%. Note that JGS and AEV came from oversold levels and are big laggards YTD, -27.13% and -16.7%, respectively. This sharp spike in some of the value and small/mid cap plays comes in contrast with a slower pace of gains in ICT +1.97%, which has been one of the best performing names in the market this year, +63.86% YTD, as it was evidently held back by investors rotating into other sectors.

Another bright spot was the consumer space, with gains by URC +13.96% MoM, PGOLD +13.51% and MONDE +7.72%, as easing inflation on the ground looks poised to help bolster consumer demand leading into 4Q. Following the slowdown in price pressures in August, September CPI due this coming Friday is expected to show a further slowdown to 2.5%, which would mark the lowest reading since October 2020. This should also support the case for more rate cuts before the year ends.

On the opposite end, biggest laggard in the index this September was SMC -9.09%, dragged primarily by outflows amid its deletion from the FTSE mid-cap index. Another notable laggard was SCC -4.13% - note that there were some headlines during the month about the Department of Finance looking to sell its 3.4% stake in the company, along with a list of other government-owned assets, in order to boost government coffers. Latest news on the matter is that state-run pension fund Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) is interested in acquiring the stake from the Department of Finance.

 
MACRO AND POLITICAL NEWS

Inflation eases to 3.3% in August. Headline inflation eased to 3.3% in August from 4.4% in July. This was below consensus forecast of 3.6% and came in at the lower-end of the BSP’s forecast range of 3.2%-4.0%. Slowdown due largely to (a) favorable base effects on rice CPI, (b) slower price increases/negative base effects in other food items such as meat, fish, vegetables, and sugar, and (c) lower fuel prices, which drove transport inflation lower. These items more than compensated for the upticks in electricity prices. Core inflation also eased to 2.6% in August from 2.9% in July, with the “non-food” core inflation moderating to 3.0% from 3.3%. Brings 8M24 average headline inflation rate to 3.6%, within the BSP’s range of 2-4%. BSP Governor Eli Remolona, Jr. previously signaled for another 25bps cut in the policy rate this year, after implementing a 25bps cut last August. Monetary Board’s next meeting is schedule on Oct 17.

BSP plans to cut RRR substantially this year. BSP Governor Eli Romolona said that the BSP will reduce the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) substantially this year and there may be further reductions next year. In June 2023, the BSP lowered the RRR of universal and commercial banks (U/KB) from 12% to 9.5%, where it remains. A RRR cut could mitigate the impact of the reduction in policy interest rates on NIMs. Excess liquidity could also ease the competitive pressure on PHP deposits. We estimate that for every 100-basis point cut in the RRR, net profit of the banks we cover could rise 1.2%-1.4%.

Finance Secretary seeks 50bps rate cut. Finance Secretary Ralph Recto said he will support a 50bps rate cut at the next Monetary Board meeting. Secretary Recto is part of the seven-member Monetary Board. The remaining BSP policy meetings in 2024 are set for Oct. 17 and Dec. 19.

BSP Governor signals two more rate cuts this year. At the sidelines of an ADB event, BSP Governor Remolona Jr. stated that policy rates could be cut by up to 50bps “in principle.” This would bring benchmark rate down to 5.75% by end-2024, in line with REGIS expectations. Governor Remolona said there could be a risk of a hard landing if the BSP decides to cut by 50bps in one meeting. Governor Remolona adds that September inflation is estimated to be lower than August print of 3.3%. The Philippine Statistics Authority is set to release September inflation data on October 4.

Current account (CA) deficit widens by 25% YoY in 2Q24; 1H24 CA deficit lower by 18% to US$7.1bn. The country’s current account deficit widened by 25% YoY to US$5.1bn in 2Q24, equivalent to 3.9% of GDP. Due to the 4.2% expansion in the trade deficit to US$16.8bn due to the 0.8% YoY drop in exports to US$13.8bn and the 1.9% YoY increase in imports to US$30.7bn. BPO export revenues, which fall under net services receipts, grew 4.9% YoY to US$7.4bn in 2Q24. Brings 1H24 current account deficit to US$7.08bn, lower by 18% YoY and equivalent to 4.1% of GDP. Due to the 5% YoY drop in trade in goods deficit of US$31.5bn. 1H24 BPO export revenues amounted to US$14.5bn, up 3.1% YoY. BSP forecasts the current account deficit will be lower by 58% YoY to US$4.7bn by year-end, equivalent to 1% of GDP.

BOP posts US$88m surplus in August; End-August GIR revised up to US$107.9bn. The country’s overall balance of payments (BOP) position posted a surplus of US$88m in August. This was a reversal of the US$57m deficit a year ago and was higher than the US$66m surplus in July 2024. Officials cited inflows from the net income from the BSP’s investments abroad. Recall that the government raised US$2.5bn from a three-tranche global bond offering in late August, which brought YT-August BOP surplus to US$1.6bn, lower than the US$2.1bn surplus recorded in the same period last year. The BSP is forecasting a BOP surplus of US$1.6bn this year.

Meanwhile, the revised end-August GIR level stood at US$107.9bn, higher than the US$106.7bn level in July 2024 and the previous preliminary figure of US$106.92bn. This is equivalent to 7.8 months’ worth of imports. BSP is forecasting an end-2024 GIR level of US$104bn.

Trade deficit widens to US$4.87bn in July. Preliminary data showed the country recorded a trade deficit of US$4.87bn in July. This is wider than the US$4.1bn deficit in July 2023 and US$4.3bn deficit in June. Exports were flat at US$6.25bn, following a 12% YoY drop in electronic exports. Imports rose 7% YoY to US$11.1bn due to higher imports of capital goods (+10% YoY), raw materials (+13%), and consumer goods (+3%) offset by lower fuel imports (-7%). Imports of electronic products rose 12% YoY, which could indicate a possible rebound in exports in succeeding months. This brings YT-July trade deficit to US$29.9bn, lower by 6% YoY.

 

BSP revises BOP forecasts; sees wider current account deficits but larger BOP surpluses in 2024/2025.The BSP is projecting higher BOP surpluses to US$2.3bn in 2024 and US$1.7bn in 2025, higher than its previous projections of US$1.6bn and US$1.5bn, respectively. Officials cited higher-than-expected non-resident inflows or financial account in 1H24, driven by higher foreign portfolio investments, which emanated from higher withdrawals of placements in foreign-issued debt securities. This offset higher current account deficit projections of US$6.8bn in 2024 and US$5.5bn in 2025 due to lower projections in merchandise exports.

Budget deficit narrows to P54bn in August. The government’s budget deficit narrowed by 59% YoY to P54bn in August. The decline is due to 24% YoY jump in revenues, alongside a mild contraction in expenses. Revenues rose 24% YoY, driven by strong non-tax collections. Tax collections grew 10%, with BIR collections rising by 12% and Customs revenues by 5%. Non-tax revenues surged >3x to P70bn, on the back of PSALM’s P10bn settlement of guarantee fee arrears, along with higher PAGCOR income. Expenses declined by 0.7%, despite a 24% YoY increase in interest expenses. Primary spending (net of interest payments) fell 3% YoY. YT-August budget deficit now stands at P697bn, down by 5% YoY. Note that the government is targeting the deficit to fall by 1.8% YoY to P1.48tn, equivalent to 5.6% of GDP.

Remittances rise 3.1% YoY in July. Cash remittances coursed through banks grew by 3.1% YoY, 7% MoM to a 7-month high of US$3.09bn in July. In PHP terms, remittances grew by 9.8% YoY. The bump likely due to the start of school year, which varied between July and Aug. Land-based remittances rose 3.6% YoY, with remittances from the US rising by 3% and UAE by 12%, while sea-based remittances grew by 0.9% YoY. This brings YT-July remittances to US$19.3bn, up 2.9% YoY. The BSP expects remittances to grow by 3% this year, which suggests remittances could reach US$34.5bn.

Lower House approves P6.35tn 2025 budget on third reading. House Bill (HB) No. 10800, which contains the P6.352tn proposed budget for 2025, was approved by the House of Representatives on the third reading. The budget is 10% higher than the approved 2024 budget and is equivalent to ~22% of GDP. It appears that the key change to the proposed budget is only the reduction in the budget of the office of the Vice President, which was cut by over half to P733m from P2.04bn previously. The budget will now be transmitted to the Senate for its own deliberations. Key items of the budget are: Debt service (i.e. interest payments) will increase by 27% YoY; Primary spending (excluding debt service) will increase by 8% YoY, driven largely by the 17% increase in allocations to LGUs; Budgets for education and infrastructure (i.e. public works and transportation) sectors are only up by +0.9% YoY and +0.8% YoY, respectively.

NEDA sees faster GDP growth in 2H. The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) sees GDP growth for 2H24 to be faster than the 6.0% print in 1H24, according to NEDA Secretary Arsenio M. Balisacan. The Secretary thinks the likelihood of the government achieving its 6-7% GDP growth goal this year is now very high, citing easing inflation. To reach the high-end of the government’s target range, GDP growth in 2H will need to average 7.9%.

FDI net inflows fall 29% YoY to four-year low in June. FDI net inflows fell 29% YoY, 28% MoM to US$394m in June, the lowest level since April 2020. The decline resulted from lower net inflows across all major FDI components. Investments in debt instruments fell by 30% YoY to US$213m, net investments in equity capital fell 33% to US$74m, while reinvestment earnings fell 23% to US$107m. By source, equity capital placements were mainly from Japan (47%), followed by the United States (15%), Sweden (14%) and Singapore (14%). These were mainly invested in the manufacturing (48%), real estate (18%), wholesale and retail trade (16%) and financial and insurance (11%) sectors. This brings 1H24 net inflows to US$4.4bn, up 7.9% YoY. Note that BSP expected FDI net inflows to reach US$9.5bn this year, up from US$8.9bn in 2023.

Government debt rises to P15.69tn in July. The national government’s outstanding debt rose by 1.3% MoM (P206bn) to P15.69tn as of end-July. Domestic debt rose 1.7% (P180bn) MoM to P10.75tn due to net issuances of government securities, while external debt rose by 0.5% (P26.5bn) MoM to P4.94tn. The increase due to net availment of project loans (P5bn) and third-currency upward revaluation (P35bn), offset by the impact of PHP’s appreciation (P14bn). Note that the government is targeting a debt-to-GDP ratio of ~60% by year-end. As of end-June 2024, the government’s debt-to-GDP ratio stood at 62.4%. Government aims to lower the ratio further to 51% by 2028.

Government hikes minimum wages in Regions IV-A, VII. The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) approved the increase in minimum wages in regions IV-A and VII. Region IV-A will be implementing minimum wage hikes of 6-17% for the non-agriculture sector and 4-18% for the agri sector of various municipalities by Sep 30 (last increase of 10-11% became effective on Sep 24, 2023.) Region VII will increase minimum wages by 7-8% by October 2 (last increase of 8-9% became effective last October 1, 2023.) Labor Secretary Bienvenido Laguesma said he expects regional wage boards across the country to finish their review before year-end.

 

 

Bicam approves CREATE MORE bill. The Congressional Bicameral Conference Committee approved the CREATE MORE bill, which aims to draw in more foreign direct investments by enhancing tax incentives. Senate agreed to adopt provisions in the House version regarding fuel VAT refunds and local taxes on economic zone locators. Bicam version allows fuel suppliers serving tax-exempt entities to be eligible for tax refunds and allows local government units to set the local tax for registered business enterprises at up to 2% of gross income

Salient provisions of the CREATE MORE bill (Bicam report) are:

>Reduction of corporate income tax rates to 20% from 25% for (a) corporations with less than P5m net taxable income and assets less that P100m and (b) Registered Business Enterprises under the enhanced deductions regime.

>Introduction of the “Registered Business Enterprise (RBE) Local Tax”, which is capped at 2% of gross income and will replace all local taxes and fees.

>Allowing RBEs 100% deduction on power expenses (from 50% previously), which could lower power costs by P3/kwh for manufacturing.

>Transfer of the responsibility of processing VAT refund claims to the Department of Finance from the Bureau of Internal Revenue to cut delays.

>Allowing firms to maintain work-from-home setup for 50% of workforce to cut costs.

>Reinstate powers of the President to give fiscal and non-fiscal incentives to enterprises without the need for a recommendation from the Fiscal Incentives Review Board.

>Exemption of international carriers from paying VAT on imported fuel, goods, and supplies used for international shipping or air transport operations.

Senate approves VAT Refund bill for foreign tourists, Lower House set to adopt Senate version. The Senate passed the VAT refund bill for foreign tourists on its third reading, with 19 senators voting in favor. Non-resident visitors can claim refunds for VAT on goods purchased worth at least P3,000 from accredited retailers. The Department of Finance expects the measure to boost tourism revenues by P10bn annually. The Department of Tourism estimates visitor receipt have reached P282bn in 1H24, up 33% YoY. In 2019, foreign visitor receipts reached P600bn, equivalent to 3.1% of GDP. The VAT refund system is projected to be fully implemented by 2025, contributing to the government’s target of 12mn foreign tourist arrivals by 2028.

Senators propose Bill banning online gambling. Senators Alan Peter Cayetano and Joel Villanueva proposed an anti-online gambling Bill. There are currently 45 licensed gaming companies offering e-games. The BIR expects to collect P15bn to P20bn in franchise fees from e-games this year (after collecting P13bn in 2023).

Senate ratifies PHL-South Korea FTA. The Senate ratified the free trade agreement (FTA) between the Philippines and South Korea. FTA will remove Philippines tariffs on 96.5% of goods from South Korea, while Seoul will lift tariffs on around 94.8% of Philippines products. FTA is still undergoing ratification process at the Korean National Assembly. One of the provisions of the FTA is the removal of 5% import duties on automobiles from South Korea while tariffs of EVs and hybrids will also be eliminated for five years. 30% tariffs of Philippine bananas will be removed within five years, while 36% tariffs of processed pineapples will be removed within seven years.

Korean vehicles (Hyundai and Kia) accounted for 3.4% of vehicle sales in 2023. Ayala Corp. (AC PM) is the main distributor of Kia in the country. The Philippines is also seen to increase banana and processed pineapple exports to Seoul. Philippines ran a trade deficit of US$4.96bn with South Korea in 2023.

 

DOJ Undersecretary Jesse Andres named OIC chairman and CEO of ERC. Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin appointed Department of Justice Undersecretary Jesse Hermogenes T. Andres as OIC Chairman and CEO of the ERC. The position will be in effect until a replacement is designated or until otherwise directed. This comes after the 6-month suspension of previous ERC Chairperson Monalisa Dimalanta last Sep 9. This move reportedly breaks from tradition, as typically the most senior commissioner of the ERC would be named OIC. Mr. Andres was previously the chief of staff of former Vice President Noli de Castro and was the previous head of the litigation department at Ponce Enrile Cayetano Bautista Reyes & Manalastas Law Offices (PECABAR) before establishing his own law firm.

Vehicle sales rise 6.6% in Aug. Vehicle sales grew by 6.6% to 39,155 units in August. On a MoM basis, this was lower by 0.4%. This brings YT-Aug retail sales to 304,765 units, up 10.3% YoY. Toyota Motor Philippines (TMP) sold 17,924 units in August, up 7.7% YoY but down 2.5% MoM. This brings YT-Aug retail sales up 11% to 140,654 units. To reach TMP’s FY24E target of 215k units (+7% YoY), monthly sales should average 18.6k units for the rest of the year. To reach Regis’ target of 206k units (+3%), monthly sales should average 16.3k units for the rest of the year. TMP is a 51%-owned unit of GT Capital (GTCAP PM) and accounts for 33% of NAV (25% of gross asset value) and 26% of gross profit (before parent expenses).

Consumer loans jump 20% YoY in 2Q24; at a record-high 22% of total loans. Consumer loans in 2Q24 surged 19.7% YoY to P2,807bn, accelerating from the already-heady 17.6% pace in the previous quarter, and the fastest YoY clip since 4Q2016. On a QoQ basis, consumer loans expanded 4.2% vs 1Q24. The growth in consumer loans far exceeded the 10.3% YoY pace of total banking sector loans, suggesting that corporate loans grew 7.9% YoY in 2Q24. As a result, consumer loans now account for 21.8% of total, a new all-time high.

Mortgages grew 13.5% YoY to P1,039bn, accelerating sharply from the 7.9% pace in 1Q24, and posting the fastest growth since 3Q2018.  Motor Vehicle (MV) loans also sped up, growing 18.7% YoY to P557bn (was +17.6% in 1Q24, and fastest since 1Q2018).  Credit card receivable growth slowed slightly, up 28.8% to P797bn (vs 30.0% in 1Q). Salary loans grew 27.2% YoY to P366bn, though this was a slowdown from the 29.3% in 1Q24 and 54.5% pace in 2Q23.

Asset quality worsens in July; loans unusually dip 1.3% MoM. Asset quality ratios of the banking sector deteriorated MoM in July, though the magnitude of the move was partly due to an unusual MoM drop in loans. NPL ratio (ex-interbank) increased 9bps MoM to 3.68%, the highest level since July 2022. In nominal terms, NPLs stood at P507.8bn, up 1.1% MoM and 15.5% YoY. Past Due (PD) ratio also blipped up 11bps MoM to 4.40%, with the PHP value up 1.9% MoM and 18.4% YoY. NPL cover dipped 110bps to 94.3% vs 95.4% in June. On a positive note, Restructured Loans (RL) were flat MoM at 2.05%, with the nominal value down 0.9% MoM and down 4.5% YoY.
